## Session Overview

이번 세션에서는 eat up, kick off, stand out을 비롯해 put off, deal with, figure out 등 일상생활 곳곳에서 자주 등장하는 구동사와 청크 100여 개를 집중적으로 학습했습니다. 동작의 시작과 완료, 감정 표현, 직장·대인 관계, 그리고 일이 진행되는 다양한 맥락에서 활용할 수 있는 표현들을 폭넓게 다루었습니다. 각 표현의 정확한 뉘앙스를 이해하고 실제 대화에서 자연스럽게 쓸 수 있도록 연습하는 것이 이번 세션의 핵심 목표입니다.

## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### eat up — 다 먹다

- *The kids ate up all the pizza in minutes.*
  - 아이들이 몇 분 만에 피자를 다 먹었다.

> **A:** Did you enjoy the meal?
>
> **B:** I ate up every last bite — it was delicious!

### kick off — 시작하다

- *The conference kicks off at nine sharp tomorrow.*
  - 회의는 내일 정각 9시에 시작된다.

> **A:** When does the game kick off?
>
> **B:** At 7 p.m. — don't be late!

### come through — 해내다, 기대에 부응하다

- *She came through for us when we needed her most.*
  - 가장 필요한 순간에 그녀가 해냈다.

> **A:** Did Jake finish the report in time?
>
> **B:** He came through — submitted it right before the deadline.

### put off — 미루다, 연기하다

- *Don't put off your homework until the last minute.*
  - 숙제를 마지막 순간까지 미루지 마라.

> **A:** Have you called the dentist yet?
>
> **B:** I keep putting it off — I'll do it today, I promise.

### stand out — 눈에 띄다

- *Her bright red jacket made her stand out in the crowd.*
  - 밝은 빨간 재킷 덕분에 그녀는 군중 속에서 눈에 띄었다.

> **A:** Why do you think they hired you?
>
> **B:** I think my portfolio really stood out from the rest.

### deal with — 처리하다, 다루다

- *He had to deal with several complaints from customers.*
  - 그는 고객들의 여러 불만을 처리해야 했다.

> **A:** There's a problem with the delivery order.
>
> **B:** Leave it to me — I'll deal with it right away.

### take apart — 분해하다

- *He took apart the old clock to see how it worked.*
  - 그는 작동 원리를 보려고 오래된 시계를 분해했다.

> **A:** Why are there screws all over the table?
>
> **B:** I took apart my laptop to clean the fan inside.

### wind down — 긴장을 풀다, 서서히 멈추다

- *She likes to wind down with a cup of tea before bed.*
  - 그녀는 자기 전에 차 한 잔으로 긴장을 푼다.

> **A:** You've been working non-stop all day.
>
> **B:** I know — I really need to wind down tonight.

### get across — 이해시키다

- *He struggled to get his point across during the debate.*
  - 그는 토론 중 자신의 요점을 이해시키려 애썼다.

> **A:** Do you think the audience understood your idea?
>
> **B:** I'm not sure I got it across clearly enough.

### call in — 요청하다, 전화하다

- *They called in an expert to fix the electrical problem.*
  - 그들은 전기 문제를 해결하기 위해 전문가를 요청했다.

> **A:** The pipes are leaking again downstairs.
>
> **B:** We need to call in a plumber immediately.

### fall behind — 뒤처지다

- *She fell behind in class after missing a week of school.*
  - 그녀는 일주일 결석한 후 수업에서 뒤처졌다.

> **A:** Are you keeping up with all the assignments?
>
> **B:** Not really — I've fallen behind on the readings.

### bring out — 돋보이게 하다, 이끌어내다

- *The new leadership role really brought out the best in him.*
  - 새로운 리더십 역할이 그의 최고의 모습을 돋보이게 했다.

> **A:** She seems so confident up on stage.
>
> **B:** Acting really brings out her true personality.

### act up — 말썽을 부리다

- *My knee has been acting up since yesterday's long run.*
  - 어제 장거리 달리기 이후로 무릎이 말썽을 부리고 있다.

> **A:** Why is the printer making that strange noise?
>
> **B:** It's been acting up all morning — so frustrating.

### put through — 전화를 연결하다

- *The receptionist put me through to the manager.*
  - 안내원이 나를 매니저에게 전화로 연결해 주었다.

> **A:** I need to speak with someone in the billing department.
>
> **B:** Of course — I'll put you through right now.

### drop out — 중도 포기하다

- *He dropped out of college to start his own business.*
  - 그는 자신의 사업을 시작하기 위해 대학을 중도 포기했다.

> **A:** I heard Tom isn't in school anymore.
>
> **B:** Yeah, he dropped out last semester to travel.

### turn into — 변하다, 바뀌다

- *The small argument turned into a serious fight.*
  - 작은 말다툼이 심각한 싸움으로 변했다.

> **A:** How did the picnic go yesterday?
>
> **B:** It turned into a disaster when it started raining.

### bear with — 참고 기다려 주다

- *Please bear with me while I look up your account.*
  - 계정을 조회하는 동안 잠시 참고 기다려 주세요.

> **A:** This is taking quite a long time.
>
> **B:** Bear with me — the system is running slowly today.

### keep to — 지키다

- *Please try to keep to the schedule we all agreed on.*
  - 우리 모두가 합의한 일정을 지키도록 노력해 주세요.

> **A:** Can we discuss a few other topics while we're here?
>
> **B:** Let's keep to the agenda for now and save that for later.

### break through — 돌파하다

- *The young singer finally broke through with her debut album.*
  - 그 젊은 가수는 데뷔 앨범으로 마침내 돌파했다.

> **A:** Has the research team made any real progress?
>
> **B:** Yes — they broke through a major obstacle this morning.

### let off — 봐주다, 처벌을 면하게 하다

- *The judge let him off with just a warning.*
  - 판사는 그에게 경고만으로 봐주었다.

> **A:** Did you get in trouble for being late?
>
> **B:** No, my boss let me off this time.

### go against — 반대하다, 맞서다

- *Her decision went against everything we had planned.*
  - 그녀의 결정은 우리가 계획한 모든 것에 반대되었다.

> **A:** Are you going to vote for that proposal?
>
> **B:** No, it goes against my principles.

### draw up — 작성하다

- *Can you draw up a contract before the meeting?*
  - 회의 전에 계약서를 작성해 줄 수 있어요?

> **A:** We need this agreement in writing.
>
> **B:** I'll draw up a document by tomorrow.

### take after — 닮다

- *She takes after her mother in both looks and personality.*
  - 그녀는 외모와 성격 모두 어머니를 닮았다.

> **A:** Your son is so calm and patient.
>
> **B:** Yes, he really takes after his dad.

### shut down — 폐쇄하다, 닫다

- *The factory was shut down due to safety violations.*
  - 그 공장은 안전 위반으로 인해 폐쇄되었다.

> **A:** I heard the old café is gone.
>
> **B:** Yes, they shut it down last month.

### come along — 함께 가다, 따라오다

- *We're heading to the beach — why don't you come along?*
  - 우리 해변에 가는데 — 같이 따라오지 않을래?

> **A:** I'm going to the farmers' market on Saturday.
>
> **B:** Can I come along? That sounds fun!

### cut in — 끼어들다

- *A car suddenly cut in right in front of us on the highway.*
  - 고속도로에서 차 한 대가 갑자기 우리 앞에 끼어들었다.

> **A:** Sorry, can I cut in for just a second?
>
> **B:** Sure — what did you want to say?

### figure out — 파악하다, 이해하다

- *It took me an hour to figure out how the machine works.*
  - 그 기계가 어떻게 작동하는지 파악하는 데 한 시간이 걸렸다.

> **A:** I can't figure out this math problem at all.
>
> **B:** Let me take a look — maybe I can help.

### turn over — 뒤집다

- *Please turn over the pancake before it burns.*
  - 타기 전에 팬케이크를 뒤집어 주세요.

> **A:** Is the steak done on that side yet?
>
> **B:** Yes, let's turn it over now.

### get rid of — 제거하다

- *We need to get rid of these old boxes in the garage.*
  - 우리는 차고에 있는 이 오래된 상자들을 제거해야 한다.

> **A:** This room is so cluttered.
>
> **B:** I know — let's get rid of things we never use.

### play along — 장단을 맞추다

- *Just play along with the plan for now, even if you disagree.*
  - 동의하지 않더라도 지금은 그 계획에 장단을 맞춰 주세요.

> **A:** My boss loves this idea but it's not great.
>
> **B:** Sometimes you just have to play along.

### break in — 침입하다

- *Someone tried to break in through the back window last night.*
  - 어젯밤 누군가 뒷창문으로 침입하려 했다.

> **A:** Why is your front door broken?
>
> **B:** A thief tried to break in while we were out.

### put down — 내려놓다

- *She put down her phone and focused on the conversation.*
  - 그녀는 휴대폰을 내려놓고 대화에 집중했다.

> **A:** You've been on your phone all evening.
>
> **B:** You're right — I'll put it down now.

### get through — 무사히 지나다, 극복하다

- *She managed to get through the tough exam with flying colors.*
  - 그녀는 어려운 시험을 훌륭하게 무사히 통과했다.

> **A:** How are you coping with all this stress?
>
> **B:** It's tough, but I'll get through it.

### carry out — 수행하다, 실행하다

- *The team carried out the experiment exactly as planned.*
  - 팀은 계획한 대로 정확하게 실험을 수행했다.

> **A:** Has the safety inspection been done yet?
>
> **B:** Yes, we carried it out this morning.

### come off — 벗겨지다, 떨어지다

- *The label came off the bottle after it got wet.*
  - 병이 젖은 후 라벨이 벗겨졌다.

> **A:** Why is there no price tag on this item?
>
> **B:** It must have come off during shipping.

### hold off — 미루다, 연기하다

- *Let's hold off on a decision until we have more data.*
  - 더 많은 데이터가 생길 때까지 결정을 미루자.

> **A:** Should we buy the concert tickets now?
>
> **B:** Let's hold off — the price might drop.

### take on — 떠맡다

- *She agreed to take on the role of team leader.*
  - 그녀는 팀 리더 역할을 떠맡는 데 동의했다.

> **A:** Are you taking on any new projects this quarter?
>
> **B:** Yes, I just took on a really big client.

### see off — 배웅하다

- *My whole family came to the airport to see me off.*
  - 가족 모두가 나를 배웅하러 공항에 왔다.

> **A:** Is anyone going to the station with you?
>
> **B:** Yes, my parents want to see me off.

### black out — 정신을 잃다

- *He blacked out from exhaustion during the marathon.*
  - 그는 마라톤 도중 탈진으로 정신을 잃었다.

> **A:** Are you okay? You don't look well at all.
>
> **B:** I nearly blacked out from the heat outside.

### run through — 검토하다, 훑어보다

- *Let's run through the presentation slides one more time.*
  - 프레젠테이션 슬라이드를 한 번 더 검토해 봅시다.

> **A:** Should we run through the contract before signing?
>
> **B:** Yes, let's make sure everything looks correct.

### go away — 떠나다

- *The headache didn't go away even after I took medicine.*
  - 약을 먹어도 두통이 사라지지 않았다.

> **A:** This stress just won't go away no matter what I try.
>
> **B:** Maybe you need a real break this weekend.

### keep on — 계속하다

- *She kept on studying even when she felt completely exhausted.*
  - 그녀는 완전히 지쳤을 때도 계속 공부했다.

> **A:** I keep on making the same grammar mistakes in my writing.
>
> **B:** Try reviewing the rules more carefully each time.

### break away — 벗어나다, 탈출하다

- *He finally broke away from his old habits and started fresh.*
  - 그는 마침내 오래된 습관에서 벗어나 새 출발을 했다.

> **A:** I really want to break away from this boring daily routine.
>
> **B:** Why not try traveling somewhere completely new?

### pass on — 전달하다

- *Please pass on the meeting notes to everyone on the team.*
  - 회의 메모를 팀원 모두에게 전달해 주세요.

> **A:** Can you pass on this message to the manager for me?
>
> **B:** Sure, I'll let her know right away.

### come up — 나타나다, 일이 생기다

- *Something urgent came up, so I had to cancel my plans.*
  - 급한 일이 생겨서 계획을 취소해야 했다.

> **A:** Why weren't you at this morning's meeting?
>
> **B:** Sorry, an unexpected issue came up at the last minute.

### clean out — 싹 비우고 정리하다

- *We need to clean out the garage before we move.*
  - 이사하기 전에 차고를 싹 비우고 정리해야 한다.

> **A:** The storage room is completely packed with old stuff.
>
> **B:** Let's clean it out this weekend and donate what we don't need.

### settle down — 본격적으로 시작하다, 진정되다

- *Let's settle down and focus on today's agenda.*
  - 자, 본격적으로 시작해서 오늘의 안건에 집중합시다.

> **A:** The kids are being really loud — I can't concentrate.
>
> **B:** They'll settle down once the movie starts.

### block off — 차단하다

- *I blocked off Friday afternoon for the client meeting.*
  - 클라이언트 회의를 위해 금요일 오후를 차단해 두었다.

> **A:** Do you have any free time on Thursday morning?
>
> **B:** No, I've already blocked off the whole morning, sorry.

### stand by — 참아주다, 용납하다, 대기하다

- *She stood by her decision even though everyone disagreed.*
  - 모두가 반대했지만 그녀는 자신의 결정을 지켰다.

> **A:** Will you stand by me if things get really difficult?
>
> **B:** Of course — I'll always be here for you.

### come around — 마음을 바꾸다

- *He finally came around and agreed with our plan.*
  - 그는 결국 마음을 바꿔 우리 계획에 동의했다.

> **A:** My dad still doesn't approve of my career change.
>
> **B:** Give him time — he'll come around eventually.

### put forward — 의견을 제시하다, 지원하다

- *She put forward a creative idea during the brainstorming session.*
  - 그녀는 브레인스토밍 시간에 창의적인 아이디어를 제시했다.

> **A:** We really need a fresh solution to this problem.
>
> **B:** I'd like to put forward a suggestion if that's okay.

### fall out — 사이가 틀어지다

- *The two friends fell out over a misunderstanding last year.*
  - 두 친구는 작년에 오해로 인해 사이가 틀어졌다.

> **A:** I heard you and Jake aren't talking to each other anymore.
>
> **B:** Yeah, we fell out after that argument at the party.

### take down — 아래로 끌어내리다, 적어두다

- *Please take down the key points from today's lecture.*
  - 오늘 강의의 핵심 포인트를 적어두세요.

> **A:** Did you get all the important information from the meeting?
>
> **B:** Yes, I took down everything the manager mentioned.

### crack down — 강력하게 단속하다

- *The city is cracking down on illegal parking this month.*
  - 시는 이번 달 불법 주차를 강력하게 단속하고 있다.

> **A:** Why are there so many tickets on the street today?
>
> **B:** The police are cracking down on traffic violations.

### bring along — 동반하다, 데려오다

- *Feel free to bring along a friend to the party.*
  - 파티에 친구를 데려와도 괜찮아요.

> **A:** Can I bring along my younger sister to the event?
>
> **B:** Of course — the more the merrier!

### let in — 들여보내다

- *Could you let me in? I forgot my key again.*
  - 저 들여보내 줄 수 있어요? 열쇠를 또 잊었어요.

> **A:** There's someone at the door — should I let them in?
>
> **B:** Yes, that should be the delivery person.

### go with — 선택하다, 어울리다

- *I'll go with the blue shirt for the interview.*
  - 면접을 위해 파란 셔츠를 선택할게요.

> **A:** Which logo design should we use for the website?
>
> **B:** Let's go with the simpler one — it looks more professional.

### back down — 뒤로 물러서다

- *He refused to back down despite the strong opposition.*
  - 강한 반대에도 불구하고 그는 물러서기를 거부했다.

> **A:** They're pressuring you to change your report findings.
>
> **B:** I won't back down — the data clearly supports my conclusion.

### run into — 우연히 발견하다, 마주치다

- *I ran into an old classmate at the grocery store today.*
  - 오늘 마트에서 옛 동창을 우연히 만났다.

> **A:** You look really surprised — what just happened?
>
> **B:** I just ran into my ex-boss in the elevator!

### take in — 이해하고 받아들이다

- *There was so much information to take in during the lecture.*
  - 강의 중에 받아들여야 할 정보가 너무 많았다.

> **A:** How was the orientation session?
>
> **B:** Overwhelming — too much to take in at once.

### cut across — 가로질러 가다

- *We cut across the park to reach the station faster.*
  - 우리는 역에 더 빨리 가기 위해 공원을 가로질러 갔다.

> **A:** We're going to be late for the movie.
>
> **B:** Let's cut across the field — it saves ten minutes.

### build up — 축적하다, 증가하다

- *Stress had been building up for weeks before she finally quit.*
  - 그녀가 결국 그만두기 전 몇 주 동안 스트레스가 쌓이고 있었다.

> **A:** Why did he suddenly lose his temper?
>
> **B:** Frustration had been building up for months.

### put out — (불이나 빛을) 끄다

- *The firefighters put out the blaze within an hour.*
  - 소방관들이 한 시간 안에 불을 껐다.

> **A:** Is the candle still burning on the table?
>
> **B:** No, I put it out before we left.

### drag out — 질질 끌다

- *The negotiations were dragged out for months with no result.*
  - 협상이 아무 결과 없이 몇 달 동안 질질 끌렸다.

> **A:** That meeting lasted three hours!
>
> **B:** I know — they always drag these things out.

### get away with — (나쁜 짓을 하고도) 처벌을 피하다

- *He thought he could get away with copying the report.*
  - 그는 보고서를 표절해도 처벌을 피할 수 있다고 생각했다.

> **A:** Did anyone notice you came in late today?
>
> **B:** I think I got away with it this time.

### look back on — 회상하다

- *Looking back on her college years, she felt truly grateful.*
  - 대학 시절을 회상하며 그녀는 진심으로 감사함을 느꼈다.

> **A:** Do you ever miss living abroad?
>
> **B:** Yes, I love looking back on those years.

### play down — 대수롭지 않게 말하다

- *He played down the seriousness of the accident in his report.*
  - 그는 보고서에서 사고의 심각성을 대수롭지 않게 말했다.

> **A:** Your injury looks really bad — are you okay?
>
> **B:** Don't play it down — please see a doctor.

### break with — 탈피하다, 단절하다

- *She decided to break with tradition and start her own business.*
  - 그녀는 전통에서 탈피하여 자신의 사업을 시작하기로 했다.

> **A:** Why didn't you follow your family into medicine?
>
> **B:** I wanted to break with tradition and try design.

### keep away — 가까이 가지 않다, 멀리하다

- *You should keep away from that area — it's under construction.*
  - 그 구역은 공사 중이니 가까이 가지 않는 게 좋아.

> **A:** Can I pet that stray dog over there?
>
> **B:** Keep away — we don't know if it's safe.

### come down with — 병에 걸리다

- *She came down with a cold right before her big presentation.*
  - 그녀는 중요한 발표 바로 직전에 감기에 걸렸다.

> **A:** Why wasn't Jake in class today?
>
> **B:** He came down with the flu last night.

### check off — 체크 표시하다

- *She checked off each task on the list as she finished it.*
  - 그녀는 일이 끝날 때마다 목록의 각 항목에 체크 표시를 했다.

> **A:** Did you complete everything on the agenda?
>
> **B:** Yes, I checked off every single item this morning.

### make for — 향하다

- *As soon as the rain started, we made for the nearest shelter.*
  - 비가 내리기 시작하자마자 우리는 가장 가까운 대피처로 향했다.

> **A:** The storm is getting worse — what should we do?
>
> **B:** Let's make for the parking garage right now.

### turn out — 결국 밝혀지다

- *It turned out that the missing keys were in her coat pocket.*
  - 잃어버린 열쇠가 그녀의 코트 주머니에 있었다는 게 결국 밝혀졌다.

> **A:** Did you enjoy the documentary?
>
> **B:** It turned out to be more interesting than I expected.

### follow through — 끝까지 해내다

- *He promised to help but never followed through on his word.*
  - 그는 돕겠다고 약속했지만 끝까지 해내지 않았다.

> **A:** Did Tom finish the project he started?
>
> **B:** No — he rarely follows through on anything.

### burst out — 갑자기 터뜨리다

- *She burst out laughing when she heard the punchline.*
  - 그녀는 펀치라인을 듣고 갑자기 웃음을 터뜨렸다.

> **A:** Why did everyone suddenly start laughing?
>
> **B:** Mark burst out with the most ridiculous joke.

### drop by — 잠깐 방문하다

- *Feel free to drop by my office if you have any questions.*
  - 질문이 있으면 언제든지 제 사무실에 잠깐 들르세요.

> **A:** I was nearby, so I thought I'd drop by.
>
> **B:** Great timing — I just made fresh coffee!

### rule over — 통치하다, 지배하다

- *The queen ruled over the vast kingdom for fifty years.*
  - 여왕은 50년 동안 광대한 왕국을 통치했다.

> **A:** Who ruled over this region in ancient times?
>
> **B:** A powerful dynasty ruled over it for three centuries.

### cut back — 줄이다

- *The company decided to cut back on travel expenses this year.*
  - 회사는 올해 출장비를 줄이기로 결정했다.

> **A:** We really need to save more money this month.
>
> **B:** Let's cut back on eating out for a start.

### hand down — 물려주다

- *This watch was handed down to me by my grandfather.*
  - 이 시계는 할아버지께서 나에게 물려주신 것이다.

> **A:** That ring looks incredibly antique.
>
> **B:** It was handed down through four generations of my family.

### look out — 조심하다, 밖을 내다보다

- *Look out! There's ice on the road ahead.*
  - 조심해! 앞 도로에 얼음이 있어.

> **A:** Look out for that step — it's really slippery.
>
> **B:** Thanks for the warning, I'll be careful.

### call off — 취소하다

- *They called off the game because of heavy rain.*
  - 폭우 때문에 경기가 취소되었다.

> **A:** Did you hear they called off the meeting today?
>
> **B:** Yes, we'll reschedule for next week.

### lay off — 해고하다

- *The company had to lay off fifty workers last month.*
  - 그 회사는 지난달 직원 50명을 해고해야 했다.

> **A:** I heard your firm laid off a lot of people.
>
> **B:** Yes, it was a really tough week for everyone.

### go on — 계속하다

- *Please go on — I want to hear the rest of your story.*
  - 계속하세요 — 나머지 이야기가 듣고 싶어요.

> **A:** Sorry, I didn't mean to interrupt you.
>
> **B:** No worries — please go on with what you were saying.

### keep back — 물러나 있다, 유지하다

- *The officers told the crowd to keep back from the scene.*
  - 경찰관들은 군중에게 현장에서 물러나 있으라고 했다.

> **A:** Why is everyone standing so far away?
>
> **B:** Police told us to keep back until it's safe.

### ease off — 약해지다, 완화되다

- *The heavy rain finally eased off by early morning.*
  - 폭우가 이른 아침이 되어서야 마침내 약해졌다.

> **A:** Is the traffic on the highway still bad?
>
> **B:** It's easing off now — you can head out soon.

### buy into — 믿다, 받아들이다

- *I just don't buy into the idea that shortcuts always work.*
  - 나는 지름길이 항상 통한다는 생각을 받아들이지 않는다.

> **A:** Do you buy into this new productivity trend?
>
> **B:** Not really — I need to see more evidence first.

### run over — 치다, 넘치다

- *The driver nearly ran over a dog that crossed suddenly.*
  - 운전자는 갑자기 뛰어든 개를 칠 뻔했다.

> **A:** The child ran right into the street!
>
> **B:** I know — the car almost ran him over.

### stick to — 고수하다

- *You should stick to your original plan and not panic.*
  - 당황하지 말고 원래 계획을 고수해야 해.

> **A:** Should we change our strategy midway through?
>
> **B:** No, let's stick to what we all agreed on.

### put aside — 제쳐두다

- *Let's put aside our differences and work as a team.*
  - 이견을 제쳐두고 팀으로 함께 일합시다.

> **A:** We keep arguing about this one small point.
>
> **B:** Can we just put it aside and move forward?

### back out — 발을 빼다, 철회하다

- *He backed out of the deal at the last minute.*
  - 그는 마지막 순간에 거래에서 발을 뺐다.

> **A:** Is Jake still joining us on the road trip?
>
> **B:** No, he backed out — said he's too busy now.

### count down — 남은 시간을 세다

- *We've been counting down the days to our vacation.*
  - 우리는 휴가까지 남은 날을 세고 있다.

> **A:** How many days until the big concert?
>
> **B:** Only three — I've been counting down all week!

### point to — 암시하다, 가리키다

- *All the evidence points to a serious software bug.*
  - 모든 증거가 심각한 소프트웨어 버그를 암시한다.

> **A:** Why do you think sales dropped this quarter?
>
> **B:** Everything points to the pricing change we made.

### give off — (냄새나 열을) 내뿜다

- *Those flowers give off a wonderful scent in the morning.*
  - 그 꽃들은 아침에 멋진 향기를 내뿜는다.

> **A:** What's that lovely smell coming from the kitchen?
>
> **B:** It's the candle — it gives off a lavender scent.

### knock down — 쓰러뜨리다

- *The strong wind knocked down several trees overnight.*
  - 강한 바람이 밤새 나무 몇 그루를 쓰러뜨렸다.

> **A:** Why is that old building suddenly gone?
>
> **B:** They knocked it down to build a new park.

### pay off — 성과를 내다

- *All those late study nights finally paid off when I passed.*
  - 늦게까지 공부한 밤들이 합격으로 마침내 성과를 냈다.

> **A:** Your hard work really paid off — congratulations!
>
> **B:** Thank you! I put in months of effort for this.

### set back — 지연시키다

- *The unexpected storm set back construction by two weeks.*
  - 예기치 못한 폭풍으로 공사가 2주 지연되었다.

> **A:** Is the new office building still on schedule?
>
> **B:** No, the supply delay set us back by a month.

  - 세 시간째 하이킹 후 피로가 시작되었다.

> **A:** You look exhausted — are you feeling okay?
>
> **B:** The jet lag is really setting in right now.

> **A:** Did the new actor seem confident on stage?

>
> **B:** Give me an hour — I'll think up something great.
