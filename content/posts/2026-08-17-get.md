## Session Overview

이번 세션에서는 일상 영어에서 가장 빈번하게 쓰이는 동사 'get'을 활용한 구동사와 청크 약 100개를 집중적으로 학습했습니다. 쇼핑·환불·견적 같은 실용 표현부터 감정·건강·이동·인간관계·직장 관련 표현, 그리고 'get carried away', 'get the hang of it' 같은 숙어적 구어 표현까지 폭넓게 다뤘습니다. 이 표현들을 익혀 두면 원어민이 일상에서 자연스럽게 구사하는 말하기 패턴에 훨씬 가깝게 다가갈 수 있습니다.

## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### Can I get a coffee? — 커피 한 잔 주시겠어요? (주문할 때 가장 흔히 쓰는 표현)

- *Can I get a coffee and a blueberry muffin, please?*
  - 커피 한 잔이랑 블루베리 머핀 하나 주시겠어요?

> **A:** Welcome! What can I get for you today?
>
> **B:** Can I get a coffee, please? Medium size.

### Can I get the check, please? — 계산서 좀 주시겠어요?

- *We're done eating — can I get the check, please?*
  - 다 먹었으니 계산서 좀 주시겠어요?

> **A:** Can I get the check, please? We're in a hurry.
>
> **B:** Of course! I'll bring it right away.

### get a deal — 좋은 거래를 성사시키다, 싸게 사다

- *I got a great deal on my new laptop at the sale.*
  - 세일 때 새 노트북을 아주 싸게 샀어요.

> **A:** How much did you pay for those shoes?
>
> **B:** Only thirty dollars — I got a great deal!

### get a refund — 환불받다

- *The jacket didn't fit, so I got a refund.*
  - 재킷이 맞지 않아서 환불을 받았어요.

> **A:** Did you end up keeping that blender?
>
> **B:** No, it was broken, so I got a refund.

### get a receipt — 영수증을 받다

- *Always get a receipt when you buy something expensive.*
  - 비싼 물건을 살 때는 항상 영수증을 받으세요.

> **A:** Should I just pay and leave?
>
> **B:** Make sure you get a receipt in case there's a problem.

### get a bargain — 싼 물건을 건지다

- *She got a bargain at the flea market — only five dollars!*
  - 그녀는 벼룩시장에서 5달러짜리 싼 물건을 건졌어요.

> **A:** That coat looks expensive — was it pricey?
>
> **B:** Not at all! I got a bargain during the clearance sale.

### get a quote — 견적을 받다

- *I need to get a quote from the plumber before deciding.*
  - 결정하기 전에 배관공에게 견적을 받아야 해요.

> **A:** Are you going with that contractor?
>
> **B:** Not yet — I want to get a quote from a few others first.

### get an estimate — 견적을 내다

- *We got an estimate for the bathroom renovation last week.*
  - 지난주에 욕실 리모델링 견적을 받았어요.

> **A:** How much will it cost to fix the roof?
>
> **B:** I don't know yet — we need to get an estimate.

### get a sample — 샘플을 받다

- *You can get a sample of the perfume before buying the full bottle.*
  - 정품을 사기 전에 향수 샘플을 받아볼 수 있어요.

> **A:** I'm not sure which flavor to choose.
>
> **B:** Why don't you get a sample of each and try them?

### get online — 인터넷에 접속하다

- *I couldn't get online because the Wi-Fi was down.*
  - 와이파이가 끊겨서 인터넷에 접속할 수 없었어요.

> **A:** Did you see the announcement on the website?
>
> **B:** No, I couldn't get online at the airport.

### get a tan — 햇빛에 타다, 구릿빛 피부를 만들다

- *She got a tan after spending a week on the beach.*
  - 해변에서 일주일을 보낸 후 그녀는 구릿빛 피부가 됐어요.

> **A:** Wow, you look so healthy! Did you go somewhere?
>
> **B:** Yeah, I went to Jeju and got a tan.

### get a sunburn — 햇빛에 화상 입다

- *Don't forget sunscreen or you'll get a sunburn.*
  - 선크림 바르는 거 잊지 마세요, 안 그러면 햇빛에 화상 입어요.

> **A:** Your skin looks really red — are you okay?
>
> **B:** I got a sunburn at the beach today.

### get a haircut — 머리를 자르다

- *I'm getting a haircut this afternoon at my usual salon.*
  - 오늘 오후에 단골 미용실에서 머리를 자를 거예요.

> **A:** You look different today — what changed?
>
> **B:** I got a haircut! Do you like it?

### get a manicure — 매니큐어를 받다, 손톱 손질을 하다

- *She got a manicure before the wedding to look her best.*
  - 그녀는 결혼식 전에 손톱 손질을 받아 최상의 모습을 연출했어요.

> **A:** Your nails look amazing! Did you do them yourself?
>
> **B:** No, I got a manicure at the nail salon downtown.

### get a massage — 마사지를 받다

- *After a long week at work, I got a massage to relax.*
  - 힘든 한 주를 보낸 후 긴장을 풀려고 마사지를 받았어요.

> **A:** You seem so relaxed — what did you do today?
>
> **B:** I got a massage. My shoulders feel so much better now.

### get in shape — 몸매를 가꾸다, 건강을 유지하다

- *He started jogging every morning to get in shape.*
  - 그는 몸매를 가꾸려고 매일 아침 조깅을 시작했어요.

> **A:** Why are you going to the gym so often lately?
>
> **B:** I want to get in shape before summer.

### get out of shape — 체력이 떨어지다, 몸매가 망가지다

- *I got out of shape after skipping the gym for two months.*
  - 두 달 동안 헬스장을 빠졌더니 몸이 망가졌어.

> **A:** You seem out of breath just from climbing the stairs.
>
> **B:** I know — I've really gotten out of shape lately.

### get a checkup — 건강 검진을 받다

- *You should get a checkup at least once a year.*
  - 적어도 1년에 한 번은 건강 검진을 받아야 해.

> **A:** When did you last visit the doctor?
>
> **B:** I got a checkup last month — everything looked fine.

### get a shot — 주사를 맞다

- *I had to get a shot before traveling abroad.*
  - 해외여행 전에 주사를 맞아야 했어.

> **A:** Are you afraid of needles?
>
> **B:** A little, but I have to get a shot today anyway.

### get pregnant — 임신하다

- *She got pregnant after trying for two years.*
  - 그녀는 2년간 노력한 끝에 임신했어.

> **A:** I heard Sarah has some exciting news!
>
> **B:** Yes, she got pregnant — she's due in the spring.

### get excited — 신이 나다, 흥분하다

- *The kids got excited when they heard about the trip.*
  - 아이들은 여행 소식을 듣고 신이 났어.

> **A:** We're going to Disneyland next weekend!
>
> **B:** Really? I'm already getting so excited!

### get nervous — 긴장하다

- *I always get nervous before a big presentation.*
  - 나는 큰 발표 전에 항상 긴장해.

> **A:** How do you feel about your interview tomorrow?
>
> **B:** Honestly, I'm starting to get nervous about it.

### get stressed — 스트레스를 받다

- *She gets stressed easily when deadlines pile up.*
  - 마감이 쌓이면 그녀는 쉽게 스트레스를 받아.

> **A:** You look tense — what's going on?
>
> **B:** I always get stressed when there's too much work.

### get bored — 지루해지다

- *He gets bored quickly if a movie is too slow.*
  - 영화가 너무 느리면 그는 금방 지루해져.

> **A:** Why did you leave the lecture early?
>
> **B:** I got bored — it wasn't interesting at all.

### get depressed — 우울해지다

- *Many people get depressed during the long winter months.*
  - 많은 사람들이 긴 겨울 동안 우울해져.

> **A:** You've been so quiet lately. Are you okay?
>
> **B:** I tend to get depressed when I'm under too much pressure.

### get annoyed — 짜증이 나다

- *I get annoyed when people talk loudly on the phone.*
  - 사람들이 전화 통화를 크게 하면 짜증이 나.

> **A:** Why did you move to a different table?
>
> **B:** I got annoyed by the noisy group next to me.

### get embarrassed — 당황스럽다, 부끄러워지다

- *She got embarrassed when she called the teacher 'Mom'.*
  - 그녀는 선생님을 '엄마'라고 불러서 당황스러웠어.

> **A:** Why is your face so red right now?
>
> **B:** I got embarrassed when I tripped in front of everyone.

### get scared — 무서워지다

- *The children got scared during the thunderstorm last night.*
  - 아이들은 어젯밤 천둥번개가 치는 동안 무서워졌어.

> **A:** Did you watch that horror film alone?
>
> **B:** Yes, and I got scared halfway through!

### get confused — 혼란스러워지다

- *I get confused when the instructions are too complicated.*
  - 설명이 너무 복잡하면 나는 혼란스러워져.

> **A:** Can you explain step three again?
>
> **B:** Sure — I got confused there the first time too.

### get curious — 궁금해지다

- *She got curious about the strange noise coming from the attic.*
  - 그녀는 다락방에서 나는 이상한 소리가 궁금해졌어.

> **A:** What made you start learning about astronomy?
>
> **B:** I got curious after watching a documentary about space.

### get attention — 관심을 끌다

- *The colorful poster helped them get attention at the fair.*
  - 화려한 포스터 덕분에 박람회에서 관심을 끌 수 있었어.

> **A:** How did your small brand grow so quickly?
>
> **B:** We got attention by posting short, funny videos online.

### get permission — 허락을 받다

- *You need to get permission before using those photos.*
  - 그 사진들을 사용하기 전에 허락을 받아야 해.

> **A:** Can I publish your article on our website?
>
> **B:** Please get permission from the author first.

### get a chance — 기회를 얻다

- *I finally got a chance to travel to Europe this summer.*
  - 이번 여름에 드디어 유럽 여행을 할 기회를 얻었어.

> **A:** Have you ever tried rock climbing?
>
> **B:** Not yet, but I hope to get a chance this year.

### get priority — 우선권을 얻다

- *Premium members get priority access to all new features.*
  - 프리미엄 회원들은 모든 새 기능에 우선 접근 권한을 얻어.

> **A:** Why did those passengers board the plane first?
>
> **B:** Business class travelers get priority at the gate.

### get the hang of it — (요령을) 터득하다, 감을 잡다

- *It took me a week to get the hang of it.*
  - 그것에 익숙해지는 데 일주일이 걸렸어요.

> **A:** Is the new software confusing you?
>
> **B:** A little, but I'm starting to get the hang of it.

### get access to — ~에 접근 권한을 얻다

- *She finally got access to the company's internal database.*
  - 그녀는 마침내 회사 내부 데이터베이스에 접근 권한을 얻었어요.

> **A:** Can I see the project files?
>
> **B:** I'll help you get access to the shared folder today.

### get an advantage — 유리한 고지를 점하다

- *Preparing early helps you get an advantage over competitors.*
  - 미리 준비하면 경쟁자들보다 유리한 고지를 점할 수 있어요.

> **A:** How do you always stay ahead in sales?
>
> **B:** I research clients first to get an advantage.

### get revenge — 복수하다

- *He wanted to get revenge on the people who betrayed him.*
  - 그는 자신을 배신한 사람들에게 복수하고 싶었어요.

> **A:** Are you still angry at your old boss?
>
> **B:** I don't want to get revenge — I'll just move on.

### get a patent — 특허를 받다

- *The startup worked for years to get a patent for their device.*
  - 그 스타트업은 자신들의 기기에 대한 특허를 받으려고 수년간 노력했어요.

> **A:** Did your invention ever get approved?
>
> **B:** Yes! We finally got a patent for it last month.

### get the green light — 승인을 받다, 허가떨어지다

- *We got the green light to launch the new marketing campaign.*
  - 우리는 새 마케팅 캠페인을 시작하라는 승인을 받았어요.

> **A:** Can we start the project this week?
>
> **B:** We just got the green light from the director!

### get home — 집에 도착하다

- *I usually get home around seven in the evening.*
  - 저는 보통 저녁 7시쯤 집에 도착해요.

> **A:** What time did you get home last night?
>
> **B:** Really late — I didn't get home until midnight.

### get to work — 회사에 도착하다

- *She takes the subway every morning to get to work.*
  - 그녀는 매일 아침 지하철을 타고 회사에 도착해요.

> **A:** How long does it take you to get to work?
>
> **B:** About 40 minutes by bus — it's not bad.

### get away — 휴가를 가다, 떠나다

- *We really need to get away for the weekend.*
  - 우리 이번 주말에 정말 어디론가 떠날 필요가 있어요.

> **A:** You look exhausted lately.
>
> **B:** I know — I just need to get away for a few days.

### get back — 돌아오다

- *When did you get back from your business trip?*
  - 출장에서 언제 돌아왔어요?

> **A:** Is Jenny in the office today?
>
> **B:** Yes, she got back from vacation this morning.

### get in — (차에) 타다

- *Get in the car — we're already running late!*
  - 차에 타, 우리 이미 늦었어!

> **A:** Should I take a taxi or the bus?
>
> **B:** Just get in my car; I'll drop you off.

### get out — (방이나 차에서) 나가다

- *Let's get out of the car and stretch our legs.*
  - 차에서 내려서 다리 좀 펴요.

> **A:** This party is so boring.
>
> **B:** Agreed — let's get out of here right now.

### get on — (버스나 지하철에) 타다

- *Hurry up and get on the bus before the doors close!*
  - 문이 닫히기 전에 얼른 버스에 타!

> **A:** Which stop do we get on at?
>
> **B:** Get on at City Hall — it's only two stops away.

### get off — (버스나 지하철에서) 내리다

- *We need to get off at the next station.*
  - 우리 다음 역에서 내려야 해요.

> **A:** Where do we get off for the museum?
>
> **B:** Get off at Gyeongbokgung Station — it's right there.

### get across — (길을) 건너다, (뜻을) 전달하다

- *It was hard to get my point across during the meeting.*
  - 회의 중에 내 요점을 전달하기가 힘들었어요.

> **A:** Did the team understand your proposal?
>
> **B:** Not fully — I struggled to get my idea across.

### get through — (문이나 난관을) 통과하다, 빠져나가다

- *She managed to get through the final exam despite the stress.*
  - 그녀는 스트레스에도 불구하고 기말시험을 통과해냈어요.

> **A:** How was your interview?
>
> **B:** Really tough, but I somehow got through it.

### get married — 결혼하다

- *They plan to get married in a small ceremony next spring.*
  - 그들은 내년 봄 소규모 결혼식을 올릴 계획이에요.

> **A:** Have you heard the news about Tom and Lisa?
>
> **B:** Yes! They're getting married in June — so exciting!

### get engaged — 약혼하다

- *He got engaged to his girlfriend on a trip to Paris.*
  - 그는 파리 여행 중에 여자친구와 약혼했어요.

> **A:** Why is Sarah wearing a ring on that finger?
>
> **B:** She just got engaged — her boyfriend proposed last night!

### get divorced — 이혼하다

- *My aunt got divorced after twenty years of marriage.*
  - 내 이모는 20년 결혼 생활 끝에 이혼했다.

> **A:** Did you hear? Jake and Lisa got divorced last month.
>
> **B:** Really? They always seemed so happy together.

### get along with — ~와 사이좋게 지내다

- *I get along with my coworkers really well.*
  - 나는 동료들과 정말 사이좋게 지낸다.

> **A:** How do you get along with your new roommate?
>
> **B:** Great! We have a lot in common.

### get acquainted with — ~와 알게 되다, 안면을 트다

- *Let's take some time to get acquainted with each other.*
  - 서로 알게 될 시간을 가져 보자.

> **A:** Have you gotten acquainted with the new team members yet?
>
> **B:** Not yet, but I plan to introduce myself today.

### get even with — ~에게 앙갚음하다

- *He wanted to get even with his rival for spreading rumors.*
  - 그는 소문을 퍼뜨린 경쟁자에게 앙갚음하고 싶었다.

> **A:** Are you still angry at Tom?
>
> **B:** I just want to get even with him for what he did.

### get close to — ~와 친해지다

- *She got close to her neighbor after they started jogging together.*
  - 그녀는 함께 조깅을 시작한 후 이웃과 친해졌다.

> **A:** Have you gotten close to anyone at your new school?
>
> **B:** Yes, I'm getting close to a girl named Mia.

### get back together — (헤어졌다가) 다시 사귀다[결합하다]

- *After three months apart, they decided to get back together.*
  - 세 달간 헤어진 후, 그들은 다시 사귀기로 결심했다.

> **A:** I heard you and Ryan got back together!
>
> **B:** We did — we realized we missed each other too much.

### get fired — 해고당하다

- *He got fired for being late to work too many times.*
  - 그는 지각을 너무 자주 해서 해고당했다.

> **A:** Why did Mark suddenly leave the company?
>
> **B:** He got fired after missing several important deadlines.

### get hired — 채용되다, 고용되다

- *She got hired as a manager right after her interview.*
  - 그녀는 면접 직후 매니저로 채용되었다.

> **A:** Did you hear back from the company yet?
>
> **B:** Yes! I got hired — I start on Monday.

### get things done — 일을 처리하다[완료하다]

- *She always finds a way to get things done under pressure.*
  - 그녀는 압박 속에서도 항상 일을 처리하는 방법을 찾는다.

> **A:** How do you manage such a busy schedule?
>
> **B:** I make a list and just get things done one by one.

### get ready — 준비하다

- *You need to get ready quickly — the bus leaves in ten minutes.*
  - 빨리 준비해야 해 — 버스가 10분 후에 출발해.

> **A:** Are you almost ready to go?
>
> **B:** Give me five more minutes to get ready.

### get started — 시작하다

- *Let's get started on the project before it gets too late.*
  - 너무 늦기 전에 프로젝트를 시작하자.

> **A:** Should we wait for everyone before we get started?
>
> **B:** No, let's get started — the others can join us later.

### get to the point — 본론으로 들어가다

- *Please get to the point — we only have a few minutes left.*
  - 본론으로 들어가 주세요 — 시간이 몇 분밖에 안 남았어요.

> **A:** You've been talking for a while and I'm confused.
>
> **B:** Sorry — let me get to the point. We need more budget.

### get over it — 잊어버려라, 극복하다

- *I know it hurts, but you need to get over it and move on.*
  - 힘들다는 거 알지만, 극복하고 앞으로 나아가야 해.

> **A:** I'm still so upset that I failed the exam.
>
> **B:** Get over it and just study harder for the next one.

### get rid of — 없애다, 버리다

- *We need to get rid of all these old boxes in the garage.*
  - 차고에 있는 오래된 상자들을 모두 없애야 해.

> **A:** This room is such a mess!
>
> **B:** Let's get rid of everything we haven't used in a year.

### get around a problem — 문제를 우회하여 해결하다

- *We found a clever way to get around the budget problem.*
  - 우리는 예산 문제를 우회하여 해결하는 영리한 방법을 찾았다.

> **A:** The main road is blocked — what do we do?
>
> **B:** Don't worry, I know how to get around that problem.

### get out of hand — 통제 불능이 되다

- *The party got out of hand when too many uninvited guests arrived.*
  - 초대받지 않은 손님이 너무 많이 오자 파티는 통제 불능이 되었다.

> **A:** How did that small argument turn into such a big fight?
>
> **B:** It just got out of hand before anyone could stop it.

### get into trouble — 곤경에 빠지다

- *He got into trouble for cheating on the exam.*
  - 그는 시험에서 부정행위를 해서 곤경에 빠졌다.

> **A:** Why does your brother look so worried today?
>
> **B:** He got into trouble at school and has to see the principal.

### get out of trouble — 곤경에서 벗어나다

- *She called a lawyer to help her get out of trouble.*
  - 그녀는 곤경에서 벗어나기 위해 변호사에게 전화했다.

> **A:** How did you manage to get out of trouble so quickly?
>
> **B:** I explained everything honestly and they understood.

### get up — 일어나다

- *I get up at 6 a.m. every morning to exercise.*
  - 나는 매일 아침 6시에 일어나 운동을 해.

> **A:** What time do you usually get up on weekdays?
>
> **B:** Around seven — I need at least two cups of coffee.

### get down — 내려오다, 우울하게 하다

- *Don't let one bad grade get you down.*
  - 나쁜 성적 하나로 우울해하지 마.

> **A:** Rainy weather really gets me down.
>
> **B:** Try some upbeat music — it always helps me.

### get by — 그럭저럭 살아가다

- *She gets by on a small salary and careful budgeting.*
  - 그녀는 적은 월급으로 알뜰하게 그럭저럭 살아간다.

> **A:** How are you managing with fewer work hours?
>
> **B:** It's tough, but we're getting by.

### get ahead — 출세하다, 성공하다

- *He worked extra hours to get ahead in his career.*
  - 그는 출세하기 위해 야근을 마다하지 않았다.

> **A:** Any tips for getting ahead in this company?
>
> **B:** Show initiative and volunteer for new projects.

### get back at — 복수하다

- *She wanted to get back at him for spreading rumors.*
  - 그녀는 소문을 퍼뜨린 그에게 복수하고 싶었다.

> **A:** Are you planning to get back at Jake for what he did?
>
> **B:** No, I'd rather just move on and forget it.

### get behind — 뒤처지다

- *I got behind on my assignments during finals week.*
  - 기말고사 기간에 과제가 뒤처져 버렸어.

> **A:** You look really stressed — what's going on?
>
> **B:** I got behind at work and now I'm swamped.

### get across to — ~에게 이해시키다

- *The teacher struggled to get the concept across to her students.*
  - 선생님은 학생들에게 그 개념을 이해시키려 애썼다.

> **A:** I just can't get this idea across to my boss.
>
> **B:** Try a simple chart — visuals always help.

### get round to — (마침내) ~하게 되다

- *I finally got round to fixing the leaky faucet last weekend.*
  - 지난 주말에 드디어 새는 수도꼭지를 고쳤어.

> **A:** Have you called the landlord yet?
>
> **B:** Not yet, but I'll get round to it tonight, I promise.

### get something off one's chest — (고민 등을) 털어놓다

- *He felt much better after getting his worries off his chest.*
  - 그는 고민을 털어놓고 나서 훨씬 마음이 편해졌다.

> **A:** You look like something's really bothering you.
>
> **B:** Can I get this off my chest? I'm completely overwhelmed.

### get under one's skin — 신경을 긁다, 짜증 나게 하다

- *His constant complaining really gets under my skin.*
  - 그의 끊임없는 불평이 정말 신경을 긁어.

> **A:** Does your coworker bother you that much?
>
> **B:** He just gets under my skin with every little comment.

### get a life — 제대로 좀 살아라, 간섭 좀 마라

- *Stop obsessing over my choices and get a life!*
  - 내 선택에 집착 좀 그만하고 네 인생이나 살아!

> **A:** You're judging everyone's posts again?
>
> **B:** Get a life — go outside and do something real.

### get real — 현실을 직시해라, 꿈 깨라

- *Get real — you can't finish that project in one day.*
  - 꿈 깨 — 그 프로젝트를 하루 만에 끝낼 순 없어.

> **A:** I think I can learn fluent Spanish in a month.
>
> **B:** Get real — it takes years of consistent practice.

### get a move on — 서두르다

- *We need to get a move on or we'll miss the train.*
  - 서두르지 않으면 기차를 놓칠 거야.

> **A:** The movie starts in ten minutes!
>
> **B:** Then get a move on — grab your coat and let's go.

### get a grip — 정신 차리다, 진정하다

- *Take a deep breath and get a grip before your interview.*
  - 면접 전에 깊게 숨 쉬고 정신 차려.

> **A:** I'm completely panicking about the presentation.
>
> **B:** Get a grip — you've practiced this a hundred times.

### get a clue — 눈치 좀 채라, 정신 좀 차려라

- *He still doesn't get a clue about why she's upset.*
  - 그는 아직도 그녀가 왜 화났는지 눈치를 못 채.

> **A:** Why does he keep making the same mistake?
>
> **B:** I know — he really needs to get a clue.

### get the picture — 상황을 파악하다

- *After reading the report, I finally got the picture.*
  - 보고서를 읽고 나서야 드디어 상황을 파악했어.

> **A:** So sales dropped because of the new pricing strategy?
>
> **B:** Exactly — now you're getting the picture.

### get the message — 눈치를 채다, 경고를 알아듣다

- *She ignored his calls until he finally got the message.*
  - 그가 드디어 눈치를 챌 때까지 그녀는 전화를 무시했다.

> **A:** Did he stop texting after you said you were busy?
>
> **B:** Yes, he finally got the message and backed off.

### get off my back — 그만 좀 잔소리해라

- *I'll clean my room later — just get off my back!*
  - 나중에 방 청소할 거니까 — 그만 좀 잔소리해!

> **A:** You really need to study harder for the exam.
>
> **B:** I know, I know — get off my back, please.

### get carried away — 흥분하다, 도를 넘다

- *She got carried away decorating and bought way too much.*
  - 그녀는 장식하다가 흥분해서 너무 많이 사버렸어.

> **A:** Why did you buy five pairs of shoes?
>
> **B:** I got carried away during the sale — totally regret it.

### get cracking — 빨리 시작하다

- *We need to get cracking if we want to finish on time.*
  - 제시간에 끝내려면 빨리 시작해야 해.

> **A:** The deadline is in two hours!
>
> **B:** Then let's get cracking right now!

### get one's way — 원하는 대로 하다

- *She always gets her way during team meetings.*
  - 그녀는 팀 회의에서 항상 원하는 대로 한다.

> **A:** Did he agree to your plan in the end?
>
> **B:** Yes, I finally got my way.

### get a word in edgewise — 간신히 말할 틈을 얻다

- *He talked so fast I couldn't get a word in edgewise.*
  - 그가 너무 빨리 말해서 나는 간신히 말할 틈도 없었다.

> **A:** How was the meeting with Jake?
>
> **B:** Exhausting — I couldn't get a word in edgewise.

### get a load of this — 이것 좀 봐라

- *Get a load of this — they offered me a promotion!*
  - 이것 좀 봐라 — 그들이 나한테 승진을 제안했어!

> **A:** Get a load of this new phone feature!
>
> **B:** Wow, that's seriously impressive!

### get a breath of fresh air — 바람을 쐬다, 기분 전환하다

- *Let's step outside and get a breath of fresh air.*
  - 밖에 나가서 바람 좀 쐬자.

> **A:** You look really stressed. Want a break?
>
> **B:** Yes, let's go get a breath of fresh air.

### get on someone's nerves — ~의 신경을 건드리다

- *His constant humming is really getting on my nerves.*
  - 그의 끊임없는 흥얼거림이 정말 내 신경을 건드리고 있어.

> **A:** Why do you look so annoyed today?
>
> **B:** My coworker's loud music is getting on my nerves.

### get down to business — 본격적으로 일에 착수하다

- *Enough small talk — let's get down to business.*
  - 잡담은 그만하고 본격적으로 일을 시작하자.

> **A:** Should we chat a bit more first?
>
> **B:** No, let's get down to business right away.

### get into character — (배우 등이) 배역에 몰입하다

  - 그녀는 악당 역할에 몰입하기 위해 몇 주를 보냈다.

> **A:** Are you ready for tomorrow's audition?

> **A:** Look at those two — they're kissing again!
>
> **B:** Seriously, just get a room already!

  - 말다툼이 너무 격해져서 그들은 거의 몸싸움을 벌일 뻔했다.

> **A:** Did the argument stay verbal at least?

**Q1.** She paid cash at the market but forgot to ____. Now she has no proof of purchase.

- get a receipt
- get a quote

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>get a receipt</strong> — 'get a receipt'는 '영수증을 받다'로 구매 증거가 없다는 문맥과 정확히 맞음. 'get a quote'(견적)나 'get online'(인터넷 접속)은 이 상황과 전혀 관계없음.</p></details>

**Q2.** The item broke on the first day, so she went straight back to the store to ____.

- get a refund
- get a sample
- get a deal
- get an estimate

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>get a refund</strong> — 불량품을 돌려받고 돈을 돌려받는 상황은 'get a refund'(환불받다)가 유일하게 적합함. 'get a sample'(샘플)이나 'get an estimate'(견적)는 구매 전 단계의 표현임.</p></details>

**Q3.** We submitted the proposal last week, and we just heard back — we ____! Construction on the new office can finally begin.

- got the green light
- got a patent
- got priority
- got an advantage

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>got the green light</strong> — 'got the green light'는 공식 승인을 받았다는 의미로 기쁨의 뉘앙스까지 담긴 표현. 'got a patent'(특허)나 'got priority'(우선권)는 이 축하의 맥락에 어색함.</p></details>

**Q4.** It took a few tries, but once she practiced enough she really started to ____ — everything clicked into place.

- get the hang of it
- get the picture
- get curious
- get ahead

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>get the hang of it</strong> — 'get the hang of it'는 반복 연습 후 요령을 터득하는 상황에 딱 맞는 표현. 'get the picture'는 상황 파악, 'get curious'는 호기심, 'get ahead'는 성공·출세를 뜻해 이 문맥에 맞지 않음.</p></details>

**Q5.** She only meant to buy one pair of shoes, but she ____ and ended up spending her entire paycheck.

- got carried away
- got out of hand
- got into trouble
- got behind

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>got carried away</strong> — 'got carried away'는 본인이 흥분이나 충동에 휩쓸렸을 때 쓰는 표현으로 충동 소비 묘사에 자연스러움. 'got out of hand'는 상황 자체가 통제 불능이 될 때, 'got into trouble'은 곤경에 빠질 때 주로 씀.</p></details>

**Q6.** Breaking up was painful, but she knew she had to ____ and focus on her own happiness.

- get over it
- get back together
- get depressed
- get a life

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>get over it</strong> — 'get over it'는 힘든 일을 극복하고 앞으로 나아간다는 뜻으로 이별 후 회복 맥락에 정확히 맞음. 'get back together'(재결합)는 반대 방향이고, 'get depressed'(우울해지다)는 포기의 의미임.</p></details>

**Q7.** It's impossible to ____ when he's talking — he never stops long enough for anyone else to speak.

- get a word in edgewise
- get the message
- get attention
- get to the point

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>get a word in edgewise</strong> — 'get a word in edgewise'는 상대방이 쉬지 않고 말해 간신히 끼어들 틈도 없는 상황에 딱 맞는 숙어. 나머지 세 표현은 이 문맥에서 문법적으로 어색하거나 의미가 전혀 다름.</p></details>

**Q8.** After fourteen hours on the plane, the first thing she wanted to do at the hotel was ____.

- get a massage
