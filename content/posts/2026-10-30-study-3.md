## Session Overview

이번 세션에서는 공부와 업무 상황에서 자주 쓰이는 표현(burn the midnight oil, race against the clock)부터 일상적인 사교 대화 청크(catch up with, visit with each other), 전공·직업 소개 어휘(data science, software development), 그리고 정책과 시사 토론에 활용되는 고급 구동사(put a moratorium on, breach of contract, express worries about)까지 폭넓게 다루었습니다. 학습 동기 부여 표현과 일상 회화 청크를 함께 익혀 두면 실제 대화에서 바로 활용할 수 있습니다. 다양한 맥락에서 반복 연습(over and over again)하면서 각 표현의 뉘앙스를 자연스럽게 체득하는 것이 이번 세션의 핵심 목표입니다.

## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### embark on — ~을 시작하다, ~에 착수하다

- *We're excited to embark on this new project together.*
  - 우리는 함께 이 새로운 프로젝트를 시작하게 되어 설레요.

> **A:** Are you nervous about starting your trip to Europe?
>
> **B:** A little, but I'm ready to embark on this adventure.

### without further ado — 더 이상 지체하지 않고, 곧바로

- *Without further ado, let me introduce tonight's special guest.*
  - 더 이상 지체하지 않고, 오늘 밤의 특별 손님을 소개할게요.

> **A:** Everyone is waiting — should we get started?
>
> **B:** Yes! Without further ado, let's begin the show.

### jump into — 바로 ~을 시작하다, ~에 뛰어들다

- *Let's jump into the first topic on our agenda.*
  - 바로 우리 안건의 첫 번째 주제로 들어가 봅시다.

> **A:** We only have thirty minutes for this meeting.
>
> **B:** Okay, let's jump into the most important points first.

### focus on — ~에 집중하다

- *Try to focus on understanding the main idea, not every word.*
  - 모든 단어가 아니라 주요 내용을 이해하는 데 집중해 보세요.

> **A:** I keep getting distracted while I study.
>
> **B:** Put your phone away and focus on one task at a time.

### don't worry — 걱정하지 마세요

- *Don't worry if you make a mistake — just keep going.*
  - 실수해도 걱정하지 마세요 — 그냥 계속하면 돼요.

> **A:** I forgot some of my lines during the presentation.
>
> **B:** Don't worry — nobody else noticed at all.

### immerse yourself in — ~에 완전히 몰입하다

- *The best way to learn a language is to immerse yourself in it daily.*
  - 언어를 배우는 가장 좋은 방법은 매일 그 언어에 완전히 몰입하는 것이에요.

> **A:** How did you get so fluent in Spanish so quickly?
>
> **B:** I immersed myself in Spanish movies, music, and podcasts.

### move on to — ~으로 넘어가다, 다음 단계로 이동하다

- *Once everyone is ready, we'll move on to the next section.*
  - 모두 준비가 되면 다음 섹션으로 넘어가겠습니다.

> **A:** Have you finished reviewing chapter three?
>
> **B:** Yes, we can move on to chapter four now.

### work on — ~을 작업하다, ~에 매달리다

- *She stayed up late to work on her essay for class.*
  - 그녀는 수업 에세이를 작업하느라 늦게까지 깨어 있었어요.

> **A:** Why are you still at the library so late?
>
> **B:** I'm working on a paper that's due tomorrow morning.

### burn the midnight oil — 밤늦게까지 공부하거나 일하다

- *She burned the midnight oil to finish her report before dawn.*
  - 그녀는 새벽 전에 보고서를 끝내려고 밤새 불을 밝혔다.

> **A:** You look exhausted. Did you sleep at all?
>
> **B:** Not really — I was burning the midnight oil again.

### race against the clock — 마감 시간에 쫓기다, 시간과 싸우다

- *The team was racing against the clock to launch the app on time.*
  - 팀은 앱을 제때 출시하기 위해 시간과 싸우고 있었다.

> **A:** How's the project coming along?
>
> **B:** We're really racing against the clock — deadline's tonight.

### finish in time — 제때에 끝내다

- *I started early so I could finish the assignment in time.*
  - 나는 과제를 제때 끝낼 수 있도록 일찍 시작했다.

> **A:** Do you think you'll finish in time for the presentation?
>
> **B:** I hope so — I only have two sections left.

### in the same boat — 같은 처지에 있다, 같은 상황이다

- *Don't worry — all of us are in the same boat this semester.*
  - 걱정 마 — 우리 모두 이번 학기에 같은 처지야.

> **A:** I feel like I'm the only one struggling with these deadlines.
>
> **B:** Trust me, we're all in the same boat right now.

### catch up with — 나중에 다시 만나 이야기를 나누다

- *Let's catch up with each other after the exams are over.*
  - 시험이 끝나고 나서 다시 만나 얘기 나누자.

> **A:** I have to run, but I'll catch up with you later, okay?
>
> **B:** Sure! Text me when you're free.

### tackle — (어려운 과제나 문제를) 처리하다, 해결하다

- *She sat down and tackled her three assignments one by one.*
  - 그녀는 자리에 앉아 세 개의 과제를 하나씩 처리했다.

> **A:** Where do you even start with so much homework?
>
> **B:** I just tackle the hardest one first and go from there.

### all the luck I can get — 최대한의 운이 필요하다, 운이 닿는 대로 다 필요하다

- *This is my first job interview, so I need all the luck I can get.*
  - 첫 취업 면접이라서 운이 최대한 필요해요.

> **A:** Your big presentation is tomorrow. Are you ready?
>
> **B:** Not really — I'll need all the luck I can get!

### open an account — 계정(계좌)을 개설하다

- *You can open an account on the app in less than a minute.*
  - 앱에서 1분도 안 돼서 계정을 만들 수 있어요.

> **A:** How do I start using this platform?
>
> **B:** Just open an account and you're all set.

### try your best — 최선을 다하다

- *Don't worry about the score — just try your best.*
  - 점수 걱정 말고 그냥 최선을 다하세요.

> **A:** I'm nervous. What if I make mistakes?
>
> **B:** That's okay — just try your best and you'll be fine.

### time to shine — 빛날 시간, 실력을 발휘할 때

- *You've practiced all week — now it's your time to shine.*
  - 일주일 내내 연습했으니 이제 실력을 발휘할 시간이에요.

> **A:** The stage is all yours. Everyone's waiting!
>
> **B:** I'm ready — it's my time to shine.

### out loud — 소리 내어, 큰 소리로

- *Read the sentences out loud to practice your pronunciation.*
  - 발음 연습을 위해 문장을 소리 내어 읽으세요.

> **A:** Should I just read this in my head?
>
> **B:** No, say it out loud — that's how you really learn it.

### don't be shy — 부끄러워하지 마세요, 망설이지 마세요

- *Don't be shy — raise your hand if you have a question.*
  - 부끄러워하지 말고 질문 있으면 손 드세요.

> **A:** I want to answer but I'm afraid of getting it wrong.
>
> **B:** Don't be shy! Making mistakes is how we learn.

### get started — 시작하다

- *We have a lot to cover today, so let's get started right away.*
  - 오늘 다룰 내용이 많으니 바로 시작합시다.

> **A:** Is everyone here? Should we wait for more people?
>
> **B:** Let's just get started — they can catch up later.

### halfway through — 절반쯤 진행된, 반도 못 한

- *I'm not even halfway through the report yet.*
  - 아직 보고서를 절반도 못 썼어.

> **A:** How's your essay going?
>
> **B:** Not great — I'm only halfway through and it's due tomorrow.

### I know the feeling — 나도 그 기분 알아, 충분히 이해해

- *You're exhausted after finals? I know the feeling.*
  - 기말고사 끝나고 지쳤다고? 나도 그 기분 알아.

> **A:** I've been stressed about this deadline all week.
>
> **B:** I know the feeling — I had three deadlines last week.

### working on — ~을 작업 중이다, ~에 매달려 있다

- *She's working on her assignment at the library right now.*
  - 그녀는 지금 도서관에서 과제를 하고 있어.

> **A:** Have you seen Emily? Is she free to hang out?
>
> **B:** No, she's working on an essay for Professor Smith's class.

### not even halfway through — 아직 절반도 못 끝낸 상태

- *I'm not even halfway through the reading assignment.*
  - 독서 과제를 아직 절반도 못 읽었어.

> **A:** How's the report coming along?
>
> **B:** Not great — I'm not even halfway through it.

### read out loud — 소리 내어 읽다

- *The teacher asked every student to read the answer out loud.*
  - 선생님은 모든 학생에게 답을 소리 내어 읽으라고 했어.

> **A:** I'm not sure I understand this sentence.
>
> **B:** Try reading it out loud — it sometimes helps.

### play one's time well — 시간을 잘 활용하다 / 시간 관리를 잘하다

- *He didn't play his time well and fell behind on every task.*
  - 그는 시간 관리를 잘 못 해서 모든 과제에서 뒤처졌어.

> **A:** Why do you have so much left to do tonight?
>
> **B:** Honestly, I just didn't play my time well today.

### be familiar with — ~을 잘 알다 / ~에 익숙하다

- *Are you familiar with the effects of social media on teenagers?*
  - 소셜 미디어가 청소년에게 미치는 영향을 잘 알고 있어?

> **A:** Have you heard of the term 'digital divide'?
>
> **B:** Yes, I'm quite familiar with it from my research.

### No worries — 괜찮아, 걱정 마

- *No worries — I can send you the notes later.*
  - 괜찮아 — 나중에 필기 보내줄게.

> **A:** Sorry I forgot to bring your book today.
>
> **B:** No worries, just bring it tomorrow.

### I wish I could help — 도움을 드리고 싶지만 (그럴 수 없어서 아쉽다)

- *I wish I could help, but I have my own deadlines to meet.*
  - 도움을 주고 싶지만, 나도 맞춰야 할 마감이 있어.

> **A:** Could you proofread my essay tonight?
>
> **B:** I wish I could help, but I'm swamped with my own work.

### good job — 잘했어, 수고했어

- *You finished all the exercises on time — good job!*
  - 모든 연습 문제를 제시간에 끝냈네요 — 잘했어요!

> **A:** I finally passed my English speaking test!
>
> **B:** Good job! All that practice really paid off.

### dive into — ~에 바로 뛰어들다, 곧장 시작하다

- *Let's dive into the shadowing practice and get started.*
  - 쉐도잉 연습에 바로 들어가 봅시다.

> **A:** Are you ready to begin the new unit?
>
> **B:** Yes, let's just dive into it right away.

### not only ~ but also — ~뿐만 아니라 ~도

- *Shadowing not only improves speaking but also sharpens listening.*
  - 쉐도잉은 말하기를 향상시킬 뿐만 아니라 듣기 실력도 키워줍니다.

> **A:** Why should I keep a vocabulary journal?
>
> **B:** It not only builds vocabulary but also improves writing.

### take ~ to the next level — ~을 한 단계 더 높이다, 발전시키다

- *Daily shadowing will take your English to the next level.*
  - 매일 쉐도잉을 하면 영어 실력을 한 단계 끌어올릴 수 있습니다.

> **A:** I feel like my English has stopped improving lately.
>
> **B:** Try speaking with natives — it'll take you to the next level.

### good luck with — ~에 행운을 빌다

- *Good luck with your presentation tomorrow — you've prepared well.*
  - 내일 발표에 행운을 빌어요 — 준비를 잘 하셨잖아요.

> **A:** I have a really tough job interview this afternoon.
>
> **B:** Good luck with that! I know you'll do great.

### I'll need all the luck I can get — 최대한 많은 행운이 필요하다 (어렵거나 불확실한 상황에서 쓰는 표현)

- *The exam is tomorrow and I'm not ready — I'll need all the luck I can get.*
  - 시험이 내일인데 준비가 안 됐어요 — 최대한 많은 행운이 필요해요.

> **A:** Good luck with your final exam next week, Sarah!
>
> **B:** Thanks — I'll need all the luck I can get, honestly.

### racing against the clock — 시간과 싸우다, 마감 시간에 쫓기다

- *The team was racing against the clock to fix the bug before launch.*
  - 팀은 출시 전에 버그를 수정하려고 시간과 싸우고 있었다.

> **A:** You look really stressed — are you okay?
>
> **B:** I'm racing against the clock to finish this essay tonight.

### know the feeling — 그 기분 알아, 나도 그런 적 있어 (공감 표현)

- *I know the feeling — pulling all-nighters is exhausting.*
  - 나도 그 기분 알아 — 밤새 공부하는 건 정말 힘들어.

> **A:** I haven't slept properly in three days because of deadlines.
>
> **B:** Trust me, I know the feeling — finals week is brutal.

### need all the luck I can get — 행운이 많이 필요하다 (어렵거나 불확실한 상황에서 쓰는 표현)

- *This exam is brutal — I'll need all the luck I can get.*
  - 이 시험 정말 힘들어 — 행운이 최대한 많이 필요해.

> **A:** Good luck on your job interview tomorrow!
>
> **B:** Thanks — I'll need all the luck I can get.

### over and over again — 반복해서, 몇 번이고 계속

- *She practiced the pronunciation over and over again until it felt natural.*
  - 그녀는 발음이 자연스러워질 때까지 반복해서 연습했다.

> **A:** How did you finally get the accent right?
>
> **B:** I just listened to native speakers over and over again.

### visit with — (누군가와) 편하게 이야기를 나누다, 담소하다

- *We had time to visit with our neighbors after the meeting.*
  - 회의가 끝난 후 우리는 이웃들과 담소를 나눌 시간이 있었다.

> **A:** What did you do while waiting for the bus?
>
> **B:** I just visited with the other passengers for a while.

### what's going on — 무슨 일이야, 어떻게 지내

- *Hey, long time no see — what's going on with you these days?*
  - 야, 오랜만이다 — 요즘 어떻게 지내?

> **A:** You look a little stressed. What's going on?
>
> **B:** Oh, I have a big presentation tomorrow, that's all.

### go on over — 저쪽으로 가다, 건너가다 (친근한 표현)

- *Once you finish signing in, go on over to the waiting area.*
  - 체크인을 마치면 대기 공간으로 가세요.

> **A:** Where should I sit for the group activity?
>
> **B:** Just go on over to that table in the corner.

### put it in — (무언가를) 안에 넣다

- *Put your homework in the folder before class starts.*
  - 수업 시작 전에 숙제를 폴더 안에 넣으세요.

> **A:** Where should I put my permission slip?
>
> **B:** Just put it in the tray on my desk.

### see what I'm saying — 내 말이 무슨 뜻인지 알겠어?, 내 말 이해해?

- *You need to review your notes every night — see what I'm saying?*
  - 매일 밤 노트를 복습해야 해 — 무슨 말인지 알겠지?

> **A:** If you skip practice, you'll fall behind. See what I'm saying?
>
> **B:** Yeah, I get it — I'll make sure I show up.

### go over there — 저쪽으로 가다, 그쪽으로 이동하다

- *Don't go over there during the test — stay in your seat.*
  - 시험 중에 저쪽으로 가지 마세요 — 자리에 앉아 있으세요.

> **A:** Can I go over there to borrow an eraser?
>
> **B:** No, wait until we finish this activity.

### visit with each other — 서로 잡담하다, 서로 어울려 이야기하다

- *Please don't visit with each other while I'm giving instructions.*
  - 제가 설명하는 동안 서로 잡담하지 마세요.

> **A:** Why do you keep talking to Sarah during work time?
>
> **B:** Sorry, we tend to visit with each other too much.

### respond to me — 나에게 대답하다, 내 말에 반응하다

- *When I ask a question, please respond to me right away.*
  - 제가 질문하면 바로 대답해 주세요.

> **A:** I called your name three times — you need to respond to me.
>
> **B:** Sorry, I didn't hear you. I'll pay attention.

### talk about — ~에 대해 이야기하다

- *What do you want to talk about today in class?*
  - 오늘 수업에서 무엇에 대해 이야기하고 싶어요?

> **A:** What do you want to talk about at lunch?
>
> **B:** Let's talk about our weekend plans!

### keep it up — 계속 유지하다, 잘 하고 있으니 계속해라

- *You're improving every week — keep it up!*
  - 매주 실력이 늘고 있어요 — 계속 그렇게 해요!

> **A:** I studied English for an hour every day this week.
>
> **B:** That's great — keep it up and you'll see big results!

### been together — 함께한 적이 있다, 같이 있어 본 적이 있다

- *We've never been together in the same study group before.*
  - 우리는 전에 같은 스터디 그룹에 함께한 적이 없어요.

> **A:** Have you and Maria ever been together in a class before?
>
> **B:** No, this is actually the first time!

### How long have you been here — 여기(이 나라/도시)에 얼마나 있었어요?

- *How long have you been here in Austin?*
  - 오스틴에 얼마나 계셨어요?

> **A:** How long have you been here in the United States?
>
> **B:** I've been here for about six months now.

### How about — ~는 어때요? (제안이나 화제 전환 시 사용)

- *How about we grab lunch after this meeting?*
  - 이 회의 끝나고 점심 먹는 건 어때요?

> **A:** I'm not sure where to go tonight.
>
> **B:** How about that new place on Main Street?

### move here — 이곳으로 이사 오다

- *She moved here from Seoul about two years ago.*
  - 그녀는 약 2년 전에 서울에서 이곳으로 이사 왔어요.

> **A:** When did you move here?
>
> **B:** I moved here last spring for a new job.

### two years ago — 2년 전에 (과거 시점을 나타내는 시간 표현)

- *Two years ago, I didn't speak any English at all.*
  - 2년 전에는 영어를 전혀 못했어요.

> **A:** Your English is really impressive now.
>
> **B:** Thanks! Two years ago I could barely say hello.

### be around — 근처에 있다, 이 일대에 있다

- *I'll be around the coffee shop if you need me.*
  - 필요하면 커피숍 근처에 있을게요.

> **A:** Are you leaving after the event?
>
> **B:** No, I'll still be around for a while.

### Do you have any — ~이/가 있나요? (소유나 존재를 묻는 표현)

- *Do you have any friends who speak Korean here?*
  - 여기에 한국어를 하는 친구가 있나요?

> **A:** Do you have any plans this weekend?
>
> **B:** Not yet — maybe just relax at home.

### How many — 몇 명/개나? (수량을 묻는 표현)

- *How many people are coming to the party?*
  - 파티에 몇 명이나 와요?

> **A:** I made a lot of food for tonight.
>
> **B:** How many guests are you expecting?

### What about you? — 당신은요? / 너는 어때? (화제를 상대방에게 돌릴 때 쓰는 표현)

- *I love hiking on weekends. What about you?*
  - 저는 주말에 하이킹을 즐겨요. 당신은요?

> **A:** I usually drink coffee in the morning. What about you?
>

### move to — (새로운 전략·방향으로) 전환하다

- *The government moved to a strategy of building on federal lands.*
  - 정부는 연방 토지에 건설하는 전략으로 전환했습니다.

> **A:** Why did they change their permit approach so suddenly?
>
> **B:** They moved to a faster approval process to cut delays.

### become contentious — 논란이 되다, 분쟁의 소지가 생기다

- *The debate over water rights quickly became contentious in the region.*
  - 수리권 논쟁은 그 지역에서 빠르게 논란이 되었습니다.

> **A:** How did the town hall meeting go last night?
>
> **B:** It became very contentious once they mentioned the tax increase.

### from the standpoint of — ~의 관점에서 (볼 때)

- *From the standpoint of the tech sector, federal land is a great option.*
  - 기술 업계의 관점에서 보면, 연방 토지는 훌륭한 선택지입니다.

> **A:** Do you think the merger is a good deal?
>
> **B:** From the standpoint of investors, it makes a lot of sense.

### bypass — 우회하다, (절차를) 건너뛰다

- *Building on federal land lets developers bypass local opposition.*
  - 연방 토지에 건설하면 개발자들은 지역 반대를 우회할 수 있습니다.

> **A:** How did they get the project approved so fast?
>
> **B:** They used a federal exemption to bypass the city council entirely.

### go beyond — ~을 넘어서다

- *This issue will go beyond Nevada and affect all of the Western states.*
  - 이 문제는 네바다를 넘어서 서부 전역에 영향을 미칠 것입니다.

> **A:** Will the new policy only affect our city?
>
> **B:** No, its impact will go beyond the city to the whole state.

### be a big issue — 큰 문제가 되다

- *Federal land use has been a big issue in the Western states for years.*
  - 연방 토지 사용은 수년간 서부 주들에서 큰 문제가 되어왔습니다.

> **A:** Why did the incumbent lose the election?
>
> **B:** Housing costs had been a big issue for most voters.

### suffer big losses — 큰 손실을 입다

- *Republicans could suffer big losses if voters turn against them.*
  - 유권자들이 등을 돌리면 공화당은 큰 손실을 입을 수 있습니다.

> **A:** How did the party perform in the local elections?
>
> **B:** They suffered big losses in nearly every competitive district.

### become reluctant to — ~하기를 꺼리게 되다

- *Officials have become reluctant to approve new data centers after the backlash.*
  - 반발 이후 공무원들은 새 데이터 센터 승인을 꺼리게 되었습니다.

> **A:** Will the city allow more warehouses to be built here?
>
> **B:** Unlikely — the council has become reluctant to approve large projects.

### move in that direction — 그 방향으로 나아가다

- *The administration has been moving in that direction with its recent policies.*
  - 행정부는 최근 정책들로 그 방향으로 나아가고 있습니다.

> **A:** Do you think they'll eventually use more federal land?
>
> **B:** It seems like they've already been moving in that direction.

### long-term strategy — 장기 전략

- *We need a long-term strategy to handle this growing problem.*
  - 우리는 이 커지는 문제를 다루기 위한 장기 전략이 필요하다.

> **A:** What's the company's plan for the next ten years?
>
> **B:** We're developing a long-term strategy for sustainable growth.

### be overtaken by — ~에 점령되다, ~에 추월당하다

- *Beautiful natural areas risk being overtaken by industrial development.*
  - 아름다운 자연 지역들이 산업 개발에 점령될 위험에 처해 있다.

> **A:** Have you been back to your hometown recently?
>
> **B:** Yes, it's been overtaken by warehouses and parking lots.

### engage in — ~에 참여하다, ~을 하다

- *The city decided not to engage in development on protected wetlands.*
  - 시는 보호된 습지에서 개발을 하지 않기로 결정했다.

> **A:** Why won't the government build anything on that land?
>
> **B:** They've chosen not to engage in any economic activity there.

### preserve for future generations — 미래 세대를 위해 보존하다

- *We must preserve these coastal ecosystems for future generations.*
  - 우리는 미래 세대를 위해 이 해안 생태계를 보존해야 한다.

> **A:** Why is the park protected from any construction?
>
> **B:** The goal is to preserve it for future generations.

### figure out — 알아내다, 이해하다

- *I've been trying to figure out why the system keeps failing.*
  - 나는 왜 시스템이 계속 실패하는지 알아내려고 노력해 왔다.

> **A:** Do you know how this new software works?
>
> **B:** Not yet — I'm still trying to figure it out.

### keep going on — 계속되다

- *The protests over data center noise are likely to keep going on.*
  - 데이터 센터 소음에 대한 시위는 계속될 것 같다.

> **A:** When do you think this dispute will finally end?
>
> **B:** Honestly, it's just going to keep going on for years.

### fight over — ~을 두고 싸우다

- *Communities fought over the placement of new power plants in the 1970s.*
  - 1970년대에 지역 사회들은 새 발전소 배치를 두고 싸웠다.

> **A:** Why is there so much tension in that town?
>
> **B:** They've been fighting over water rights for twenty years.

### organize against — ~에 반대하여 조직하다

- *Farmers organized against railroad companies that they believed had too much power.*
  - 농부들은 너무 많은 권력을 가졌다고 생각하는 철도 회사들에 반대하여 조직을 꾸렸다.

> **A:** How did residents respond to the new highway plan?
>
> **B:** They organized against it and packed every city council meeting.

### stretch the comparison — 비교를 과장하다, 억지로 비교하다

- *Am I stretching the comparison between today's tech giants and old railroad monopolies?*
  - 오늘날의 기술 대기업과 옛 철도 독점을 비교하는 것이 억지스러운가요?

> **A:** I think this CEO is basically like a medieval king.
>
> **B:** Aren't you stretching the comparison just a little bit?

### grassroots movement — 대중에서 자발적으로 생겨난 풀뿌리 운동

- *The grassroots movement against plastic waste is growing fast.*
  - 플라스틱 쓰레기에 반대하는 풀뿌리 운동이 빠르게 성장하고 있어.

> **A:** How did the campaign start? Nobody organized it.
>
> **B:** Exactly — it was a grassroots movement from ordinary people.

### it's no accident (that) — 우연이 아니다 (그럴 만한 이유가 있다)

- *It's no accident that she got promoted — she worked incredibly hard.*
  - 그녀가 승진한 건 우연이 아니야 — 정말 열심히 했거든.

> **A:** Why did they open their office in Seoul of all places?
>
> **B:** It's no accident — Seoul has the best tech talent in Asia.

### make the transition (from X to Y) — X에서 Y로 전환하다/이행하다

- *Many companies are making the transition from paper records to digital ones.*
  - 많은 회사들이 종이 기록에서 디지털 기록으로 전환하고 있어.

> **A:** Is switching from teaching to corporate work difficult?
>
> **B:** Yes, but once you make the transition, it gets much easier.

### take place — (행사·사건이) 일어나다, 발생하다

- *Major changes in the job market are taking place right now.*
  - 취업 시장에서 큰 변화들이 지금 일어나고 있어.

> **A:** When will the ceremony take place?
>
> **B:** It's taking place this Saturday at noon in the main hall.

### go far beyond — ~을 훨씬 넘어서다

  - AI의 영향은 단순 작업을 대체하는 것을 훨씬 넘어서.

> **A:** Is the data privacy issue only about hacking?
>
> **B:** No, it goes far beyond that — it's about power and control.

### upset about — ~에 대해 화가 나다/속상해하다

- *Both sides were upset about the sudden rule change.*
  - 양측 모두 갑작스러운 규칙 변경에 대해 화가 났어.

> **A:** Why is everyone so tense in the office today?
>
> **B:** People are upset about the new overtime policy.

### strange bedfellows — 공통점 없는 사람들이 같은 편이 되는 상황 (이상한 동침자들)

- *The merger made strange bedfellows out of longtime rivals.*
  - 그 합병은 오랜 라이벌들을 뜻밖의 동료로 만들었어.

> **A:** Did environmentalists and oil workers really join the same protest?
>
> **B:** Yes — politics makes strange bedfellows sometimes.

### come together — 한데 모이다, 뭉치다

- *Conservatives and liberals came together to oppose the new bill.*
  - 보수와 진보가 새 법안에 반대하기 위해 한데 뭉쳤어.

> **A:** How did you finish such a huge project so fast?
>
> **B:** The whole team came together and everyone gave their best.

### extend into — ~까지 이어지다/확장되다

- *The political debate could extend into the next election cycle.*
  - 그 정치적 논쟁은 다음 선거 주기까지 이어질 수 있어.

> **A:** Is the construction project almost done?
>
> **B:** Not even close — it'll extend into next spring at least.

### worried about — ~에 대해 걱정하다/우려하다

- *Many people are worried about the growing power of big tech companies.*
  - 많은 사람들이 거대 기술 기업들의 커지는 권력에 대해 우려하고 있어.

> **A:** Why are regulators getting involved in social media now?
>
> **B:** They're worried about how much user data these platforms control.

### define as — ~을 …으로 정의하다/규정하다

- *They united against what they defined as a common threat.*
  - 그들은 공통의 위협이라고 규정한 것에 맞서 단결했어.

> **A:** How do you define success in this project?
>
> **B:** I define it as completing on time with no major bugs.
