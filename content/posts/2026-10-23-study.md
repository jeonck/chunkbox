## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### get through — (힘든 상황·과정을) 이겨내다, 통과하다

- *She finally got through her first round of chemotherapy.*
  - 그녀는 마침내 1차 항암 치료를 버텨냈다.

> **A:** This training program is brutal—I'm not sure I can handle it.
>
> **B:** Just take it day by day; you'll get through it.

### get back to — ~로 돌아가다, 복귀하다

- *The doctor says he can get back to training in six weeks.*
  - 의사는 그가 6주 후에 훈련으로 복귀할 수 있다고 했다.

> **A:** When will you get back to work after your surgery?
>
> **B:** My doctor thinks maybe in about a month.

### follow closely — 면밀히 주시하다, 열심히 챙겨보다

- *I follow the championship standings very closely every week.*
  - 나는 매주 챔피언십 순위를 아주 면밀히 챙겨본다.

> **A:** Do you follow the election news closely?
>
> **B:** Every day—I check multiple sources each morning.

### put X over Y — Y보다 X를 우선시하다

- *The company put profits over employee safety for years.*
  - 그 회사는 수년간 직원 안전보다 이익을 우선시했다.

> **A:** Did they really put the schedule over the player's health?
>
> **B:** It looks that way, and that's exactly the problem.

### find out — 알아내다, 알게 되다

- *We only found out about his injury after the game ended.*
  - 우리는 경기가 끝난 후에야 그의 부상 사실을 알게 됐다.

> **A:** How did you find out she was leaving the company?
>
> **B:** A coworker told me just this morning.

### be cleared to play — 경기 출전 허가를 받다 (의료 승인을 받다)

- *He was cleared to play despite ongoing concerns about his condition.*
  - 그는 건강 상태에 대한 우려에도 불구하고 출전 허가를 받았다.

> **A:** Is she cleared to play in tomorrow's semifinal?
>
> **B:** The team doctor gave her the all-clear this afternoon.

### wee hours of the morning — 새벽 (이른 아침 시간대)

- *The medical team was still at the clinic during the wee hours of the morning.*
  - 의료팀은 새벽까지도 여전히 병원에 있었다.

> **A:** When did you finally get home last night?
>
> **B:** The wee hours—I didn't leave the clinic until 3 a.m.

### look out for — ~를 돌보다, 챙기다

- *As team captain, it's his job to look out for his players.*
  - 팀 주장으로서 선수들을 챙기는 것이 그의 역할이다.

> **A:** I worry nobody is really looking out for him at that club.
>
> **B:** His agent is supposed to, but I'm not so sure.

### step in — 개입하다, 끼어들다

- *The league needed to step in before the situation got any worse.*
  - 상황이 더 악화되기 전에 리그가 개입할 필요가 있었다.

> **A:** Why didn't the independent doctor step in and stop him?
>
> **B:** That's the question everyone is asking right now.

### get into — (주제를) 깊이 파고들다, 본격적으로 이야기하다

- *Let's get into the medical details of what actually happened.*
  - 실제로 무슨 일이 있었는지 의학적 세부 사항을 파고들어 보자.

> **A:** I want to get into the budget numbers when you're ready.
>
> **B:** Sure, let's get into that right now.

### put in harm's way — ~를 위험에 빠뜨리다, 위험에 노출시키다

- *No team should ever put a player in harm's way just to win a game.*
  - 어떤 팀도 경기 승리를 위해 선수를 위험에 빠뜨려서는 안 된다.

> **A:** Do you think the coaches knowingly put him in harm's way?
>
> **B:** That's a serious accusation, but many people are asking it.

### come up — (화제나 생각이) 떠오르다, 나오다

- *An important issue came up during our team meeting.*
  - 팀 회의 중에 중요한 문제가 떠올랐다.

> **A:** Did anything come up while I was away?
>
> **B:** Yes, we discussed the budget problem.

### at odds with — ~와 상충하다, 충돌하다

- *His personal goals are at odds with the company's direction.*
  - 그의 개인 목표는 회사의 방향과 상충한다.

> **A:** Why won't he agree to the new schedule?
>
> **B:** It's at odds with his other commitments.

### have someone's back — ~의 편이 되다, 지지하다, 보호해주다

- *Don't worry — your teammates always have your back.*
  - 걱정 마 — 팀원들이 항상 네 편이야.

> **A:** I'm nervous about speaking up in the meeting.
>
> **B:** Go ahead — I've got your back.

### be aligned — 의견이 일치하다, 같은 방향을 향하다

- *The two departments are finally aligned on the project goals.*
  - 두 부서가 마침내 프로젝트 목표에 대해 의견이 일치했다.

> **A:** Are the coaches and players on the same page?
>
> **B:** Yes, they're very much aligned right now.

### call for — ~을 요구하다, 촉구하다

- *The union is calling for a full review of safety procedures.*
  - 노조는 안전 절차에 대한 전면 재검토를 요구하고 있다.

> **A:** What is the players' association doing now?
>
> **B:** They're calling for an independent investigation.

### due diligence — 충분한 주의(조사), 실사

- *Before signing, make sure you do your due diligence.*
  - 서명 전에 충분한 조사를 해야 한다.

> **A:** Should we trust this new business partner?
>
> **B:** We need to do our due diligence first.

### look into — ~을 조사하다, 알아보다

- *The league said they are no longer looking into the matter.*
  - 리그 측은 더 이상 그 사안을 조사하지 않겠다고 밝혔다.

> **A:** Is anyone investigating the complaint?
>
> **B:** Yes, management is looking into it now.

### on their own — 스스로, 독자적으로

- *She solved the entire problem on her own.*
  - 그녀는 문제 전체를 혼자서 해결했다.

> **A:** Can the union conduct the investigation independently?
>
> **B:** It's unclear if they can do it on their own.

### break (a story breaks) — (뉴스·사건이) 터지다, 세상에 알려지다

- *The scandal broke just before the championship game.*
  - 그 스캔들은 챔피언십 경기 직전에 터졌다.

> **A:** When did you first hear about the injury story?
>
> **B:** It broke online late last night.

### shot through with — ~로 가득 차 있는, ~이 깊이 배어 있는

- *His childhood memories are shot through with feelings of loneliness.*
  - 그의 어린 시절 기억은 외로움으로 가득 차 있다.

> **A:** Her writing is so dark — what's going on?
>
> **B:** Her whole novel is shot through with grief and loss.

### bring up — 화제로 꺼내다, 언급하다

- *He always brings up his past mistakes at the worst moments.*
  - 그는 항상 최악의 순간에 과거 실수를 꺼낸다.

> **A:** Should we bring up the budget issue at the meeting?
>
> **B:** Better wait — the boss is already in a bad mood.

### four-letter word — 입에 담기 어려운 말, 금기어처럼 취급되는 단어나 개념

- *In this office culture, failure is practically a four-letter word.*
  - 이 직장 문화에서 실패는 사실상 금기어나 다름없다.

> **A:** Why does nobody talk about mental health here?
>
> **B:** Around here, therapy is basically a four-letter word.

### No regrets — 후회 없다 (삶의 태도나 선택에 대해)

- *She quit her stable job to travel the world — no regrets.*
  - 그녀는 안정적인 직장을 그만두고 세계 여행을 떠났다 — 후회 없다.

> **A:** Don't you wish you'd stayed in your hometown?
>
> **B:** Honestly? No regrets — moving here changed my life.

### sorry, not sorry — 미안하지 않다, 솔직히 후회하지 않는다 (반어적 표현)

- *I ate the last slice of cake — sorry, not sorry.*
  - 마지막 케이크 한 조각을 먹어버렸어 — 솔직히 하나도 안 미안해.

> **A:** Did you really tell him exactly what you thought?
>
> **B:** Yep, sorry, not sorry — he needed to hear the truth.

### filled with — ~로 가득 찬

- *After the win, the team was filled with pride.*
  - 승리 후 팀은 자부심으로 가득 찼다.

> **A:** You look different today — what happened?
>
> **B:** I'm just filled with hope after the interview.

### feel sorry — 미안하다; 후회하다

- *I feel sorry for snapping at you this morning.*
  - 오늘 아침에 당신에게 짜증 낸 것이 미안해요.

> **A:** Did you apologize to her after the argument?
>
> **B:** Yes, I really feel sorry for what I said.

### if only for — ~만을 위해서라도

- *You should travel abroad, if only for a week.*
  - 일주일만이라도 해외여행을 해보는 게 좋아.

> **A:** I don't think I should go to the party.
>
> **B:** Go, if only for the chance to meet new people.

### get one life — 인생은 한 번뿐이다

- *You get one life, so chase the things you love.*
  - 인생은 한 번뿐이니, 사랑하는 것을 좇아가.

> **A:** Should I quit my job and start my own business?
>
> **B:** You get one life — take the risk while you can.

### hungry for — ~을 갈망하는, ~에 굶주린

- *After years of setbacks, she was hungry for success.*
  - 수년간의 좌절 끝에 그녀는 성공을 간절히 원했다.

> **A:** Why does he push himself so hard every day?
>
> **B:** He's hungry for recognition from the team.

### a tough deal — 힘든 일, 어려운 상황

- *Balancing work and family is a tough deal.*
  - 일과 가정을 동시에 챙기는 것은 정말 힘든 일이다.

> **A:** How are you handling the new role so far?
>
> **B:** It's a tough deal, but I'm getting used to it.

### done differently — 다르게 했더라면 (과거를 돌아보며 아쉬움을 표현할 때)

- *Looking back, I wish I had done things differently.*
  - 돌이켜보면, 일을 다르게 했더라면 좋았을 텐데.

> **A:** Do you have any regrets about your college years?
>
> **B:** A few — I wish I had done things differently back then.

### take up — (시간·공간을) 차지하다, 잡아먹다

- *This side project takes up all my evenings.*
  - 이 사이드 프로젝트가 내 저녁 시간을 몽땅 잡아먹는다.

> **A:** Why don't you have time for friends anymore?
>
> **B:** Work just takes up every bit of free time I have.

### apart from — ~ 외에, ~을 제외하고

- *Apart from writing, she had little time for herself.*
  - 글쓰기를 제외하면, 그녀는 자신을 위한 시간이 거의 없었다.

> **A:** Was the trip everything you hoped for?
>
> **B:** Yes — apart from the weather, it was perfect.

### the bummer about — ~의 안타까운 점, ~의 아쉬운 부분

- *The bummer about time is that you can't get it back.*
  - 시간의 안타까운 점은 되돌릴 수 없다는 거야.

> **A:** Are you excited to finally retire next spring?
>
> **B:** Mostly yes, but the bummer about it is losing the routine.

### take steps — 조치를 취하다, 행동에 나서다

- *She took steps to improve her work-life balance.*
  - 그녀는 일과 삶의 균형을 개선하기 위해 조치를 취했다.

> **A:** Are you still spending all day on social media?
>
> **B:** No, I've taken steps to cut back on screen time.

### freak out — 극도로 불안해하다, 패닉 상태가 되다

- *Everyone was freaking out when the flight got cancelled.*
  - 비행기가 취소되자 모두가 패닉 상태가 됐다.

> **A:** Did you see the news this morning?
>
> **B:** Yes, half my office was freaking out about it.

### take it personally — 개인적으로 받아들이다, 자기 일처럼 상처받다

- *Don't take it personally — he's just having a rough day.*
  - 개인적으로 받아들이지 마 — 그는 그냥 힘든 하루를 보내고 있는 거야.

> **A:** My boss didn't even say hi to me today.
>
> **B:** Try not to take it personally — she's super stressed.

### wake-up call — 경각심을 일깨워 주는 사건, 정신이 번쩍 드는 계기

- *Getting fired was a real wake-up call for him.*
  - 해고된 것은 그에게 정말 정신이 번쩍 드는 계기가 됐다.

> **A:** He started exercising every day after his checkup.
>
> **B:** Yeah, the doctor's report was a serious wake-up call.

### the worst thing imaginable — 상상할 수 있는 최악의 것, 생각도 하기 싫은 일

- *To her, public speaking is the worst thing imaginable.*
  - 그녀에게 대중 앞에서 말하는 것은 상상할 수 있는 최악의 일이다.

> **A:** Would you ever go camping with no phone signal?
>
> **B:** That sounds like the worst thing imaginable to me.

### try doing — 시험 삼아 ~을 해보다

- *If you're bored, try doing something creative like drawing.*
  - 지루하다면, 그림 그리기 같은 창의적인 걸 해봐.

> **A:** I can't seem to relax after work.
>
> **B:** Try doing a short meditation before bed.

### search for — ~을 검색하다

- *You can search for the full interview on YouTube.*
  - 전체 인터뷰를 유튜브에서 검색할 수 있어.

> **A:** I want to watch that podcast episode again.
>
> **B:** Just search for the title on YouTube.

### one to one — 일대일 (개인 지도)

- *She improved quickly with one to one tutoring sessions.*
  - 그녀는 일대일 과외 수업으로 빠르게 실력이 늘었다.

> **A:** My son really struggles in big classes.
>
> **B:** Maybe one to one instruction would help him more.

### in person — 직접 대면으로

- *We offer classes online or in person, whichever you prefer.*
  - 온라인이나 직접 대면으로 수업을 제공해, 원하는 걸로 골라.

> **A:** Can we do the interview over a video call?
>
> **B:** I'd prefer to meet in person if that's okay.

### struggle with — ~에 어려움을 겪다, ~으로 힘들어하다

- *Many students struggle with self-confidence in English class.*
  - 많은 학생들이 영어 수업에서 자신감 부족으로 힘들어한다.

> **A:** How's your new job going?
>
> **B:** I'm still struggling with all the new software.

### head out — (어딘가를 향해) 나가다, 출발하다

- *Don't forget your ID when you head out to vote.*
  - 투표하러 나갈 때 신분증 챙기는 거 잊지 마.

> **A:** What time are you heading out?
>
> **B:** Around noon — want to grab lunch on the way?

### be called up — 소집되다, 불려 나가다

- *Thirty volunteers were called up to serve as poll chaplains.*
  - 30명의 자원봉사자들이 투표소 채플린으로 소집되었다.

> **A:** How did you end up working the event?
>
> **B:** I was called up at the very last minute.

### face (threats) — 위협에 직면하다

- *Election workers are facing historically high levels of threats.*
  - 선거 관리원들이 역사적으로 높은 수준의 위협에 직면하고 있다.

> **A:** Is the new official staying in the role?
>
> **B:** It's uncertain — she's facing serious threats online.

### spillover onto — ~으로 번지다, ~에 파급되다

- *There are fears the harassment could spill over onto voters.*
  - 괴롭힘이 유권자들에게까지 번질 수 있다는 우려가 있다.

> **A:** Is the protest staying contained?
>
> **B:** We're worried it might spill over onto nearby streets.

### make a big difference — 큰 차이를 만들다, 크게 영향을 미치다

- *In swing states, a small turnout shift can make a big difference.*
  - 경합 주에서는 소규모 투표율 변화도 큰 차이를 만들 수 있다.

> **A:** Should I really study vocabulary every single day?
>
> **B:** Yes, even ten minutes a day can make a big difference.

### get ready for — ~을 준비하다

- *The faith groups have been getting ready for this year's midterms.*
  - 종교 단체들이 이번 중간선거를 준비해 오고 있다.

> **A:** Are you nervous about the big presentation?
>
> **B:** Not really — I've been getting ready for it all week.

### unpack — (짐이나 물건을) 꺼내다, 풀다

- *Reverend Elaine was unpacking yellow caps and purple vests.*
  - 엘레인 목사는 노란 모자와 보라색 조끼를 꺼내고 있었다.

> **A:** Did you just arrive at the venue?
>
> **B:** Yes, I'm still unpacking all our supplies.

### come up against — (어려움·반대 등에) 맞닥뜨리다, 직면하다

- *The new policy came up against fierce resistance from local officials.*
  - 새 정책은 지역 공무원들의 거센 반발에 맞닥뜨렸다.

> **A:** Did the project get approved without any problems?
>
> **B:** No, we came up against a lot of unexpected opposition.

### be in this together — 함께 같은 상황에 처해 있다, 우리는 한편이다

- *No matter how hard it gets, remember we're all in this together.*
  - 아무리 힘들어도, 우리 모두가 함께라는 걸 기억하세요.

> **A:** I feel like I'm carrying the whole team by myself.
>
> **B:** You're not — we're in this together, so let's split the work.

### guard one's turf — 자신의 영역[이권]을 지키다

- *Each department guards its turf instead of cooperating with others.*
  - 각 부서는 서로 협력하는 대신 자기 영역만 지키려 한다.

> **A:** Why won't the legal team share those documents with us?
>
> **B:** They're always guarding their turf — just escalate to the manager.

### be suspicious of — ~을 의심하다, ~을 수상하게 여기다

- *We shouldn't have to be suspicious of our neighbors at the polling station.*
  - 투표소에서 이웃을 의심할 필요는 없어야 한다.

> **A:** He keeps asking everyone the same questions about the vote.
>
> **B:** I know — he seems suspicious of everyone who shows up.

### spread across — ~전체에 퍼지다, 확산되다

- *Her personal information was spread across the internet without her knowledge.*
  - 그녀의 개인 정보가 본인도 모르는 사이 인터넷 전체에 퍼졌다.

> **A:** How did the rumor reach every office so quickly?
>
> **B:** Someone posted it once and it spread across the whole company chat.

### take out a restraining order against — ~에 대한 접근금지 명령을 받다[신청하다]

- *The election clerk had to take out a restraining order against a threatening resident.*
  - 그 선거 담당자는 위협적인 주민에 대한 접근금지 명령을 신청해야 했다.

> **A:** Is she still being harassed by that man from the meeting?
>
> **B:** Yes, so she took out a restraining order against him yesterday.

**Q1.** The journalist decided to ____ the corruption allegations before publishing her story.

- look into
- find out
- get into

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>look into</strong> — look into = ~을 조사하다. 'find out'은 뒤에 의문절 없이 단독으로 쓰기 어렵고, 'get into'는 대화 중 주제를 깊이 파고든다는 뜻이라 취재 맥락과 맞지 않습니다.</p></details>

**Q2.** The band was still rehearsing at the ____ — the show was just ten hours away.

- wee hours of the morning
- right around the same time
- at this point

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>wee hours of the morning</strong> — wee hours of the morning = 새벽. 'right around the same time'은 두 사건의 동시성, 'at this point'는 현재 시점을 나타내므로 리허설 시간대를 묘사하는 문장과 맞지 않습니다.</p></details>

**Q3.** Losing his job at 40 was a real ____ — he finally started taking his health and savings seriously.

- wake-up call

- four-letter word

**Q4.** When the editor cut her article in half, she tried not to ____, reminding herself it was just business.

**Q7.** We've been ____ for months now, putting in 80-hour weeks to get this product launched.

- in the trenches
