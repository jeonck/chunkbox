## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### by the time — ~할 때쯤에는, ~할 때까지는

- *By the time we arrived, the show had already started.*
  - 우리가 도착했을 때쯤에는 공연이 이미 시작해 있었다.

> **A:** Did you catch the announcement about the schedule change?
>
> **B:** No, by the time I checked my email, it was too late.

### used to be — 예전에는 ~이었다 (과거의 상태나 습관을 나타냄)

- *This neighborhood used to be very quiet before all the new buildings went up.*
  - 이 동네는 새 건물들이 들어서기 전에는 아주 조용했었다.

> **A:** Wow, this street looks so different now.
>
> **B:** I know — it used to be just empty lots around here.

### take the day off — 하루 휴가를 내다

- *I'm going to take the day off on Friday to rest.*
  - 나는 쉬려고 금요일에 하루 휴가를 낼 것이다.

> **A:** You look exhausted — are you okay?
>
> **B:** I think I'll take the day off tomorrow and recharge.

### have options — 선택의 여지가 있다, 선택지가 있다

- *You have options — you can take the train or the bus.*
  - 선택지가 있어요. 기차를 타거나 버스를 탈 수 있어요.

> **A:** I don't know which job offer to accept.
>
> **B:** You have options, so take your time deciding.

### make it hard to — ～하기 어렵게 만들다

- *Heavy snow can make it hard to drive on the highway.*
  - 폭설은 고속도로 운전을 어렵게 만들 수 있어요.

> **A:** Why are you always late on Monday mornings?
>
> **B:** The roadwork makes it hard to get through downtown.

### not all the time — 항상 그런 건 아니다

- *The highway gets congested, but not all the time.*
  - 고속도로가 막히긴 하지만, 항상 그런 건 아니에요.

> **A:** Is Seoul traffic always this terrible?
>
> **B:** Not all the time — it's worst during holidays.

### at the same time — 동시에, 같은 시간에

- *During Lunar New Year, everyone travels at the same time.*
  - 설날에는 모두가 동시에 이동해요.

> **A:** Why is the highway completely blocked right now?
>
> **B:** Millions of people are heading home at the same time.

### have an experience — 경험한 적이 있다, 경험이 있다

- *I have an experience of being stuck in traffic for ten hours.*
  - 교통 정체로 10시간 동안 갇혀 있어본 경험이 있어요.

> **A:** Have you ever driven during Chuseok holidays?
>
> **B:** Yes, I have an experience of sitting in traffic all day.

### just like that — 그냥 그렇게, 별것 아닌 듯이, 순식간에

- *The five-hour trip became fifteen hours — just like that.*
  - 5시간짜리 여행이 그냥 그렇게 15시간이 되어 버렸어요.

> **A:** How did you miss the last train home?
>
> **B:** I dozed off for a moment, and just like that, it was midnight.

### at the time — 그 당시에, 그때는

- *At the time, we didn't know the roads would be that bad.*
  - 그 당시에, 우리는 도로가 그렇게 막힐 줄 몰랐어요.

> **A:** Why didn't you leave earlier to avoid the traffic?
>
> **B:** At the time, we thought it would only take five hours.

### it took — (시간이) 걸렸다

- *It took us fifteen hours to drive from Busan to Seoul.*
  - 부산에서 서울까지 운전하는 데 15시간이 걸렸어요.

> **A:** How was your Lunar New Year trip back home?
>
> **B:** It took nearly twelve hours — the highway was a parking lot.

### go back to — ~로 돌아가다

- *After the long weekend, I have to go back to school.*
  - 긴 연휴가 끝나면 학교로 돌아가야 해.

> **A:** Are you staying in Seoul for the break?
>
> **B:** No, I have to go back to Busan tonight.

### take a long time — 오래 걸리다

- *It took a long time to drive home from the city.*
  - 도시에서 집까지 운전해 오는 데 오래 걸렸어.

> **A:** How was your trip back home?
>
> **B:** It took a long time — almost twelve hours.

### used to — 예전에 ~하곤 했다

- *We used to stay in the city every week for work.*
  - 우리는 일 때문에 매주 시내에 머물곤 했어.

> **A:** Do you still commute to Chennai every day?
>
> **B:** No, but we used to stay there for weeks at a time.

### go by car — 차로 가다

- *If we go by car, the journey will take one full day.*
  - 차로 가면 꼬박 하루가 걸릴 거야.

> **A:** How are you getting to your hometown?
>
> **B:** We'll go by car — the train tickets are sold out.

### go by bus — 버스로 가다

- *During the holidays, it is very hard to go by bus.*
  - 명절 시즌에는 버스로 가기가 정말 힘들어.

> **A:** Should we fly home for the festival?
>
> **B:** Let's go by bus — it's much cheaper than flying.

### book tickets — 표를 예매하다

- *People book tickets almost a year in advance for the holidays.*
  - 사람들은 명절 표를 거의 1년 전에 미리 예매해.

> **A:** I want to go home for Chuseok this year.
>
> **B:** You should book tickets now — they sell out really fast.

### on the way — 가는 도중에

- *We stopped at a rest area on the way to the hometown.*
  - 고향으로 가는 도중에 휴게소에 들렀어.

> **A:** Did you drive straight through without stopping?
>
> **B:** No, we grabbed food on the way — it was a long drive.

### a year back — 1년 전에

- *A year back, she finally booked her tickets two months early.*
  - 1년 전에 그녀는 마침내 두 달 일찍 표를 예매했어.

> **A:** When did you first start planning these long trips?
>
> **B:** About a year back — I got stuck without tickets once before.

### go abroad — 해외에 가다

- *Many Koreans want to go abroad during the holiday season.*
  - 많은 한국인들이 연휴 시즌에 해외에 가고 싶어 한다.

> **A:** Are you planning to go abroad this summer?
>
> **B:** Yes, I finally saved enough for a trip to Europe.

### personal leave — 개인 사유 휴가

- *She took personal leave to spend time with her family.*
  - 그녀는 가족과 시간을 보내기 위해 개인 휴가를 냈다.

> **A:** Why isn't Tom at work this week?
>
> **B:** He used his personal leave for a family trip.

### holiday season — 연휴 시즌, 명절 기간

- *Airports feel like subway stations during the holiday season.*
  - 연휴 시즌에는 공항이 지하철역처럼 붐빈다.

> **A:** The airport was absolutely packed yesterday!
>
> **B:** Of course — it's the holiday season, everyone travels.

### go back — 돌아가다

- *Are you going back to Korea for the new year?*
  - 새해를 맞아 한국으로 돌아가시나요?

> **A:** So are you going back home for the holidays?
>
> **B:** No, I have to work — maybe next year.

### move here — 이곳으로 이사 오다

- *My whole family moved here from Korea last year.*
  - 우리 가족 전체가 작년에 한국에서 이곳으로 이사 왔다.

> **A:** How do you like living in the U.S.?
>
> **B:** I just moved here, so I'm still adjusting.

### I see — 아, 그렇군요 (이해했을 때 쓰는 표현)

- *I see — so your family came first, and you followed later.*
  - 아, 그렇군요 — 가족이 먼저 오고 나중에 합류하셨군요.

> **A:** My family moved here last year, but I came this year.
>
> **B:** I see, so you came at different times.

### how long have you been here — 여기 온 지 얼마나 됐어요?

- *How long have you been here in the United States?*
  - 미국에 오신 지 얼마나 됐나요?

> **A:** How long have you been here? Your English sounds great!
>
> **B:** Only six months — I study every day.

### get a job — 취직하다, 일자리를 구하다

- *I'm trying to get a job before my visa expires.*
  - 비자가 만료되기 전에 취직하려고 노력 중이다.

> **A:** What's your plan now that you've moved here?
>
> **B:** I'm trying to get a job in IT as soon as possible.

### no chance to meet — 만날 기회가 없다

- *We've both been so busy there's no chance to meet.*
  - 둘 다 너무 바빠서 만날 기회가 없다.

> **A:** Have you caught up with your friends here yet?
>
> **B:** Not yet — our schedules clash and there's no chance to meet.

### up to now — 지금까지, 현재까지

- *Up to now, no one has offered me a position.*
  - 지금까지 아무도 저에게 일자리를 제안하지 않았어요.

> **A:** Have you found a job up to now?
>
> **B:** Not yet — I'm still waiting to hear back.

### would like to — ~하고 싶다 (정중한 표현)

- *I would like to find a stable job in the U.S.*
  - 저는 미국에서 안정적인 직장을 찾고 싶어요.

> **A:** What are your plans for next year?
>
> **B:** I would like to get promoted if possible.

### try more — 더 시도해보다, 계속 도전하다

- *Don't give up — just try more and you'll succeed.*
  - 포기하지 마세요 — 더 시도하면 성공할 거예요.

> **A:** I've been rejected by ten companies already.
>
> **B:** You can do it — just try more and stay confident.

### apply for — (직장·학교 등에) 지원하다

- *She applied for five different jobs last week.*
  - 그녀는 지난주에 다섯 군데 회사에 지원했다.

> **A:** Have you applied for any positions at Google yet?
>
> **B:** Yes, I applied for a software engineer role yesterday.

### just taking time — 그냥 시간이 걸리는 것이다 (인내가 필요한 상황을 달랠 때)

- *Don't worry — finding the right job is just taking time.*
  - 걱정하지 마 — 맞는 직장을 찾는 건 그냥 시간이 걸리는 거야.

> **A:** I've sent out 50 resumes and still no offer.
>
> **B:** It's just taking time — keep going, you'll get there.

### add value to — ~에 가치를 더하다, 도움이 되다

- *My international experience can add real value to your team.*
  - 제 해외 경험이 여러분 팀에 실질적인 가치를 더할 수 있습니다.

> **A:** Why should we hire you over other candidates?
>
> **B:** I add value to every team through my diverse IT background.

### come across — 우연히 마주치다, 우연히 발견하다

- *I came across a great article about AI while browsing online.*
  - 온라인을 둘러보다가 AI에 관한 좋은 기사를 우연히 발견했어요.

> **A:** How did you find out about that job opening?
>
> **B:** I came across it on LinkedIn while looking for something else.

### going on — 진행 중인, 일어나고 있는

- *There are a lot of layoffs going on in the tech industry right now.*
  - 지금 테크 업계에서 정리해고가 많이 진행되고 있어요.

> **A:** Why haven't you heard back from that company yet?
>
> **B:** I think there's a hiring freeze going on over there.

### in the face of — ~에 직면하여, ~에도 불구하고

- *She stayed motivated in the face of repeated rejections.*
  - 그녀는 거듭되는 거절에도 불구하고 의욕을 잃지 않았어요.

> **A:** How did you keep applying after five rejections?
>
> **B:** You have to stay positive in the face of setbacks.

### get an interview — 면접 기회를 얻다

- *It took months of networking before I finally got an interview.*
  - 몇 달간의 네트워킹 끝에 드디어 면접 기회를 얻었어요.

> **A:** Did you ever hear back from that company you applied to?
>
> **B:** Yes! I finally got an interview scheduled for next week.

### resign my job — 직장을 그만두다, 사직하다

- *She had to resign her job when the family moved abroad.*
  - 가족이 해외로 이사할 때 그녀는 직장을 그만둬야 했어요.

> **A:** Why did you leave that company so suddenly?
>
> **B:** I had to resign my job when we moved countries.

### move as a family — 가족 전체가 함께 이사하다

- *Once he got the visa, they moved as a family to the U.S.*
  - 그가 비자를 받자마자 그들은 가족 전체가 미국으로 이사했어요.

> **A:** Did your kids stay back in India with your parents?
>
> **B:** No, we all moved as a family together.

### for a while — 한동안, 잠시 동안

- *After graduating, we both worked in India for a while.*
  - 졸업 후, 우리 둘 다 한동안 인도에서 일했어요.

> **A:** Did you come to the U.S. straight after university?
>
> **B:** No, we stayed in India for a while first.

### work right away — 바로/즉시 일을 시작하다

- *With this visa, spouses can work right away after arriving.*
  - 이 비자로는 배우자가 도착하자마자 바로 일을 시작할 수 있어요.

> **A:** Do I have to wait months before I can start working?
>
> **B:** Not with this visa — you can work right away.

### take a break — 잠시 쉬다, 활동을 중단하다

- *They don't have to take a break between jobs with this visa.*
  - 이 비자로는 직장 사이에 쉬지 않아도 돼요.

> **A:** Did you have to stop working when your visa changed?
>
> **B:** No, I didn't have to take a break at all.

### come in — (때가 되어) 오다, 도달하다

- *We are hoping the approval will come in within five years.*
  - 우리는 5년 안에 승인이 나오기를 바라고 있어요.

> **A:** When do you think the decision will come in?
>
> **B:** Hopefully before the end of the year.

### move on — 앞으로 나아가다, 진행되다

- *During COVID, the paperwork moved on much faster than usual.*
  - 코로나 기간 동안 서류 처리가 평소보다 훨씬 빠르게 진행됐어요.

> **A:** The application has been stuck for months.
>
> **B:** I know — I wish things would move on already.

### work on — ~에 노력을 기울이다, 처리하다

- *The office said they didn't work on any new cases last month.*
  - 사무소 측에서는 지난달에 새 사건을 처리하지 않았다고 했어요.

> **A:** Is anyone working on our file right now?
>
> **B:** They said they'd work on it after the holiday.

### wait until — ~할 때까지 기다리다

- *We just have to wait until they process all the documents.*
  - 서류가 모두 처리될 때까지 그냥 기다려야 해요.

> **A:** Can we do anything to speed this up?
>
> **B:** Not really — we just have to wait until they call us.

### be open to — ~에 열려 있다, 기꺼이 받아들이다

- *We are open to trying new teaching methods in class.*
  - 우리는 수업에서 새로운 교수법을 시도하는 것에 열려 있다.

> **A:** Would you ever consider moving abroad for work?
>
> **B:** Yes, I'm open to it if the opportunity is right.

### work out — 잘 되다, 잘 풀리다

- *I hope things work out for you at your new job.*
  - 새 직장에서 일이 잘 풀리길 바랍니다.

> **A:** I'm nervous about the interview tomorrow.
>
> **B:** Don't worry — I'm sure it'll work out fine.

### stick to — ~을 고수하다, ~에 충실하다

- *You should stick to your original plan and not give up.*
  - 원래 계획을 고수하고 포기하지 마세요.

> **A:** I keep changing my career goals every few months.
>
> **B:** Try to stick to one path and give it a real chance.

### be open-minded — 열린 마음을 갖다, 편견 없이 생각하다

- *You need to be open-minded when living in a new country.*
  - 새로운 나라에서 살 때는 열린 마음을 가져야 한다.

> **A:** The food here is so different — I'm not sure I like it.
>
> **B:** Just be open-minded; you might be surprised.

### kick out — 쫓아내다, 강제로 퇴출시키다

- *The university can kick students out for serious violations.*
  - 대학교는 심각한 규정 위반 시 학생을 퇴학시킬 수 있다.

> **A:** I heard someone got kicked out of the program last semester.
>
> **B:** Yes, they broke the code of conduct too many times.

### step-by-step — 단계적으로, 한 단계씩

- *He explained the visa process step-by-step so everyone could follow.*
  - 그는 모든 사람이 따라갈 수 있도록 비자 과정을 단계적으로 설명했다.

> **A:** I don't understand how to apply for this permit.
>
> **B:** Don't worry, I'll walk you through it step-by-step.

### let them be — 그냥 내버려 두다, 간섭하지 않다

- *If the company is okay, they will just let them be in the process.*
  - 회사가 괜찮다면, 그들을 그냥 프로세스 안에 내버려 둘 것이다.

> **A:** Should we ask him to change his filing status?
>
> **B:** No, just let him be — he knows what he's doing.

### move to — (다른 도시·나라로) 이사하다, 이동하다

- *A lot of people move to Atlanta because there are many job opportunities there.*
  - 많은 사람들이 일자리가 많아서 애틀랜타로 이사한다.

> **A:** Have you thought about where you want to settle down?
>
> **B:** I'm thinking of moving to Austin — the tech scene is huge there.

### at least — 적어도, 최소한

- *You can at least apply even if you're not sure you'll get it.*
  - 합격할지 모르더라도 적어도 지원은 해볼 수 있어요.

> **A:** I don't think I'm qualified enough for that job.
>
> **B:** You should at least send in your resume and try.

### far from — ~에서 멀리; ~과는 거리가 멀다

- *The office is far from downtown, so the commute is really long.*
  - 사무실이 시내에서 멀어서 출퇴근이 정말 길어요.

> **A:** Is the new campus close to public transport?
>
> **B:** No, it's actually far from the nearest subway station.

### living cost — 생활비

- *The living cost in San Francisco is extremely high for new graduates.*
  - 샌프란시스코의 생활비는 신입 졸업생들에게 매우 높습니다.

> **A:** Are you thinking about moving to California for work?
>
> **B:** The salary is great, but the living cost scares me.

### try getting — ~을 얻으려고 시도해보다

- *You can try getting an interview by networking with people there.*
  - 그곳 사람들과 네트워킹해서 면접 기회를 얻으려고 시도해볼 수 있어요.

> **A:** I really want to work at that tech company in Austin.
>
> **B:** You should try getting a referral from someone who works there.

### a couple of years — 몇 년, 2년쯤

- *She worked at that startup for a couple of years before getting promoted.*
  - 그녀는 승진하기 전에 그 스타트업에서 몇 년 동안 일했어요.

> **A:** When did you first move to Toronto?
>
> **B:** A couple of years ago — time really flies here.

### apply to every company — 모든 회사에 지원하다

- *When the market is slow, some people apply to every company they can find.*
  - 시장이 침체될 때 어떤 사람들은 찾을 수 있는 모든 회사에 지원해요.

  - 그녀는 수술 후 생긴 의료 부채를 갚기 위해 두 가지 일을 해야 했습니다.

> **A:** Why is she so stressed about money lately?
>
> **B:** She's been dealing with a lot of medical debt since her accident.

### talk to us about — ~에 대해 우리에게 이야기해 주다 (인터뷰나 대화에서 상대방에게 설명을 요청할 때 쓰는 표현)

- *Can you talk to us about what you've been seeing with your clients recently?*
  - 최근 고객들과 관련해 보고 계신 것들에 대해 이야기해 주실 수 있나요?

> **A:** Rebecca, talk to us about the fraud cases you've encountered.
>
> **B:** Sure — clients are being signed up for plans they never agreed to.

### as far as I can tell — 내가 알기로는, 내가 보기에는

- *As far as I can tell, no one informed the clients of the change.*
  - 내가 알기로는, 아무도 고객들에게 변경 사항을 알리지 않았어.

> **A:** Is the policy still valid?
>
> **B:** As far as I can tell, it expired last month.

### sign up for — (프로그램·서비스 등에) 가입하다, 등록하다

- *He was signed up for a health plan without his knowledge.*
  - 그는 자신도 모르는 사이에 건강 보험에 가입되어 있었어.

> **A:** Did you sign up for the new rewards program?
>
> **B:** Not yet — I'm still reading the terms first.

### dovetail into — 딱 맞아 들어가다, 자연스럽게 연결되다

- *This new evidence dovetails into what investigators already suspected.*
  - 이 새로운 증거는 수사관들이 이미 의심하던 것과 딱 맞아 들어가.

> **A:** How does the witness's story fit with ours?
>
> **B:** It dovetails into our timeline perfectly.

### be slammed with — (예상치 못한 청구서·문제 등을) 갑자기 맞닥뜨리다

- *Families were suddenly slammed with huge health care fees they never expected.*
  - 가족들은 전혀 예상하지 못한 거액의 의료비 청구서를 갑자기 받았어.

> **A:** Why is she so upset this morning?
>
> **B:** She got slammed with a surprise hospital bill.

### be aware of — ~을 알다, 인식하다 (부정형: ~을 알지 못하다)

- *Most enrollees weren't even aware of the plan they had been put on.*
  - 대부분의 가입자들은 자신들이 어떤 플랜에 등록되었는지조차 몰랐어.

> **A:** Did you know your account was being charged monthly?
>
> **B:** No, I wasn't aware of that at all.

### lie about — ~에 대해 거짓말하다

- *The brokers lied about clients' income to qualify them for bigger subsidies.*
  - 그 브로커들은 더 큰 보조금을 받게 하려고 고객들의 소득에 대해 거짓말했어.

> **A:** Why was the agent's license revoked?
>
> **B:** He lied about his clients' income on the forms.

### on top of all of this — 이 모든 것에 더해서, 게다가

- *On top of all of this, we still have to submit the report by Friday.*
  - 이 모든 것에 더해서, 우리는 아직 금요일까지 보고서를 제출해야 해.

> **A:** I missed the bus and then spilled coffee on my shirt.
>
> **B:** And on top of all of this, your meeting starts in ten minutes!

### on the phone — 전화 통화 중인

- *I was on the phone for two hours sorting out my insurance issue.*
  - 보험 문제를 해결하느라 두 시간 동안 전화 통화를 했어.

> **A:** Why haven't you answered my messages all morning?
>
> **B:** Sorry, I've been on the phone with a client the whole time.

### put a strain on — ~에 부담을 주다

- *The staff cuts put a strain on everyone still working at the clinic.*
  - 직원 감축은 여전히 그 클리닉에서 일하는 모든 사람에게 부담을 주었다.

> **A:** Has losing two team members affected your workload?
>
> **B:** Definitely — it's really put a strain on the whole department.

### get rid of — ~을 없애다, 제거하다

- *Getting rid of navigators will leave many patients without guidance.*
  - 네비게이터를 없애면 많은 환자들이 안내 없이 남겨질 것이다.

> **A:** Should we keep these old forms in the filing cabinet?
>
> **B:** No, let's get rid of them — we've gone fully digital now.

### fill in — (공백·빈자리를) 채우다

- *Volunteers stepped up to fill in the gaps left by the budget cuts.*
  - 자원봉사자들이 예산 삭감으로 생긴 공백을 채우기 위해 나섰다.

> **A:** Who will handle Jake's clients while he's out sick?
>
> **B:** I can fill in for now — I know most of his accounts.

### stay in touch with — ~와 계속 연락하다

- *We'd love to stay in touch with you as the situation develops.*
  - 상황이 진전됨에 따라 당신과 계속 연락하고 싶습니다.

> **A:** It was so great meeting you at the conference last week.
>
> **B:** Absolutely — let's stay in touch with each other on LinkedIn.

### follow up on — ~에 대해 후속 조치를 취하다

- *The reporter promised to follow up on the story next week.*
  - 그 기자는 다음 주에 그 이야기에 대해 후속 조치를 취하겠다고 약속했다.

> **A:** Did you ever hear back about your insurance complaint?
>
> **B:** Not yet, but I plan to follow up on it tomorrow morning.

### thrown under the bus — 배신당하다, 희생양이 되다

- *She felt thrown under the bus when her boss blamed her for the mistake.*
  - 상사가 실수의 책임을 그녀에게 전가했을 때 그녀는 배신당한 기분이었다.

> **A:** Why did Jake suddenly resign from the team?
>
> **B:** He got thrown under the bus for the failed project.

### a lot more coming up — (방송에서) 더 많은 내용이 곧 이어집니다

- *Stay tuned — there's a lot more coming up after the break.*
  - 채널 고정하세요 — 광고 후에 더 많은 내용이 이어집니다.

> **A:** Is the interview the last segment today?
>
> **B:** No, there's a lot more coming up — don't change the channel!

### here and now — 지금 이 순간, 현재

- *Stop worrying about tomorrow and focus on the here and now.*
  - 내일 걱정은 그만하고 지금 이 순간에 집중하세요.

> **A:** I keep thinking about what might go wrong next year.
>
> **B:** Try to live in the here and now — it really helps.

### carved out — 분리되다, 새로 나뉘어지다 (선거구 등이)

- *The new district was carved out of the old one to shift political power.*
  - 정치적 힘을 재편하기 위해 기존 선거구에서 새 선거구가 분리되었다.

> **A:** Why does our city suddenly have three different districts?
>
> **B:** This area got carved out during the last redistricting process.

### break down — 분석하다, 쉽게 나눠서 설명하다

- *Let me break down the key issues from tonight's debate for you.*
  - 오늘 밤 토론의 핵심 쟁점들을 쉽게 분석해 드리겠습니다.

> **A:** The new healthcare policy is so complicated.
>
> **B:** I'll break it down into three simple points.

### keep an eye on — 주시하다, 지켜보다

- *NPR will keep an eye on the national results as they come in.*
  - NPR은 전국 선거 결과가 집계되는 동안 계속 주시할 것이다.

> **A:** Can you watch my bag while I grab coffee?
>
> **B:** Sure, I'll keep an eye on it for you.

### kick off — 시작하다, 개시하다

- *The live election night coverage kicks off at 6:30 on the radio.*
  - 라디오 선거 밤 생방송은 6시 30분에 시작된다.

> **A:** What time does the election special broadcast start?
>
> **B:** It kicks off at half past six — don't be late!

### drive voter turnout — 투표율을 높이다, 투표 참여를 이끌어내다

- *Both parties are sending volunteers door-to-door to drive voter turnout.*
  - 양당 모두 투표율을 높이기 위해 자원봉사자들을 가가호호 파견하고 있다.

> **A:** Why are campaigns calling people so much this week?
>
> **B:** They're doing everything they can to drive voter turnout.

### ratchet up — 점점 높이다, 단계적으로 강화하다

- *The coach ratcheted up the training intensity before the finals.*
  - 코치는 결승전을 앞두고 훈련 강도를 점점 높였다.

> **A:** Why is everyone so stressed about the new policy?
>
> **B:** The manager keeps ratcheting up the deadlines every week.

### all in one place — 한 곳에 모두, 한데 모아서

- *You can manage your photos and videos all in one place.*
  - 사진과 동영상을 한 곳에서 모두 관리할 수 있어요.

> **A:** I keep switching between three different apps for work.
>
> **B:** Try this tool — it keeps everything all in one place.

**Q1.** ____ I arrived at the station, the last train had already left.

- By the time
- At the time
- On the way
- A year back

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>By the time</strong> — "By the time"은 '~할 때쯤에는 이미'라는 의미로 과거완료와 함께 앞선 완료 결과를 나타낸다. "At the time"은 당시 상황 설명, "On the way"는 이동 중, "A year back"은 1년 전을 뜻해 문맥에 맞지 않는다.</p></details>

**Q2.** She called her boss and said she needed to ____ because she had a high fever.

- take the day off
- take a break
- take a long time
- take a moment

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>take the day off</strong> — "take the day off"는 하루 결근·휴가를 뜻한다. "take a break"는 잠깐 쉬는 것, "take a long time"은 시간이 오래 걸린다는 표현, "take a moment"는 잠시 멈추는 것이라 하루 결근 상황과는 다르다.</p></details>

**Q3.** The latest polls showed the race was a ____, with both candidates tied at 47%.

- dead heat
- close the gap
- flip
- lean Republican

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>dead heat</strong> — "dead heat"는 명사로 '초접전·막상막하'를 뜻한다. "close the gap"은 '격차를 줄이다'는 동사구, "flip"은 '의석을 뒤집다', "lean Republican"은 '공화당 쪽으로 기울다'는 표현이라 빈칸에 맞지 않는다.</p></details>

**Q4.** Investigators found that many customers had been ____ a health insurance policy they never requested.

- enrolled in
- signed up for
- qualified for
- switched to

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>enrolled in</strong> — "enrolled in"은 수동형으로 '(본인 모르게) 가입·등록되다'를 뜻해 피해 상황에 가장 자연스럽다. "signed up for"·"switched to"도 수동 구문은 되지만 '스스로 선택'하는 뉘앙스가 강하고, "qualified for"는 자격이 된다는 의미라 문맥에 맞지 않는다.</p></details>

**Q5.** The campaign workers decided to ____ to speak with voters directly before Election Day.

- go door to door
- make an appearance
- rally for
- get exposure

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>go door to door</strong> — "go door to door"는 집집마다 직접 방문해 유권자를 만나는 활동이다. "make an appearance"는 한 장소에 등장하는 것, "rally for"는 지지 유세, "get exposure"는 미디어 노출을 얻는 것으로 의미가 다르다.</p></details>

**Q6.** I know the job search is tough, but I hope things ____ for you soon.

- work out
- come in
- move on
- go up

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>work out</strong> — "work out"은 '일이 잘 풀리다'를 뜻해 응원의 말로 가장 자연스럽다. "come in"은 도달하다, "move on"은 앞으로 나아가다, "go up"은 오르다는 뜻으로 이 맥락에는 어울리지 않는다.</p></details>

**Q7.** She realized her impressive résumé was a case of ____ — she was simply too experienced for the roles she targeted.

- over-qualifying
- mask the level
- lower your level

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>over-qualifying</strong> — "over-qualifying"은 스펙이 지나치게 높아 직무에 맞지 않는 상황을 가리키는 명사로 쓰였다. 나머지는 동사구 또는 복합명사로 'a case of' 뒤에 오는 명사 자리에 어울리지 않는다.</p></details>
