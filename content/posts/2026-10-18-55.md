## Session Overview

이번 세션에서는 일상 대화에서 빈번히 쓰이는 55개의 핵심 영어 청크와 구동사를 다뤘습니다. 감정·의도·계획·과거 습관·현재 적응 상태를 표현하는 구문들을 폭넓게 익혔으며, 특히 'I'm used to ~'와 'I'm getting used to ~'처럼 혼동하기 쉬운 표현 사이의 미묘한 차이도 살펴봤습니다. 이 청크들을 체화하면 원어민의 자연스러운 말투에 한층 가까워질 수 있습니다.

## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### I'm really into ~ — ~에 푹 빠져 있어요

- *I'm really into learning Korean these days.*
  - 요즘 한국어 배우는 것에 푹 빠져 있어.

> **A:** You've been watching dramas all day. What's going on?
>
> **B:** I'm really into this series — I can't stop watching.

### Do you know where ~ is? — ~가 어디 있는지 아시나요?

- *Do you know where the nearest subway station is?*
  - 가장 가까운 지하철역이 어디 있는지 아시나요?

> **A:** Excuse me, do you know where the exit is?
>
> **B:** Sure, it's just around the corner on your left.

### I was just wondering if you could ~ — ~해주실 수 있는지 궁금했어요

- *I was just wondering if you could help me with this.*
  - 이것 좀 도와주실 수 있는지 궁금했어요.

> **A:** I was just wondering if you could turn the music down.
>
> **B:** Of course, sorry about that — I'll lower it now.

### It's been [시간] since ~ — ~한 지 [시간]이 되었어요

- *It's been two years since we last met.*
  - 우리가 마지막으로 만난 지 2년이 되었어.

> **A:** It's been so long since we grabbed coffee together!
>
> **B:** I know, it's been almost a year! We need to catch up.

### I feel like ~ — ~하고 싶은 기분이에요

- *I feel like ordering pizza tonight.*
  - 오늘 밤 피자를 시켜 먹고 싶은 기분이야.

> **A:** I feel like staying in and watching a movie tonight.
>
> **B:** That sounds perfect — I'll make some popcorn.

### I'll stick with ~ — ~로 할게요 / ~을 고수할게요

- *I'll stick with my usual coffee order, thanks.*
  - 평소에 마시던 커피로 할게요, 감사합니다.

> **A:** Do you want to try the new menu item?
>
> **B:** No thanks, I'll stick with the classic burger.

### I'm about to ~ — 막 ~하려고 해요

- *I'm about to leave, so I'll call you later.*
  - 막 출발하려던 참이라서 나중에 전화할게.

> **A:** Can you talk right now?
>
> **B:** Sorry, I'm about to walk into a meeting — text me!

### I've got to ~ — ~해야 해요

- *I've got to finish this report before midnight.*
  - 자정 전에 이 보고서를 끝내야 해.

> **A:** Come on, one more game!
>
> **B:** I've got to get up early tomorrow, so not tonight.

### I'm supposed to ~ — ~하기로 되어 있어요 / ~해야 해요

- *I'm supposed to meet her at three, but I'm running late.*
  - 3시에 그녀를 만나기로 되어 있는데 늦을 것 같아.

> **A:** Are you eating that cake? You're supposed to be dieting!
>
> **B:** I know, I know — just one small slice won't hurt.

### Could I get ~? — ~를 좀 받을 수 있을까요?

- *Could I get a refund for this item?*
  - 이 물건 환불받을 수 있을까요?

> **A:** Could I get an extra blanket for tonight?
>
> **B:** Of course, I'll bring one right away.

### Could you ~? — ~해주실 수 있나요?

- *Could you repeat that one more time, please?*
  - 한 번 더 말씀해 주실 수 있나요?

> **A:** Could you take a photo of us?
>
> **B:** Sure, stand right there and smile!

### I'm trying to find ~ — ~를 찾으려고 하는 중이에요

- *I'm trying to find a good coffee shop nearby.*
  - 근처에 좋은 카페를 찾으려고 하는 중이에요.

> **A:** I'm trying to find the nearest subway station.
>
> **B:** It's just two blocks down on your left.

### How do I ~? — 어떻게 ~하나요?

- *How do I reset my password on this app?*
  - 이 앱에서 비밀번호를 어떻게 재설정하나요?

> **A:** How do I get to the city center from here?
>
> **B:** Take bus number 5 — it goes directly there.

### I was just about to ~ — 막 ~하려던 참이었어요

- *I was just about to call you when you texted me.*
  - 네가 문자를 보냈을 때 막 전화하려던 참이었어.

> **A:** Perfect timing — I was just about to order lunch.
>
> **B:** Great, let's order together then!

### I'm all set to ~ — ~할 준비가 다 되었어요

- *I'm all set to start the presentation whenever you're ready.*
  - 준비되시면 언제든지 발표 시작할 준비 다 됐어요.

> **A:** I'm all set to go — did you grab your bag?
>
> **B:** Yes, let's head out!

### I'm thinking about ~ — ~할까 생각 중이에요

- *I'm thinking about moving to a new neighborhood.*
  - 새로운 동네로 이사할까 생각 중이에요.

> **A:** I'm thinking about taking an online English course.
>
> **B:** That's a great idea — which platform are you looking at?

### I'm planning to ~ — ~할 계획이에요

- *I'm planning to visit my family next weekend.*
  - 다음 주말에 가족을 방문할 계획이에요.

> **A:** I'm planning to study abroad next year.
>
> **B:** Wow, which country are you thinking of?

### I'm able to ~ — ~할 수 있어요

- *I'm able to finish the report by this afternoon.*
  - 오늘 오후까지 보고서를 끝낼 수 있어요.

> **A:** We need someone to cover the morning shift.
>
> **B:** I'm able to come in early if that helps.

### I don't think I can ~ — ~할 수 있을지 모르겠어요 / 못할 것 같아요

- *I don't think I can make it to the party tonight.*
  - 오늘 밤 파티에 갈 수 있을지 모르겠어요.

> **A:** Can you finish this by tomorrow morning?
>
> **B:** I don't think I can — I have too much on my plate.

### I don't think I can make it — 오늘 가기 어려울 것 같아

- *I don't think I can make it to the party tonight.*
  - 오늘 밤 파티에 가기 어려울 것 같아요.

> **A:** Are you coming to the team dinner on Friday?
>
> **B:** I don't think I can make it — I have a prior commitment.

### Why not try ~? — ~해보는 게 어때요?

- *Why not try a different route to avoid the traffic?*
  - 교통 체증을 피하려면 다른 길로 가보는 게 어때요?

> **A:** I've been stuck on this math problem for an hour.
>
> **B:** Why not try drawing a diagram to visualize it?

### It's not about ~ — ~에 관한 것이 아니에요

- *It's not about the price — I just don't like the style.*
  - 가격 문제가 아니에요 — 그냥 스타일이 마음에 안 들어요.

> **A:** Are you upset because I spent too much?
>
> **B:** No, it's not about the money — I felt left out.

### Don't forget to ~ — ~하는 것을 잊지 마세요

- *Don't forget to charge your phone before the long trip.*
  - 긴 여행 전에 핸드폰 충전하는 것 잊지 마세요.

> **A:** I'm heading out to the airport now.
>
> **B:** Don't forget to print your boarding pass!

### How come ~? — 어째서 ~인가요? / 왜 ~죠?

- *How come you didn't tell me about the schedule change?*
  - 왜 일정 변경에 대해 나한테 말 안 했어요?

> **A:** The meeting got moved to Monday.
>
> **B:** How come nobody sent me an email about it?

### What do you think about ~? — ~에 대해 어떻게 생각하세요?

- *What do you think about switching to a four-day work week?*
  - 주 4일제 근무로 전환하는 것에 대해 어떻게 생각하세요?

> **A:** What do you think about the new logo design?
>
> **B:** Honestly, I think the font looks a bit too modern.

### Then what about ~? — 그럼 ~은 어때요?

- *Then what about meeting on Thursday instead?*
  - 그럼 대신 목요일에 만나는 건 어때요?

> **A:** I can't do Tuesday or Wednesday this week.
>
> **B:** Then what about Friday morning?

### Have you tried ~? — ~해본 적 있나요? / ~해봤어요?

- *Have you tried restarting the app to fix the glitch?*
  - 오류를 고치려고 앱을 재시작해봤어요?

> **A:** My laptop keeps freezing every few minutes.
>
> **B:** Have you tried updating the operating system?

### I'm working on ~ — ~을 작업/노력 중이에요

- *I'm working on finishing the report by end of day.*
  - 오늘 안으로 보고서를 마무리하려고 작업 중이에요.

> **A:** Is the new feature ready to launch yet?
>
> **B:** Not yet — I'm working on fixing the last bug.

### I'm trying to avoid ~ — ~를 피하려고 하는 중이에요

- *I'm trying to avoid checking my phone too often at work.*
  - 직장에서 핸드폰을 너무 자주 확인하는 것을 피하려고 노력 중이에요.

> **A:** Want to grab some coffee before the meeting?
>
> **B:** Thanks, but I'm trying to avoid caffeine after noon.

### I'm getting ~ — ~해지고 있어요

- *I'm getting hungry — should we grab a bite?*
  - 배가 고파지고 있어 — 뭐 좀 먹을까?

> **A:** You look a little pale. Are you feeling okay?
>
> **B:** I'm getting dizzy — I need some fresh air.

### It turns out ~ — 알고 보니 ~이네요

- *It turns out the meeting was canceled this morning.*
  - 알고 보니 회의가 오늘 아침에 취소됐더라고.

> **A:** Did you ever find out why she left so early?
>
> **B:** It turns out she had a flight to catch.

### I didn't mean to ~ — ~하려던 의도는 아니었어요

- *I didn't mean to interrupt — please keep talking.*
  - 방해하려던 건 아니었어 — 계속 얘기해.

> **A:** You stepped on my foot!
>
> **B:** Sorry, I didn't mean to — it was an accident.

### I ended up ~ — 결국 ~하게 되었어요

- *I ended up staying at the party until midnight.*
  - 결국 자정까지 파티에 있게 되었어.

> **A:** Did you take the bus home last night?
>
> **B:** No, I ended up calling a cab instead.

### I used to ~ — 예전에 ~하곤 했어요

- *I used to play the piano when I was a kid.*
  - 예전에 어릴 때 피아노를 치곤 했어.

> **A:** You seem so comfortable on stage.
>
> **B:** Well, I used to act in school plays.

### I'm used to ~ — ~에 익숙해요

- *I'm used to waking up early for work.*
  - 출근 때문에 일찍 일어나는 것에 익숙해.

> **A:** Doesn't the noise from the street bother you?
>
> **B:** Not really — I'm used to living near the train tracks.

### I'm getting used to ~ — ~에 익숙해지는 중이에요

- *I'm getting used to the new office layout.*
  - 새 사무실 구조에 익숙해지는 중이야.

> **A:** How's the new work schedule treating you?
>
> **B:** It's tough, but I'm getting used to it.

### I've got ~ — ~가 있어요

- *I've got a doctor's appointment this afternoon.*
  - 오늘 오후에 병원 예약이 있어.

> **A:** Can you help me move this weekend?
>
> **B:** Sorry, I've got a family thing on Saturday.

### I got to ~ — ~해야 해요

- *I got to finish this report before five o'clock.*
  - 5시 전에 이 보고서를 끝내야 해.

> **A:** Want to grab coffee after work?
>
> **B:** I can't — I got to pick up my kids.

### I'm okay — 괜찮아요

- *Don't worry about me — I'm okay now.*
  - 나 걱정하지 마 — 이제 괜찮아.

> **A:** You fell pretty hard. Are you hurt?
>
> **B:** I'm okay, just a little surprised.

### I'm okay thanks — 괜찮아요, 고마워요

- *I'm okay thanks, I don't need any help.*
  - 괜찮아요 고마워요, 도움은 필요 없어요.

> **A:** Do you need a hand with those bags?
>
> **B:** I'm okay thanks, they're not that heavy.

### Have you been to ~? — ~에 가본 적 있나요?

- *Have you been to New York before?*
  - 뉴욕에 가본 적 있어요?

> **A:** Have you been to that new Italian place downtown?
>
> **B:** Not yet, but I've heard it's amazing.

### I've been ~ — ~하고 있는 중이에요

- *I've been waiting for your call all morning.*
  - 아침 내내 당신의 전화를 기다리고 있었어요.

> **A:** Why do you look so tired?
>
> **B:** I've been studying for my exam all night.

### I'm considering ~ — ~를 고려하고 있어요

- *I'm considering moving to a new apartment.*
  - 새 아파트로 이사하는 걸 고려하고 있어요.

> **A:** Are you going to accept that job offer?
>
> **B:** I'm considering it, but I need more time.

### I would ~ — ~하겠어요 / ~하는 편이겠어요

- *I would take the subway if I were you.*
  - 나라면 지하철을 타겠어요.

> **A:** Should I call him or send a text?
>
> **B:** I would just send a quick message.

### Do you have ~? — ~가 있나요?

- *Do you have a table for two available tonight?*
  - 오늘 밤 두 명 자리 있나요?

> **A:** Do you have any vegetarian options on the menu?
>
> **B:** Yes, we have a few great ones.

### Where can I ~? — 어디서 ~할 수 있나요?

- *Where can I find the nearest pharmacy?*
  - 가장 가까운 약국이 어디 있나요?

> **A:** Where can I buy a SIM card around here?
>
> **B:** There's a phone shop right around the corner.

### How long does it take ~? — ~하는 데 얼마나 걸리나요?

- *How long does it take to fly from Seoul to Tokyo?*
  - 서울에서 도쿄까지 비행하는 데 얼마나 걸리나요?

> **A:** How long does it take to get to the airport by bus?
>
> **B:** About an hour, depending on traffic.

### I think ~ — ~인 것 같아요

- *I think I left my wallet on the table.*
  - 지갑을 테이블에 두고 온 것 같아요.

> **A:** Why are you sneezing so much today?
>
> **B:** I think I'm coming down with a cold.

### You should have ~ — ~했어야 했어요

- *You should have told me about the meeting earlier.*
  - 그 회의에 대해 더 일찍 말해줬어야 했어요.

> **A:** The concert was completely sold out!
>
> **B:** You should have bought the tickets last week.

### I'm obsessed with ~ — ~에 푹 빠져 있어요

- *I'm obsessed with this new café downtown.*
  - 저는 시내에 새로 생긴 카페에 푹 빠져 있어요.

> **A:** You've been there every single day this week.
>
> **B:** I know — I'm obsessed with their matcha latte.

### I've been busy ~ — ~하느라 바빴어요

- *I've been busy planning a surprise party for her.*
  - 그녀를 위해 깜짝 파티를 계획하느라 바빴어요.

> **A:** Hey, I haven't heard from you in ages!
>
> **B:** Sorry, I've been busy dealing with deadlines at work.

### I'm not sure if I should ~ — ~해야 할지 잘 모르겠어요

- *I'm not sure if I should apply for that position.*
  - 그 자리에 지원해야 할지 잘 모르겠어요.

> **A:** Are you going to tell him what happened?
>
> **B:** I'm not sure if I should bring it up now.

### What if ~? — ~하면 어쩌죠? / ~라면 어떡할 거야?

- *What if we take the wrong bus and get lost?*
  - 잘못된 버스를 타서 길을 잃으면 어쩌죠?

> **A:** I'm thinking about quitting my job next month.
>
> **B:** What if you can't find another one quickly?

### How long did it take ~? — ~하는 데 얼마나 걸렸나요?

- *How long did it take you to finish reading that novel?*
  - 그 소설 다 읽는 데 얼마나 걸렸나요?

> **A:** Wow, your English has improved so much!
>
> **B:** How long did it take to reach that level?

### That's what happens when ~ — ~할 때 일어나는 일이 바로 그거죠

- *That's what happens when you skip warming up before a run.*
  - 달리기 전에 준비운동을 빠뜨리면 그렇게 되는 거예요.

> **A:** I stayed up all night and can't focus at all today.
>
> **B:** That's what happens when you ignore your sleep schedule.
