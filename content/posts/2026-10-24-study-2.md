## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### play out — (상황이) 전개되다, 펼쳐지다

- *The effects of the new policy are already playing out in rural areas.*
  - 새 정책의 영향이 농촌 지역에서 이미 전개되고 있다.

> **A:** How do you think the election drama will play out?
>
> **B:** Hard to say — things are changing fast on the ground.

### on the ground — 현장에서, 실제 상황에서

- *The journalist reported what she saw on the ground in the affected region.*
  - 그 기자는 피해 지역 현장에서 목격한 것을 보도했다.

> **A:** What's the situation on the ground over there?
>
> **B:** It's much more chaotic than the news reports suggest.

### take a step back — 한 발 물러서서 더 넓게 바라보다

- *Before deciding, it's worth taking a step back and looking at the big picture.*
  - 결정하기 전에, 한 발 물러서서 큰 그림을 보는 것이 좋다.

> **A:** We keep arguing about the details and getting nowhere.
>
> **B:** Let's take a step back and rethink our whole approach.

### pay attention to — ~에 주의를 기울이다, ~을 주목하다

- *Voters have been paying attention to redistricting debates in neighboring states.*
  - 유권자들은 이웃 주들의 선거구 재조정 논쟁에 주목해 왔다.

> **A:** Do local voters even care about redistricting?
>
> **B:** Absolutely — they've been paying close attention to every move.

### intertwined with — ~와 얽혀 있다, 밀접하게 연결되어 있다

- *Race and politics have long been intertwined with housing policy in the South.*
  - 남부에서 인종과 정치는 오랫동안 주거 정책과 얽혀 있었다.

> **A:** Can we really separate economic issues from racial ones here?
>
> **B:** Not really — they're deeply intertwined with each other in this region.

### hold onto — (자리·것을) 유지하다, 놓지 않다

- *He's fighting hard to hold onto his seat in Congress.*
  - 그는 의회 의석을 유지하기 위해 열심히 싸우고 있다.

> **A:** Do you think she'll hold onto her position after the scandal?
>
> **B:** It's hard to say — the pressure on her is enormous.

### carve up — (지역·이익 등을) 잘게 나누다, 분할하다

- *Politicians tried to carve up the district to gain more votes.*
  - 정치인들은 더 많은 표를 얻으려고 선거구를 분할하려 했다.

> **A:** Why are they redrawing the map again?
>
> **B:** They want to carve up the region for a political advantage.

### set up — (상황·구조를) 만들다, 설정하다

- *They're setting up a scenario that guarantees them a majority.*
  - 그들은 다수를 보장하는 상황을 만들고 있다.

> **A:** Why did they change the rules before the vote?
>
> **B:** They were setting up a scenario where they couldn't lose.

### weigh in on — ~에 대해 의견을 말하다, 논의에 참여하다

- *Citizens were invited to weigh in on whether the maps should change.*
  - 시민들은 지도 변경 여부에 대해 의견을 말하도록 초대되었다.

> **A:** Did anyone from the public weigh in on the new policy?
>
> **B:** Yes, dozens of residents showed up to share their views.

### get out — (사람들을) 밖으로 나오게 하다, 동원하다

- *Organizers worked all week to get voters out to the hearing.*
  - 조직가들은 유권자들을 청문회에 나오게 하려고 일주일 내내 일했다.

> **A:** How did they manage to fill the hall so quickly?
>
> **B:** Activists spent weeks trying to get people out.

### rely on — ~에 의지하다, ~을 믿다

- *You have to rely on the power of your vote to make change.*
  - 변화를 이끌려면 투표의 힘을 믿어야 한다.

> **A:** What's the best way citizens can fight back?
>
> **B:** They need to rely on organized community action.

### move ahead — 계획대로 진행하다, 추진하다

- *How will people react if lawmakers move ahead with the redistricting?*
  - 의원들이 선거구 재획정을 추진하면 사람들은 어떻게 반응할까?

> **A:** Are they still planning to move ahead with the new law?
>
> **B:** Yes, the vote is scheduled for next Monday.

### hand over — (통제권·차례를) 넘기다, 양도하다

- *The chairperson handed the floor over to the community members.*
  - 의장은 발언권을 지역 주민들에게 넘겼다.

> **A:** When do the officials stop talking at these hearings?
>
> **B:** They hand it over to the public pretty early on.

### speak out — 공개적으로 목소리를 내다, 의사를 표명하다

- *Many residents came to speak out against the proposed changes.*
  - 많은 주민들이 제안된 변경 사항에 반대 목소리를 내기 위해 왔다.

> **A:** Why are so many people at the town hall tonight?
>
> **B:** They want to speak out before the final decision is made.

### come out — (행사·집회 등에) 나오다, 참석하다

- *Hundreds of supporters came out to make their voices heard.*
  - 수백 명의 지지자들이 목소리를 내기 위해 나왔다.

> **A:** Did many people come out for the redistricting hearing?
>
> **B:** The room was packed — they even needed overflow seating.

### stay on — (주제·경로에서) 벗어나지 않다, 계속 집중하다

- *The moderator reminded speakers to stay on the main topic.*
  - 진행자는 발언자들에게 주제에서 벗어나지 말라고 상기시켰다.

> **A:** He kept going off on unrelated tangents during his speech.
>
> **B:** I know — someone really should have told him to stay on topic.

### in the near future — 가까운 미래에, 머지않아

- *She plans to open her own bakery in the near future.*
  - 그녀는 머지않아 자신의 베이커리를 열 계획이다.

> **A:** Are you going to apply for that promotion?
>
> **B:** Yes, I'm planning to do it in the near future.

### considering a run for — (선거) 출마를 고려하다

- *The senator is considering a run for president next year.*
  - 그 상원의원은 내년 대통령 출마를 고려하고 있다.

> **A:** I heard Marcus is considering a run for mayor.
>
> **B:** Really? He'd have a strong chance of winning.

### take up — (문제나 주제를) 다루기 시작하다, 착수하다

- *The committee agreed to take up the housing issue next month.*
  - 위원회는 다음 달 주거 문제를 다루기로 합의했다.

> **A:** When will the council take up the road repair proposal?
>
> **B:** They're scheduled to take it up at Friday's meeting.

### in favor of — ~에 찬성하여, ~을 지지하여

- *Most students were in favor of extending the lunch break.*
  - 대부분의 학생들은 점심시간 연장에 찬성했다.

> **A:** Are you in favor of the new school policy?
>
> **B:** Honestly, I'm in favor of most of it, but not all.

### all over — 전역에, 곳곳에

- *He has traveled all over Europe, visiting over twenty countries.*
  - 그는 유럽 전역을 여행하며 20개국 이상을 방문했다.

> **A:** How do you know so much about regional food?
>
> **B:** I've eaten all over the country for the last ten years.

### at the bottom of — ~의 최하위에, ~에서 가장 낮은 순위에

- *Our city keeps landing at the bottom of the quality-of-life rankings.*
  - 우리 도시는 삶의 질 순위에서 계속 최하위권에 머물고 있다.

> **A:** Why are they pushing for new investment in that district?
>
> **B:** It's been at the bottom of every economic index for years.

### far too long — 너무나 오랫동안, 지나치게 오랜 시간 동안

- *We've been waiting for far too long without any updates.*
  - 우리는 아무런 소식도 없이 너무나 오랫동안 기다려왔다.

> **A:** Why are people finally speaking up about this issue?
>
> **B:** Because it's been ignored for far too long.

### voted into office — 투표로 선출되다, 당선되다

- *She was voted into office on a platform of affordable healthcare.*
  - 그녀는 의료비 절감 공약을 내세워 투표로 당선되었다.

> **A:** How did such an unknown candidate get voted into office?
>
> **B:** She knocked on every door in the district for six months.

### take into account — 고려하다, 감안하다

- *The judge must take into account the defendant's age and background.*
  - 판사는 피고의 나이와 배경을 고려해야 한다.

> **A:** Did they take the weather into account when planning the event?
>
> **B:** No, and that's exactly why everything went wrong.

### color blind — 인종을 초월한, 피부색에 관계없이 평등하게 대하는

- *He believes the justice system should be color blind in every ruling.*
  - 그는 사법 체계가 모든 판결에서 인종을 초월해야 한다고 믿는다.

> **A:** How should a fair hiring process work?
>
> **B:** It should be color blind and based purely on qualifications.

### go through — 힘든 일을 겪다, 감내하다

- *She couldn't go through another year of that stressful job.*
  - 그녀는 그 스트레스 가득한 직장에서 또 한 해를 버틸 수 없었다.

> **A:** How did he survive such a difficult childhood?
>
> **B:** I honestly don't know how he went through all that.

### power grab — 권력 장악 시도, 권력 찬탈

- *Critics called the new legislation a blatant power grab by the ruling party.*
  - 비평가들은 그 새 법안을 집권당의 노골적인 권력 장악 시도라고 불렀다.

> **A:** Why is everyone so upset about the new election rules?
>
> **B:** Most people see it as a partisan power grab, not real reform.

### What's the rush? — 왜 서두르는 거야? 급할 게 뭐야?

- *What's the rush? We still have two hours before the deadline.*
  - 왜 서두르는 거야? 마감까지 아직 두 시간이나 있잖아.

> **A:** Hurry up! We need to leave right now!
>
> **B:** What's the rush? The movie doesn't start for thirty minutes.

### stand out — 눈에 띄다, 두드러지다

- *Her confident speech made her stand out among all the candidates.*
  - 그녀의 자신감 있는 연설이 모든 후보들 중에서 그녀를 돋보이게 했다.

> **A:** What stood out to you most about last night's forum?
>
> **B:** The number of young voters there really stood out.

### show up — 나타나다, 참석하다

- *Hundreds of young activists showed up to support the cause.*
  - 수백 명의 젊은 활동가들이 그 운동을 지지하기 위해 참석했다.

> **A:** Did many students come to the community meeting?
>
> **B:** Way more than expected — nearly two hundred showed up.

### riled up — 흥분한, 화가 잔뜩 난

- *The unfair maps got voters angry and riled up across the state.*
  - 불공정한 선거구 지도에 주 전역의 유권자들이 분노하고 흥분했다.

> **A:** Why is everyone shouting at the town hall tonight?
>
> **B:** The redistricting news has everyone riled up.

### get engaged in — ~에 참여하다, ~에 관여하다

- *Young people need to get engaged in local politics now.*
  - 젊은 사람들은 지금 지역 정치에 참여해야 한다.

> **A:** Do you think college students care about voting rights?
>
> **B:** Definitely — they're getting engaged in the fight more than ever.

### keep going — 계속 나아가다, 멈추지 않다

- *Our elders urge us to keep going no matter how hard it gets.*
  - 어른들은 아무리 힘들어도 계속 나아가라고 우리를 격려한다.

> **A:** I'm exhausted — I feel like giving up on this campaign.
>
> **B:** Think of those who came before us — we have to keep going.

### go down — (역사에) 기록되다, 남다

- *This protest will go down as a turning point in our community.*
  - 이 시위는 우리 지역사회의 전환점으로 역사에 기록될 것이다.

> **A:** Do you think people will remember what happened here?
>
> **B:** Absolutely — this will go down in history.

### let alone — ~은 말할 것도 없고, ~커녕

- *My grandparents had no voting rights, let alone political representation.*
  - 우리 조부모님은 정치적 대표는 말할 것도 없고 투표권조차 없었다.

> **A:** Could sharecroppers even own land back then?
>
> **B:** No — they couldn't own land, let alone vote.

### in particular — 특히, 특별히

- *What stood out in particular was the turnout of young voters.*
  - 특히 두드러진 것은 젊은 유권자들의 참석률이었다.

> **A:** Was there anything special about tonight's rally?
>
> **B:** The college students in particular were incredibly passionate.

### get in front of — ~앞에 서다, ~에게 직접 발언하다

- *She got in front of the crowd to share her family's story.*
  - 그녀는 군중 앞에 서서 자신의 가족 이야기를 전했다.

> **A:** How did you convince the committee to hear your concerns?
>
> **B:** I got in front of them and spoke from personal experience.

### go forward with — ~을 추진하다, ~을 진행하다

- *They decided to go forward with the new plan despite opposition.*
  - 그들은 반대에도 불구하고 새 계획을 추진하기로 결정했다.

> **A:** Are we still going forward with the original proposal?
>
> **B:** Yes, the board gave us the green light this morning.

### not go anywhere — 물러서지 않다, 사라지지 않다 (굳건히 남다)

- *This movement is not going anywhere — we will keep fighting.*
  - 이 운동은 사라지지 않을 것이다 — 우리는 계속 싸워 나갈 것이다.

> **A:** Do you think the protests will just die out soon?
>
> **B:** No way — we're not going anywhere until things change.

### back down — 물러서다, 입장을 철회하다

- *She refused to back down even under intense pressure.*
  - 그녀는 강한 압박에도 물러서기를 거부했다.

> **A:** They're threatening to cut your funding if you disagree.
>
> **B:** I won't back down — I know what's right.

### give up — 포기하다

- *No matter how difficult it gets, we will not give up.*
  - 아무리 힘들어도, 우리는 절대 포기하지 않을 것이다.

> **A:** This is taking so long — should we just give up?
>
> **B:** Absolutely not. We're almost there.

### pull aside — ~를 따로 불러내다, 옆으로 잠깐 데려가다

- *Can I pull you aside for a couple of quick questions?*
  - 잠깐 따로 얘기할 수 있을까요? 몇 가지 여쭤볼 게 있어서요.

> **A:** Hey, can I pull you aside for a second?
>
> **B:** Sure, what's on your mind?

### end up — 결국 ~하게 되다

- *She ended up talking to him for over an hour.*
  - 그녀는 결국 그와 한 시간이 넘도록 이야기하게 되었다.

> **A:** How did the interview go?
>
> **B:** I ended up talking way longer than I planned.

### come to life — 생기를 띠다, 생생하게 살아나다

- *The stories came to life when he described his own memories.*
  - 그가 자신의 기억을 이야기하자 그 이야기들이 생생하게 살아났다.

> **A:** History felt so dry in the textbook.
>
> **B:** But it really came to life when he started sharing his story.

### take care — 조심하다, 몸 건강히 지내다

- *In times like these, you really need to take care of yourself.*
  - 이런 시기에는 스스로를 잘 돌보는 것이 정말 중요하다.

> **A:** Things have been so stressful lately.
>
> **B:** I know — please take care, okay?

### see parallels between — ~사이에서 유사점을 발견하다

- *He saw parallels between the civil rights era and today's protests.*
  - 그는 민권 운동 시대와 오늘날의 시위 사이에서 유사점을 발견했다.

> **A:** Do you think history is repeating itself here?
>
> **B:** He sees real parallels between then and what's happening now.

### turn back — (이미 이루어진 발전이나 변화를) 되돌리다, 원상태로 돌리다

- *They fought hard to keep those civil rights gains from being turned back.*
  - 그들은 그 시민권 성과들이 되돌려지지 않도록 열심히 싸웠다.

> **A:** Do you think the new law will turn back progress on equality?
>
> **B:** I hope not — we've come too far to go backwards.

### turn out (to vote) — (특히 투표하러) 나오다, 참여하다

- *Young voters need to turn out more reliably in every election.*
  - 젊은 유권자들은 매 선거마다 더 꾸준히 투표하러 나와야 한다.

> **A:** Are you worried not enough people will turn out to vote?
>
> **B:** Yes, low turnout is the biggest threat to this campaign.

### ballot box — 투표함; (비유적으로) 투표, 선거

- *Real change only happens when people show up at the ballot box.*
  - 진짜 변화는 사람들이 투표함 앞에 나타날 때만 일어난다.

> **A:** Protests are great, but what comes next?
>
> **B:** The ballot box — that's where we make it count.

### thank you for having me — 초대해 주셔서 감사합니다 (게스트가 호스트에게 하는 인사)

- *After the interview, she said, 'Thank you for having me.'*
  - 인터뷰가 끝난 후 그녀는 '초대해 주셔서 감사합니다'라고 말했다.

> **A:** It was great having you on the show today!
>
> **B:** Thank you for having me — I really enjoyed it.

### get to the heart of — 핵심에 다가가다, 본질을 파악하다

- *Good journalism helps us get to the heart of complex issues.*
  - 좋은 저널리즘은 복잡한 문제의 핵심을 파악하도록 도와준다.

> **A:** This article is so long — what's the main point?
>
> **B:** Keep reading; it gets to the heart of the problem on page two.

### go deeper into — 더 깊이 파고들다, 더 자세히 살펴보다

- *You can go deeper into these stories on our website.*
  - 우리 웹사이트에서 이 이야기들을 더 깊이 살펴볼 수 있습니다.

> **A:** I want to understand the history behind this conflict better.
>
> **B:** Let's go deeper into it — I'll share some background articles.

### all things considered — 모든 것을 고려해 보면

- *All things considered, the project turned out to be a success.*
  - 모든 것을 고려해 보면, 그 프로젝트는 결국 성공이었다.

> **A:** Was the camping trip fun despite all the rain?
>
> **B:** All things considered, we actually had a great time.

### limit access to — ~에 대한 접근을 제한하다

- *The agency limited public access to the immigration data.*
  - 그 기관은 이민 데이터에 대한 대중의 접근을 제한했다.

> **A:** Why can't I find that report on their website anymore?
>
> **B:** The government has limited access to those records.
