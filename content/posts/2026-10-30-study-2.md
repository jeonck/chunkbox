## Session Overview

이번 세션에서는 미국 알래스카 상원의원 선거를 다룬 탐사 보도 인터뷰에서 추출한 약 120개의 영어 청크와 구동사를 학습했습니다. 선거 출마·투표 용지 조작 의혹·소송 제기 등 정치·법률 맥락의 핵심 표현부터 인터뷰 진행, 일상 대화, 감정 표현에 이르는 다양한 실용 표현을 익혔습니다. 특히 순위 선택 투표(ranked choice voting)와 관련된 선거 전략 표현들을 통해 미국 정치 영어의 실제 쓰임을 생생하게 경험할 수 있었습니다.

## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### cry foul — 불공정하다고 항의하다, 부정이라고 소리치다

- *The losing team cried foul after the referee's controversial call.*
  - 패팀은 심판의 논란적인 판정 후 부정이라고 항의했다.

> **A:** Did anyone complain about the new grading policy?
>
> **B:** Oh, half the class cried foul immediately.

### enter the race — 선거전 또는 경쟁에 뛰어들다

- *A third candidate unexpectedly entered the race last week.*
  - 지난주에 세 번째 후보가 예상치 못하게 선거전에 뛰어들었다.

> **A:** I heard someone new entered the race for mayor.
>
> **B:** Yes, it really shook up the whole campaign.

### on the ballot — 투표용지에 올라 있는, 후보로 등재된

- *There are five candidates on the ballot for the district election.*
  - 지역구 선거 투표용지에는 다섯 명의 후보가 올라 있다.

> **A:** How many names are on the ballot this year?
>
> **B:** Four, which is more than usual for a Senate race.

### run for reelection — 재선에 출마하다

- *The senator announced he would run for reelection next November.*
  - 그 상원의원은 다음 11월에 재선에 출마하겠다고 발표했다.

> **A:** Is the current governor running for reelection?
>
> **B:** Yes, but she's facing a very tough challenger.

### on one's own accord — 자발적으로, 스스로 원해서

- *She left the company on her own accord, without any pressure.*
  - 그녀는 어떤 압박도 없이 자발적으로 회사를 떠났다.

> **A:** Did someone force him to apologize publicly?
>
> **B:** No, he came forward on his own accord.

### have no connection to — ~와 아무 관련이 없다

- *The campaign manager stated she had no connection to the leaked documents.*
  - 선거 캠프 매니저는 유출된 문서와 아무 관련이 없다고 밝혔다.

> **A:** Do you think the party was behind that rumor?
>
> **B:** They insist they have no connection to it at all.

### have nothing to do with — ~와 전혀 관련이 없다

- *I have nothing to do with that decision.*
  - 나는 그 결정과 전혀 관련이 없어.

> **A:** Did you have anything to do with that leaked document?
>
> **B:** No, I had nothing to do with it at all.

### conspiracy theory — 음모론

- *Don't believe that — it's just a wild conspiracy theory.*
  - 그걸 믿지 마 — 그건 그냥 황당한 음모론이야.

> **A:** Do you believe the election was secretly stolen from him?
>
> **B:** That sounds like just another conspiracy theory to me.

### maintain the stance — 입장을 유지하다

- *She maintained the stance despite heavy criticism from her party.*
  - 그녀는 당 내의 강한 비판에도 불구하고 입장을 유지했다.

> **A:** Did the senator change his position on immigration?
>
> **B:** No, he maintained the stance throughout the whole debate.

### remove from the ballot — 투표 용지에서 제외시키다

- *They tried to remove her from the ballot in three states.*
  - 그들은 세 개 주에서 그녀를 투표 용지에서 제외시키려 했다.

> **A:** Why isn't the opposition candidate on the ballot?
>
> **B:** Courts tried to remove her from the ballot last month.

### file a lawsuit — 소송을 제기하다

- *The company filed a lawsuit against its former employee last year.*
  - 그 회사는 작년에 전직 직원을 상대로 소송을 제기했다.

> **A:** What are they going to do about the broken contract?
>
> **B:** They're planning to file a lawsuit against the other party.

### turn out — (결과적으로) ~로 드러나다, ~임이 밝혀지다

- *It turned out the rumors about him were completely false.*
  - 그에 대한 소문들이 완전히 거짓임이 밝혀졌다.

> **A:** Was the new candidate as popular as everyone expected?
>
> **B:** Not really — it turned out voters didn't trust him.

### date back to — ~로 거슬러 올라가다, ~부터 시작되다

- *Their rivalry dates back to a land dispute in the 1980s.*
  - 그들의 경쟁 관계는 1980년대의 토지 분쟁으로 거슬러 올라간다.

> **A:** When did this feud between the two families begin?
>
> **B:** It dates back to a bitter argument over property rights.

### stack the ballot in one's favor — 자신에게 유리하게 투표를 조작하다

- *They were accused of stacking the ballot in their favor.*
  - 그들은 자신들에게 유리하게 투표를 조작한 혐의를 받았다.

> **A:** Why do you think the election results look so suspicious?
>
> **B:** Someone clearly stacked the ballot in their favor this time.

### be involved in — ~에 관여하다, ~에 개입하다

- *She has been involved in community health projects for years.*
  - 그녀는 수년간 지역사회 건강 프로젝트에 관여해 왔다.

> **A:** Did you have any knowledge of the secret deal?
>
> **B:** Yes, I was directly involved in the early planning stages.

### major scoop — 중대한 특종

- *The reporter landed a major scoop about the political scandal.*
  - 그 기자는 정치 스캔들에 관한 중대한 특종을 잡았다.

> **A:** Why is everyone talking about that journalist's article today?
>
> **B:** She just broke a major scoop on government corruption.

### come to do something — 어쩌다 ~하게 되다, ~하게 이르다

- *How did you come to specialize in election law?*
  - 어쩌다 선거법 전문가가 되셨나요?

> **A:** How did you come to work on this investigation?
>
> **B:** A tip from a source got me started on the whole story.

### uncover — (숨겨진 것을) 밝혀내다, 폭로하다

- *Investigators uncovered evidence of widespread fraud in the election.*
  - 수사관들은 선거에서 광범위한 사기의 증거를 밝혀냈다.

> **A:** How did journalists find out about the corruption scheme?
>
> **B:** An insider helped them uncover the whole illegal operation.

### secret scheme — 비밀 계획, 음모

- *The investigation revealed a secret scheme to manipulate the vote.*
  - 수사를 통해 투표를 조작하려는 비밀 계획이 드러났다.

> **A:** Why were those officials meeting in private so often?
>
> **B:** Turns out they were running a secret scheme together.

### tangled tale — 복잡하게 얽힌 이야기

- *It's a pretty tangled tale involving money, power, and betrayal.*
  - 돈과 권력, 배신이 얽힌 꽤 복잡한 이야기다.

> **A:** Can you explain what happened between those two candidates?
>
> **B:** It's a really tangled tale — it'll take a while.

### go back to — ~로 거슬러 올라가다, ~에서 비롯되다

- *The story goes back to a scandal that broke in 2018.*
  - 이 이야기는 2018년에 터진 스캔들로 거슬러 올라간다.

> **A:** When did the rivalry between those two senators start?
>
> **B:** It goes back to a heated budget debate years ago.

### jump into the race — 선거전에 뛰어들다

- *Three more candidates jumped into the race last month.*
  - 지난달 세 명의 후보가 더 선거전에 뛰어들었다.

> **A:** I heard she's actually running for Senate — is that true?
>
> **B:** Yes, she jumped into the race just last week.

### pose a threat to — ~에 위협이 되다

- *The new challenger poses a real threat to the incumbent's reelection.*
  - 새로운 도전자가 현직 의원의 재선에 실질적인 위협이 된다.

> **A:** Should the senator be worried about his new opponent?
>
> **B:** Definitely — she poses a serious threat to his seat.

### across the aisle — 당파를 초월하여, 상대 정당과 협력하여

- *The bill passed because several senators reached across the aisle.*
  - 몇몇 의원들이 당파를 초월해 협력했기 때문에 그 법안이 통과되었다.

> **A:** How did such a controversial bill manage to pass?
>
> **B:** They found support from members across the aisle.

### serve as — ~로 재직하다, ~로 활동하다

- *He has served as the state's senator since 2015.*
  - 그는 2015년부터 그 주의 상원의원으로 재직해 왔다.

> **A:** What is her political background before running for governor?
>
> **B:** She served as a state representative for eight years.

### upset race — 이변이 일어난 선거, 파란을 일으킨 경쟁

- *She won an upset race against a well-funded incumbent in 2022.*
  - 그녀는 2022년에 자금이 풍부한 현직 의원을 상대로 이변을 일으키며 당선됐다.

> **A:** Did anyone expect that independent candidate to actually win?
>
> **B:** No, it was one of the biggest upset races in years.

### know of — 누군가에 대해 (직접 알지 못해도 이름이나 명성을) 알다

- *I don't know her personally, but I know of her reputation.*
  - 그녀를 직접 알지는 못하지만 그녀의 명성에 대해서는 알아요.

> **A:** Have you met the new director?
>
> **B:** No, but I know of him — he made that award-winning film.

### well liked — 인기가 많은, 호감을 사는

- *She is well liked by her teammates for her calm leadership.*
  - 그녀는 침착한 리더십 덕분에 팀원들에게 인기가 많아요.

> **A:** Do you think he'll win the election?
>
> **B:** Probably — he's well liked across most of the district.

### sort of — 어느 정도, 말하자면, 일종의

- *She's sort of a local legend in the music scene here.*
  - 그녀는 여기 음악계에서 말하자면 지역 전설 같은 존재예요.

> **A:** Is he the manager of the team?
>
> **B:** Sort of — he leads meetings but has no official title.

### open to — ~에 열린 자세를 가지다, ~을 수용할 의향이 있다

- *Are you open to trying a completely different approach?*
  - 완전히 다른 방식을 시도해 볼 의향이 있나요?

> **A:** I want to propose a major change to the plan.
>
> **B:** I'm open to it — let's hear what you have in mind.

### suffering from — ~로 고통받다, ~에 시달리다

- *The region is suffering from a severe drought this year.*
  - 그 지역은 올해 심각한 가뭄으로 고통받고 있어요.

> **A:** Why are so many people leaving that town?
>
> **B:** It's been suffering from high unemployment for years.

### cost of living — 생활비

- *The cost of living in this city has doubled in ten years.*
  - 이 도시의 생활비는 10년 만에 두 배가 됐어요.

> **A:** Are you thinking about moving to San Francisco?
>
> **B:** I'd love to, but the cost of living there is too high.

### to blame — 책임이 있는, ~의 탓인

- *Nobody knows exactly who is to blame for the system failure.*
  - 시스템 오류에 대해 정확히 누구의 책임인지 아무도 몰라요.

> **A:** The project missed the deadline — what happened?
>
> **B:** Poor communication is to blame, honestly.

### seize on — (기회나 약점 등을) 재빨리 잡아 활용하다

- *The rival brand seized on the recall to boost their own sales.*
  - 경쟁 브랜드는 제품 리콜 사태를 재빨리 활용해 자사 매출을 높였어요.

> **A:** The company made a huge mistake in their press release.
>
> **B:** Their competitors will definitely seize on that.

### jump into — ~에 (갑자기·적극적으로) 뛰어들다

- *She jumped into the debate before anyone else could respond.*
  - 그녀는 누구보다 먼저 토론에 뛰어들었어요.

> **A:** I heard Tom is running for city council.
>
> **B:** Yeah, he jumped into the race at the last minute.

### turn into — ~으로 변하다, ~이 되다

- *A small misunderstanding turned into a serious conflict.*
  - 사소한 오해가 심각한 갈등으로 변했어요.

> **A:** How did the interview go?
>
> **B:** It turned into a two-hour conversation — really intense.

### all of a sudden — 갑자기, 별안간

- *All of a sudden, she changed her mind about the whole plan.*
  - 갑자기 그녀가 계획 전체에 대한 마음을 바꿨어요.

> **A:** Why did everyone get quiet?
>
> **B:** All of a sudden the CEO walked in — nobody expected it.

### in good faith — 선의로, 성실하게, 진심을 다해

- *Both parties negotiated in good faith and reached an agreement.*
  - 양측은 성실하게 협상에 임해 합의에 도달했어요.

> **A:** Do you think he's genuinely trying to help?
>
> **B:** Yes, I believe he's acting in good faith.

### figure out — 알아내다, 파악하다

- *I need to figure out why the app keeps crashing.*
  - 앱이 왜 계속 충돌하는지 알아내야 해요.

> **A:** Why did sales drop so sharply last month?
>
> **B:** We're still trying to figure that out — the data is unclear.

### get on a plane — 비행기를 타다, 비행기로 이동하다

- *He got on a plane to close the deal in person.*
  - 그는 직접 계약을 성사시키기 위해 비행기를 탔어요.

> **A:** The client needs a face-to-face meeting urgently.
>
> **B:** Then we need to get on a plane first thing tomorrow.

### walk around — 돌아다니다, (이곳저곳을) 걸어서 둘러보다

- *We spent the whole morning walking around the old part of town.*
  - 우리는 오전 내내 구시가지를 걸어서 돌아다녔어요.

> **A:** What's the best way to get a feel for the neighborhood?
>
> **B:** Just walk around for an hour — you'll get the vibe quickly.

### engage with — ~와 소통하다, 대화하다, 교류하다

- *The candidate refused to engage with the press after the scandal.*
  - 그 후보는 스캔들 이후 언론과의 소통을 거부했어요.

> **A:** Did the speaker take any audience questions?
>
> **B:** No, she didn't seem willing to engage with us at all.

### up until that point — 그 시점까지, 그때까지는

- *Up until that point, the campaign had been running smoothly.*
  - 그 시점까지 선거운동은 순조롭게 진행되고 있었어요.

> **A:** When did things start to go wrong between them?
>
> **B:** Up until that point, they'd always gotten along well.

### knock on (someone's) door — 누군가의 문을 두드리다; 직접 찾아가다

- *I decided to knock on his door to introduce myself.*
  - 나는 자기소개를 하러 그의 집 문을 두드리기로 했다.

> **A:** How did you finally get in touch with him?
>
> **B:** I just knocked on his door and hoped for the best.

### to my surprise — 놀랍게도

- *To my surprise, he replied within an hour.*
  - 놀랍게도, 그는 한 시간 안에 답장을 보내왔다.

> **A:** Did you expect him to answer your email?
>
> **B:** No — to my surprise, he wrote back the same day.

### be set to — ~할 예정이다, ~하기로 되어 있다

- *She was set to leave for the conference the next morning.*
  - 그녀는 다음 날 아침 학회를 향해 떠날 예정이었다.

> **A:** When are you heading to Anchorage?
>
> **B:** I'm set to fly out first thing tomorrow.

### pick (someone) up — ~를 데리러 오다, 차로 태워주다

- *He offered to pick us up from the airport.*
  - 그는 공항에서 우리를 데리러 오겠다고 했다.

> **A:** Do you need a ride from the station?
>
> **B:** That would be great — could you pick me up at noon?

### drive around — 여기저기 차를 몰고 돌아다니다

- *We drove around town looking for a good place to eat.*
  - 우리는 맛있는 식당을 찾아 시내를 이리저리 돌아다녔다.

> **A:** How did you get to know the neighborhood so well?
>
> **B:** I just drove around for an afternoon and explored.

### for the most part — 대부분, 주로

- *For the most part, the residents were friendly and welcoming.*
  - 대부분의 주민들은 친절하고 따뜻하게 맞아주었다.

> **A:** Did you enjoy living in that small town?
>
> **B:** For the most part, yes — it was very peaceful.

### serve on — (위원회·이사회 등에서) 활동하다, 임원으로 봉사하다

- *He had served on the city's Arts Council for several years.*
  - 그는 수년간 시 예술위원회에서 활동했다.

> **A:** How does she know so much about local policy?
>
> **B:** She served on the school board for six years.

### overlook — (경치가) 내려다보이다, ~을 조망하다

- *The park overlooks the water and the distant mountains.*
  - 그 공원에서는 바다와 저 멀리 산들이 내려다보인다.

> **A:** What's the view like from your hotel room?
>
> **B:** It overlooks the harbor — absolutely stunning at sunset.

### a better way of putting it — 그것을 표현하는 더 나은 방법 (더 적절한 표현)

- *He was angry, or maybe 'frustrated' is a better way of putting it.*
  - 그는 화가 났다, 아니면 '답답하다'는 게 더 적절한 표현일 것 같다.

> **A:** She seemed really upset at the meeting.
>
> **B:** Disappointed is a better way of putting it.

### get down to — 본론으로 들어가다, 핵심 문제를 다루다

- *Let's get down to the real issue before we run out of time.*
  - 시간이 다 되기 전에 핵심 문제로 들어가 봅시다.

> **A:** We've been chatting for a while — shall we get down to business?
>
> **B:** Yes, let's go over the main agenda now.

### get into — ~에 참여하다, ~에 뛰어들다

- *Why did you decide to get into politics in the first place?*
  - 애초에 왜 정치에 뛰어들기로 결심했나요?

> **A:** How did you get into teaching English?
>
> **B:** I just wanted to help people communicate better.

### step up — 나서다, 더 큰 책임을 맡다

- *Nobody else volunteered, so he stepped up and took charge.*
  - 아무도 자원하지 않아서, 그가 나서서 책임을 맡았다.

> **A:** We really need a team leader for this project.
>
> **B:** I'll step up — I'm ready for the challenge.

### fed up with — ~에 질리다, ~에 신물이 나다

- *She was completely fed up with the constant delays at work.*
  - 그녀는 직장에서의 끊임없는 지연에 완전히 질려 버렸다.

> **A:** Are you still at that company?
>
> **B:** No, I got fed up with the poor management and quit.

### throw one's hat in the ring — 선거나 경쟁에 출마하다, 도전장을 내밀다

- *She finally decided to throw her hat in the ring for the city council race.*
  - 그녀는 마침내 시의회 선거에 출마하기로 결심했다.

> **A:** Did you hear that Mark threw his hat in the ring?
>
> **B:** Really? I hope he wins the election!

### run against — ~에 맞서 선거에 출마하다, ~와 경쟁하다

- *He plans to run against the incumbent senator in the next election.*
  - 그는 다음 선거에서 현직 상원의원에 맞서 출마할 계획이다.

> **A:** Who are you running against in November?

> **A:** How do I apply for the new library card?
>
> **B:** Just fill out the short form at the front desk.

### ranked choice voting — 순위 선택 투표 (선호도 순서대로 후보에 순위를 매기는 투표 방식)

- *Ranked choice voting lets each voter express more than one preference.*
  - 순위 선택 투표는 유권자가 두 명 이상의 후보에 대한 선호를 표현할 수 있게 한다.

> **A:** I've never heard of ranked choice voting — how does it work?
>
> **B:** You rank the candidates one, two, three, in your preferred order.

### in order of preference — 선호도 순서로, 좋아하는 순서대로

- *Please list the three positions you want in order of preference.*
  - 원하는 세 가지 직책을 선호도 순서대로 나열해 주세요.

> **A:** How should I fill out the survey about lunch options?
>
> **B:** Rank all five choices in order of preference, most liked first.

### be in contention — 경쟁에 남아 있다, 경쟁 대상이 되다

- *Three candidates are still in contention for the open senate seat.*
  - 세 후보가 아직 비어 있는 상원 의석을 두고 경쟁 중이다.

> **A:** Is our team still being considered for the project?
>
> **B:** Yes, we're still in contention — final decision is Friday.

### tally the votes — 표를 집계하다

- *Election workers stayed up all night to tally the votes.*
  - 선거 관리원들은 밤새 표를 집계했다.

> **A:** When will we know who won the student council election?
>
> **B:** They're tallying the votes now — results by this afternoon.

### get on the ballot — 투표용지에 (후보로) 이름을 올리다

- *The independent candidate collected enough signatures to get on the ballot.*
  - 무소속 후보는 투표용지에 이름을 올리기 위해 충분한 서명을 모았다.

> **A:** Is the third-party candidate actually running in November?
>
> **B:** Yes, she officially got on the ballot last week.

### in the end — 결국, 최종적으로

- *In the end, the voters chose the lesser-known challenger over the incumbent.*
  - 결국, 유권자들은 현직자보다 덜 알려진 도전자를 선택했다.

> **A:** Did the two parties ever reach a deal?
>
> **B:** In the end, they agreed on a compromise budget.

### on the line — 위태롭다, 위험에 처해 있다

- *With this vote, the senator's entire political career was on the line.*
  - 이번 투표로 그 의원의 정치 경력 전체가 위태로워졌다.

> **A:** Why is everyone treating this election like it's so critical?
>
> **B:** They feel democracy itself is on the line this year.

### all-out — 총력적인, 전면적인

- *The party launched an all-out effort to mobilize voters in key districts.*
  - 그 당은 핵심 선거구에서 유권자를 동원하기 위해 총력적인 노력을 기울였다.

> **A:** How seriously is the campaign taking this race?
>
> **B:** It's all-out — rallies, ads, and door-knocking every single day.

### search for — ~을 찾다, 물색하다

- *Party officials searched for a candidate with strong local name recognition.*
  - 당 관계자들은 지역 인지도가 높은 후보를 물색했다.

> **A:** Why is the campaign director traveling to so many small towns?
>
> **B:** She's searching for local volunteers to help with get-out-the-vote efforts.

### make a field day out of — (상대방의 실수나 약점을) 최대한 이용하다, 신나게 활용하다

- *The opposition will make a field day out of the minister's scandal.*
  - 야당은 장관의 스캔들을 최대한 이용할 것이다.

> **A:** Did you hear the CEO made a huge accounting error?
>
> **B:** The press will make a field day out of that for weeks.

### play out — (상황이) 전개되다, 결말이 나다

- *No one knows yet how the negotiation will play out.*
  - 협상이 어떻게 전개될지 아직 아무도 모른다.

> **A:** Do you think the two teams will draw tonight?
>
> **B:** Hard to say — let's just watch how it plays out.

### come out — (정보, 뉴스가) 나오다, 드러나다, 알려지다

- *The report came out right before the deadline, shocking everyone.*
  - 보고서가 마감 직전에 나와 모두를 놀라게 했다.

> **A:** When did the story about the data breach come out?
>
> **B:** It came out late last night — total chaos since then.

### focused on — ~에 집중하다, ~에 초점을 맞추다

- *Most residents are focused on rising food prices, not politics.*
  - 대부분의 주민들은 정치가 아니라 오르는 식료품 물가에 집중하고 있다.

> **A:** Are voters worried about the candidate's past statements?
>
> **B:** Not really — they're focused on jobs and the cost of living.

### too early to tell — 아직 판단하기 이르다, 아직 알 수 없다

- *It's too early to tell whether the new policy will succeed.*
  - 새 정책이 성공할지는 아직 판단하기 이르다.

> **A:** Will the startup be profitable by next year?
>
> **B:** It's too early to tell — they just launched last month.

### know for sure — 확실히 알다, 틀림없이 알다

- *We don't know for sure who will win until all votes are counted.*
  - 모든 표가 집계될 때까지 누가 이길지 확실히 알 수 없다.

> **A:** Is she definitely coming to the meeting tomorrow?
>
> **B:** I don't know for sure — she hasn't confirmed yet.
