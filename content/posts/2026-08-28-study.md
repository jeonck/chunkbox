## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### Hang in there — 버텨, 포기하지 마

- *The exam is tough, but hang in there — you're almost done.*
  - 시험이 힘들지만 버텨 — 거의 다 왔어.

> **A:** I've been studying for ten hours and I want to quit.
>
> **B:** Hang in there — the test is tomorrow morning.

### Are you decent? — 들어가도 되나요? (옷을 입고 있나요?)

- *She knocked and called, 'Are you decent?' before opening the door.*
  - 그녀는 문을 열기 전에 노크하며 '들어가도 돼?'라고 불렀다.

> **A:** Are you decent? I'm coming in with your laundry.
>
> **B:** Yeah, come on in, I'm fully dressed.

### Icing on the cake — 금상첨화, 덤으로 좋은 것

- *Getting a bonus was icing on the cake after my promotion.*
  - 승진에 보너스까지 받은 건 금상첨화였다.

> **A:** We won the championship and the crowd gave us a standing ovation.
>
> **B:** That ovation was truly icing on the cake!

### I'm dead serious — 진짜 진심이야

- *I'm dead serious — quit your job and start your own business.*
  - 진짜 진심이야 — 직장 그만두고 사업 시작해.

> **A:** Are you actually moving to another country?
>
> **B:** I'm dead serious. I leave next month.

### Meet me halfway — 서로 양보하자, 중간에서 만나자

- *I'll lower my price if you meet me halfway on delivery costs.*
  - 배송비를 반반 부담하면 내가 가격을 낮출게.

> **A:** I can't agree to all your terms.
>
> **B:** Just meet me halfway and we'll have a deal.

### Keep out of this — 이 일에 끼어들지 마

- *This is between me and Jake, so keep out of this.*
  - 이건 나와 제이크 사이의 일이니 끼어들지 마.

> **A:** Let me help you settle this argument with your sister.
>
> **B:** No, please keep out of this — it's personal.

### Mind your own business — 네 일이나 신경 써

- *When he asked about my salary, I told him to mind his own business.*
  - 그가 내 월급을 묻자 나는 네 일이나 신경 쓰라고 했다.

> **A:** Why did you break up with your boyfriend?
>
> **B:** Mind your own business, please.

### Bumper to bumper — 차가 꽉 막혀서 (범퍼가 맞닿을 정도로)

- *Traffic was bumper to bumper on the highway this morning.*
  - 오늘 아침 고속도로가 차로 꽉 막혔다.

> **A:** Why are you so late to the meeting?
>
> **B:** The road was bumper to bumper for two hours.

### Catch you later — 나중에 봐, 다음에 봐

- *I have to run to class — catch you later!*
  - 수업 가야 해 — 나중에 봐!

> **A:** Are you leaving already?
>
> **B:** Yeah, gotta go — catch you later!

### Once-in-a-lifetime chance — 일생에 한 번뿐인 기회

- *Studying abroad at Oxford is a once-in-a-lifetime chance.*
  - 옥스퍼드에서 유학하는 건 일생에 한 번뿐인 기회야.

> **A:** Should I audition for that Broadway show?
>
> **B:** Absolutely — it's a once-in-a-lifetime chance!

### Everything came out wrong — 모든 게 잘못됐어, 말이 다 꼬였어

- *I tried to apologize, but everything came out wrong.*
  - 사과하려 했는데 말이 다 꼬여버렸어.

> **A:** Why is she upset with you now?
>
> **B:** I tried to explain, but everything came out wrong.

### Fair is fair — 공평한 건 공평한 거야

- *You worked more hours, so fair is fair — you get a bigger share.*
  - 네가 더 많이 일했으니 공평한 건 공평한 거야 — 더 많이 가져가.

> **A:** Why should I pay the same if I ate less?
>
> **B:** Fair is fair — let's split it by what we ordered.

### Sticky fingers — 손버릇이 나쁜, 도벽이 있는

- *The manager fired him because he had sticky fingers at the register.*
  - 매니저는 그가 금전 등록기에서 손버릇이 나빠 해고했다.

> **A:** Someone keeps taking snacks from my desk.
>
> **B:** I think Jake has sticky fingers — I'd keep your stuff locked up.

### Save face — 체면을 지키다

- *He blamed the weather to save face after losing the game.*
  - 그는 경기에서 진 후 체면을 지키려고 날씨 탓을 했다.

> **A:** Why did he give such a weird excuse for missing the deadline?
>
> **B:** He needed to save face in front of the whole team.

### If you've seen one, you've seen them all — 하나를 보면 다 알 수 있어

- *These tourist shops are boring — if you've seen one, you've seen them all.*
  - 이 관광 상점들은 지루해 — 하나를 보면 다 아는 거야.

> **A:** Should we visit another souvenir market?
>
> **B:** Nah, if you've seen one, you've seen them all.

### On the house — 서비스로, 무료로

- *The waiter smiled and said dessert was on the house.*
  - 웨이터는 웃으며 디저트는 서비스라고 했다.

> **A:** How much do I owe for the extra coffee?
>
> **B:** Nothing — it's on the house. Enjoy!

### Wouldn't do any harm — 해가 될 건 없어

- *Asking for a raise wouldn't do any harm — the worst is a no.*
  - 연봉 인상을 요청해도 해가 될 건 없어 — 최악은 거절이잖아.

> **A:** Should I email the professor to ask for an extension?
>
> **B:** It wouldn't do any harm to try.

### Penny-pincher — 구두쇠

- *He's such a penny-pincher that he reuses paper towels.*
  - 그는 너무 구두쇠라 종이타월을 재사용한다.

> **A:** Did Tom really bring his own coffee to the café?
>
> **B:** Yes, he's a total penny-pincher.

### Take the fifth — 묵비권을 행사하다, 진술을 거부하다

- *When asked where he was, he decided to take the fifth.*
  - 어디 있었냐고 묻자 그는 묵비권을 행사하기로 했다.

> **A:** Did you eat the last slice of pizza?
>
> **B:** I'm going to take the fifth on that one.

### Tongue-tied — 말문이 막힌

- *She got tongue-tied when she met her favorite celebrity.*
  - 그녀는 좋아하는 연예인을 만나자 말문이 막혔다.

> **A:** How did your speech go?
>
> **B:** Terrible — I got completely tongue-tied on stage.

### Drop the subject — 그 얘기 그만해, 화제를 바꿔

- *He was clearly upset, so she decided to drop the subject.*
  - 그가 분명히 화난 것 같아서 그녀는 그 얘기를 그만하기로 했다.

> **A:** Can we just drop the subject? I don't want to argue anymore.
>
> **B:** Fine, let's talk about something else.

### A stitch in time saves nine — 제때 한 땀이 아홉 땀을 아낀다 (호미로 막을 걸 가래로 막는다)

- *Fix that small leak now — a stitch in time saves nine.*
  - 그 작은 누수를 지금 고쳐 — 호미로 막을 걸 가래로 막는 법이야.

> **A:** It's just a tiny crack in the wall — I'll deal with it later.
>
> **B:** Don't wait. A stitch in time saves nine.

### A friend in need is a friend indeed — 어려울 때 친구가 진짜 친구다

- *She helped me move when no one else would — a friend in need is a friend indeed.*
  - 아무도 도와주지 않을 때 그녀가 이사를 도와줬어 — 어려울 때 친구가 진짜 친구야.

> **A:** Jake stayed up all night helping me finish my project.
>
> **B:** A friend in need is a friend indeed — he's a keeper.

### Here's mud in your eye — 건배! (격식 없는 건배 표현)

- *He raised his glass and said, 'Here's mud in your eye!'*
  - 그는 잔을 들며 '건배!'라고 외쳤다.

> **A:** Here's mud in your eye — congrats on the new job!
>
> **B:** Cheers! I couldn't have done it without your support.

### Money makes the mare go — 돈이 있어야 일이 된다

- *They finally approved the project once the budget came through — money makes the mare go.*
  - 예산이 확보되자 드디어 프로젝트가 승인됐어 — 돈이 있어야 일이 되는 법이지.

> **A:** Why did the contractor suddenly start working so fast?
>
> **B:** We paid upfront — money makes the mare go.

### I'll say — 그러게 말이야, 정말 그래

- *'That concert was amazing!' 'I'll say — best show I've ever seen.'*
  - '그 콘서트 정말 대단했어!' '그러게 말이야 — 내가 본 최고의 공연이었어.'

> **A:** This summer heat is absolutely brutal.
>
> **B:** I'll say — I've never sweated this much in my life.

### Lightning never strikes the same place twice — 같은 불운이 두 번 찾아오지 않는다

- *I doubt I'll fail again — lightning never strikes the same place twice.*
  - 또 실패할 것 같지 않아 — 같은 불운이 두 번 오지 않잖아.

> **A:** Are you worried about another car accident on that road?
>
> **B:** Not really — lightning never strikes the same place twice.

### The squeaky wheel gets the oil — 삐걱거리는 바퀴가 기름을 얻는다 (우는 아이 젖 준다)

- *He kept complaining and finally got a refund — the squeaky wheel gets the oil.*
  - 그는 계속 항의했고 결국 환불을 받았다 — 우는 아이 젖 주는 법이야.

> **A:** How did she get a room upgrade at the hotel?
>
> **B:** She asked three times — the squeaky wheel gets the oil.

### Break the ice — 어색한 분위기를 깨다

- *He told a funny joke to break the ice at the first meeting.*
  - 그는 첫 만남에서 어색한 분위기를 깨려고 재미있는 농담을 했다.

> **A:** Everyone at the party looks so awkward and quiet.
>
> **B:** Let's play a quick game to break the ice.

### Piece of cake — 식은 죽 먹기, 매우 쉬운 일

- *The driving test was a piece of cake for her.*
  - 운전 면허 시험은 그녀에게 식은 죽 먹기였다.

> **A:** How was the math quiz?
>
> **B:** A piece of cake — I finished in ten minutes.

### Cost an arm and a leg — 엄청나게 비싸다

- *That luxury watch cost an arm and a leg.*
  - 그 명품 시계는 엄청나게 비쌌다.

> **A:** Did you buy tickets for the concert?
>
> **B:** No, they cost an arm and a leg.

### Hit the nail on the head — 핵심을 찌르다, 정확히 맞추다

- *Your analysis really hit the nail on the head.*
  - 네 분석이 정말 핵심을 정확히 찔렀어.

> **A:** I think the problem is poor communication between teams.
>
> **B:** You hit the nail on the head — that's exactly it.

### Spill the beans — 비밀을 누설하다

- *Don't spill the beans about the surprise party.*
  - 깜짝 파티에 대한 비밀을 누설하지 마.

> **A:** Did you tell anyone about the new product launch?
>
> **B:** I accidentally spilled the beans to my coworker.

### Under the weather — 몸이 좋지 않은

- *She called in sick because she was feeling under the weather.*
  - 그녀는 몸이 좋지 않아 결근 전화를 했다.

> **A:** You look pale — are you okay?
>
> **B:** I'm a bit under the weather. Probably just a cold.

### Burn the midnight oil — 밤새워 공부하다/일하다

- *She burned the midnight oil to finish her thesis.*
  - 그녀는 논문을 완성하기 위해 밤새워 작업했다.

> **A:** When did you sleep? You look exhausted.
>
> **B:** I had to burn the midnight oil for the deadline.

### Cut corners — 절차를 무시하고 대충 하다

- *The builder cut corners, and the roof started leaking.*
  - 건축업자가 대충 공사를 했더니 지붕에서 물이 새기 시작했다.

> **A:** Why did the app crash on launch day?
>
> **B:** The team cut corners during testing.

### Bite the bullet — 이를 악물고 참다

- *I hated the dentist, but I bit the bullet and made an appointment.*
  - 치과가 싫었지만 이를 악물고 예약했다.

> **A:** I really don't want to apologize to him.
>
> **B:** Just bite the bullet and get it over with.

### Blessing in disguise — 전화위복

- *Losing that job was a blessing in disguise — I found a better one.*
  - 그 직장을 잃은 건 전화위복이었어 — 더 좋은 곳을 찾았거든.

> **A:** I'm devastated that I missed the flight.
>
> **B:** It might be a blessing in disguise — that flight got delayed six hours.

### Call it a day — 오늘은 여기서 마치다

- *We've been working for eight hours — let's call it a day.*
  - 여덟 시간이나 일했으니 오늘은 여기서 마치자.

> **A:** We're not going to finish this report tonight.
>
> **B:** Agreed — let's call it a day and continue tomorrow.

### Get out of hand — 걷잡을 수 없게 되다

- *The argument got out of hand and someone called the police.*
  - 말다툼이 걷잡을 수 없게 되어 누군가 경찰을 불렀다.

> **A:** The kids' game is getting really loud and wild.
>
> **B:** Yeah, things are starting to get out of hand.

### Give someone the benefit of the doubt — 의심스럽지만 일단 믿어주다

- *She seemed late on purpose, but I gave her the benefit of the doubt.*
  - 일부러 늦은 것 같았지만 일단 믿어주기로 했다.

> **A:** Do you think he's telling the truth about being sick?
>
> **B:** I'll give him the benefit of the doubt this time.

### Go back to the drawing board — 처음부터 다시 시작하다

- *The client rejected our proposal, so we had to go back to the drawing board.*
  - 고객이 제안서를 거절해서 처음부터 다시 시작해야 했다.

> **A:** The prototype completely failed the safety test.
>
> **B:** Back to the drawing board — let's rethink the whole design.

### Hang on — 잠깐 기다려, 버텨

- *Hang on — let me double-check the address before we leave.*
  - 잠깐만 — 출발 전에 주소를 다시 확인할게.

> **A:** I'm about to send the email to all staff.
>
> **B:** Hang on — there's a typo in the subject line.

### Hit the sack — 잠자리에 들다

- *I'm exhausted — I'm going to hit the sack early tonight.*
  - 너무 피곤해 — 오늘은 일찍 잠자리에 들 거야.

> **A:** Are you coming to the late-night movie?
>
> **B:** No, I need to hit the sack — work starts at six.

### Jump on the bandwagon — 유행을 따르다, 대세에 편승하다

- *Everyone started using that app, so he jumped on the bandwagon too.*
  - 모두가 그 앱을 쓰기 시작하자 그도 대세에 편승했다.

> **A:** You're doing intermittent fasting now too?
>
> **B:** I know, I totally jumped on the bandwagon.

### Keep your chin up — 힘내, 기운 내

- *Keep your chin up — things will get better soon.*
  - 기운 내 — 곧 좋아질 거야.

> **A:** I failed the interview again. I feel hopeless.
>
> **B:** Keep your chin up — the right job is coming.

### Leave no stone unturned — 모든 수단을 다 써보다

- *The detective left no stone unturned in the investigation.*
  - 형사는 수사에서 모든 수단을 다 동원했다.

> **A:** We still can't find her missing cat.
>
> **B:** Let's leave no stone unturned — check every room again.

### Let the cat out of the bag — 비밀을 (실수로) 누설하다

- *He let the cat out of the bag about the surprise party.*
  - 그가 깜짝 파티에 대한 비밀을 실수로 누설해 버렸다.

> **A:** How does Maria already know about her birthday party?
>
> **B:** Sorry — I let the cat out of the bag by accident.

### Make ends meet — 근근이 생계를 유지하다

- *With two part-time jobs, she barely makes ends meet.*
  - 두 개의 파트타임 일을 하며 그녀는 겨우 생계를 유지한다.

> **A:** Is Tom doing okay financially?
>
> **B:** He's struggling — can barely make ends meet.

### No pain, no gain — 고통 없이 얻는 것도 없다

- *Training hurts, but no pain, no gain.*
  - 훈련은 힘들지만 고통 없이 얻는 것도 없다.

> **A:** My legs are killing me after yesterday's workout.
>
> **B:** No pain, no gain — keep going!

### Out of the blue — 갑자기, 예고 없이

- *Out of the blue, she received a job offer from abroad.*
  - 갑자기 그녀는 해외에서 채용 제안을 받았다.

> **A:** Did you expect your ex to call you last night?
>
> **B:** Not at all — it came completely out of the blue.

### Over my dead body — 내 눈에 흙이 들어가기 전엔 안 돼

- *You'll sell this house over my dead body.*
  - 내 눈에 흙이 들어가기 전엔 이 집 절대 못 팔아.

> **A:** Dad, I want to drop out of university.
>
> **B:** Over my dead body — you're finishing that degree.

### Play it by ear — 상황을 봐가며 하다

- *We don't have a set plan — we'll just play it by ear.*
  - 정해진 계획은 없어 — 상황 보면서 하자.

> **A:** What's the plan if it rains during the picnic?
>
> **B:** Let's just play it by ear and see what happens.

### Pull someone's leg — 놀리다, 농담하다

- *Don't worry — I was just pulling your leg.*
  - 걱정 마 — 그냥 놀린 거야.

> **A:** Wait, is our flight really cancelled?
>
> **B:** Ha! I'm just pulling your leg. We're good.

### Put all your eggs in one basket — 한 곳에 모든 것을 걸다

- *Don't put all your eggs in one basket — apply to several schools.*
  - 한 곳에 모든 것을 걸지 마 — 여러 학교에 지원해.

> **A:** I only applied to one company for my internship.
>
> **B:** Don't put all your eggs in one basket — apply to more.

### Raining cats and dogs — 비가 억수같이 쏟아지다

- *It's raining cats and dogs — grab an umbrella before you go.*
  - 비가 억수같이 쏟아지고 있어 — 나가기 전에 우산 챙겨.

> **A:** Should we walk to the restaurant?
>
> **B:** No way — it's raining cats and dogs out there.

### Read between the lines — 행간을 읽다, 속뜻을 파악하다

- *She didn't say she was angry, but you could read between the lines.*
  - 그녀는 화났다고 말하진 않았지만 행간을 읽을 수 있었다.

> **A:** His email was very polite, but something felt off.
>
> **B:** You have to read between the lines — he's clearly unhappy.

### Rule of thumb — 경험에서 나온 대략적인 기준

- *As a rule of thumb, drink eight glasses of water a day.*
  - 경험상, 하루에 물 여덟 잔을 마시는 것이 좋습니다.

> **A:** How long should I bake this chicken?
>
> **B:** As a rule of thumb, about 30 minutes at 180°C.

### See eye to eye — 의견이 일치하다

- *My sister and I don't always see eye to eye on politics.*
  - 제 언니와 저는 정치에 대해 항상 의견이 일치하지는 않아요.

> **A:** Do you and Tom agree on the new plan?
>
> **B:** Not really — we rarely see eye to eye.

### Sit on the fence — 중립을 지키다, 어느 쪽도 편들지 않다
