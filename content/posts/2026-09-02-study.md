## Session Overview

이번 세션에서는 처음 인사와 자기소개, 날씨·취미·가족 등 스몰토크 주제, 오랜만의 재회 인사, 대화 중 자연스러운 반응 표현, 그리고 작별 인사와 연락처 교환까지 일상 영어 회화에 꼭 필요한 90여 개의 청크를 집중 학습했습니다. 낯선 사람과의 첫 만남부터 파티·네트워킹 자리, 예상치 못한 재회 상황까지 다양한 실생활 맥락을 다뤘습니다. 각 표현의 뉘앙스와 사용 상황을 잘 익혀 두면 영어로 더욱 자연스럽고 자신감 있게 소통할 수 있습니다.

## 📦 Phrasal Verbs & Chunks

### Nice to finally meet you. — 드디어 만나게 되어 반갑습니다 (기다리던 만남에 쓰는 인사)

- *Nice to finally meet you — I've seen your work online!*
  - 드디어 만나게 되어 반가워요 — 온라인에서 작업물 봤어요!

> **A:** Hi! You must be the famous Chef Marco!
>
> **B:** That's me! Nice to finally meet you in person.

### Let me introduce you. — 제가 소개해 드릴게요 (다른 사람을 소개할 때)

- *Let me introduce you to my manager, Mr. Kim.*
  - 제 매니저 김 씨에게 소개해 드릴게요.

> **A:** Do you know anyone here at this party?
>
> **B:** Not yet — let me introduce you to a few people.

### I've heard so much about you. — 말씀 많이 들었어요 (처음 만나는 상대에게 하는 인사)

- *I've heard so much about you from Jenny!*
  - 제니한테서 말씀 정말 많이 들었어요!

> **A:** Hi, I'm David — Jake's college roommate.
>
> **B:** Oh! I've heard so much about you!

### It's nice to meet you. — 만나서 반가워요 (처음 만남의 기본 인사)

- *It's nice to meet you — thanks so much for coming today.*
  - 만나서 반가워요 — 오늘 와 주셔서 감사해요.

> **A:** Hi, I'm Lisa from the marketing team.
>
> **B:** It's nice to meet you, Lisa!

### See you again. — 또 봐요 / 다시 만나요 (작별 인사)

- *I hope to see you again at next year's conference.*
  - 내년 컨퍼런스에서 다시 만났으면 좋겠어요.

> **A:** It was really great chatting with you tonight!
>
> **B:** Likewise! Hope to see you again soon.

### Do you mind if I join you? — 함께 앉아도 / 같이 해도 될까요? (정중히 허락 구하기)

- *Do you mind if I join you for lunch today?*
  - 오늘 점심 같이 먹어도 될까요?

> **A:** Do you mind if I join you at this table?
>
> **B:** Not at all — please, have a seat!

### How do you guys know each other? — 두 분은 어떻게 아는 사이예요? (관계를 물을 때)

- *So, how do you guys know each other exactly?*
  - 그래서, 두 분은 정확히 어떻게 아는 사이예요?

> **A:** You two seem really close already!
>
> **B:** Ha, how do you guys know each other anyway?

### What was your name again? — 성함이 어떻게 되셨더라? (이름을 잊었을 때 다시 묻기)

- *Sorry, what was your name again? I've already forgotten.*
  - 죄송한데, 성함이 어떻게 되셨더라? 벌써 잊어버렸네요.

> **A:** We met briefly at the workshop last week.
>
> **B:** Right! I'm so sorry — what was your name again?

### I'm terrible with names. — 저는 이름을 잘 못 외워요 (이름 기억을 못 한다고 할 때)

- *I'm terrible with names, so please remind me later.*
  - 저는 이름을 잘 못 외워서 나중에 다시 알려 주세요.

> **A:** Don't worry if you forget mine — everyone does.
>
> **B:** Ha, I'm terrible with names too, honestly.

### I feel like I've seen you somewhere before. — 어디서 본 것 같아요 (낯이 익을 때 쓰는 표현)

- *I feel like I've seen you somewhere before — maybe online?*
  - 어디서 본 것 같은데 — 혹시 온라인에서요?

> **A:** I feel like I've seen you somewhere before.
>
> **B:** Really? I work at the café down the street!

### I never forget a face. — 얼굴은 절대 안 잊어버려요 (얼굴 기억력이 좋다고 할 때)

- *I never forget a face — we met at Tom's party!*
  - 얼굴은 절대 안 잊어버리는데 — 톰 파티에서 만났잖아요!

> **A:** Did we meet before? I honestly can't remember.
>
> **B:** Yes, last spring! I never forget a face.

### What do you do for a living? — 무슨 일을 하세요? (직업을 물을 때 쓰는 표현)

- *So, what do you do for a living these days?*
  - 그래서, 요즘 무슨 일을 하고 계세요?

> **A:** You seem really passionate about technology.
>
> **B:** I am! What do you do for a living?

### What kind of work are you in? — 어떤 분야 일을 하세요? (업종이나 직종을 물을 때)

- *What kind of work are you in? You look very busy.*
  - 어떤 분야 일을 하세요? 많이 바빠 보이네요.

> **A:** I just got back from a business trip to Seoul.
>
> **B:** Interesting! What kind of work are you in?

### How long have you been here? — 여기 온 지 얼마나 됐어요? (거주 기간을 물을 때)

- *How long have you been here in this city?*
  - 이 도시에 온 지 얼마나 됐어요?

> **A:** You seem to know this neighborhood really well!
>
> **B:** How long have you been here? It really shows.

### Are you here for the first time? — 여기 처음이에요? (처음 방문인지 물을 때)

- *Are you here for the first time, or have you visited before?*
  - 여기 처음이에요, 아니면 전에도 오신 적 있어요?

> **A:** You look a little lost — are you here for the first time?
>
> **B:** Yes! Do you have any recommendations?

### I just moved here. — 저 여기 막 이사 왔어요 (최근에 이사 왔다고 말할 때)

- *I just moved here last month and I'm still exploring.*
  - 지난달에 막 이사 와서 아직 여기저기 둘러보는 중이에요.

> **A:** Do you know any good restaurants nearby?
>
> **B:** Not yet — I just moved here a few weeks ago.

### I was born and raised here. — 여기서 태어나고 자랐어요 (고향임을 밝힐 때)

- *I was born and raised here, so I know every corner.*
  - 여기서 태어나고 자라서 모르는 곳이 없어요.

> **A:** You must know this city incredibly well.
>
> **B:** I do — I was born and raised here!

### Are you from around here? — 이 근처 출신이에요? (출신지를 물을 때)

- *Are you from around here, or did you move recently?*
  - 이 근처 출신이에요, 아니면 최근에 이사 오셨어요?

> **A:** Are you from around here? Your accent sounds local.
>
> **B:** Born and raised in this town, actually!

### Do you have any kids? — 자녀가 있으세요? (자녀 유무를 물을 때)

- *Do you have any kids, or is it just the two of you?*
  - 자녀가 있으세요, 아니면 둘이서만 사세요?

> **A:** I love how family-friendly this neighborhood is.
>
> **B:** Same! Do you have any kids yourself?

### Are you married or not? — 결혼하셨어요? (결혼 여부를 물을 때)

- *Are you married or not? I never know what to assume.*
  - 결혼하셨어요? 어떤지 몰라서요.

> **A:** I keep seeing you wear a ring — are you married or not?
>
> **B:** Ha, yes! Five years now — time really flies.

### Where did you go to school? — 어디서 학교를 다녔어요? (학교 배경을 묻는 스몰토크)

- *Where did you go to school? I graduated from Boston College.*
  - 어디서 학교를 다녔어요? 저는 보스턴 칼리지를 졸업했어요.

> **A:** Where did you go to school? We might have overlapped.
>
> **B:** I went to NYU — class of 2015!

### Nice weather, isn't it? — 날씨 좋죠? (날씨로 대화를 시작하는 스몰토크)

- *Nice weather, isn't it? Perfect for a walk in the park.*
  - 날씨 좋죠? 공원 산책하기 딱 좋네요.

> **A:** Nice weather, isn't it? Finally some sunshine!
>
> **B:** Absolutely — I've been waiting all week for this.

### It's freezing outside. — 밖이 꽁꽁 얼 정도로 추워요.

- *It's freezing outside, so make sure you wear a heavy coat.*
  - 밖이 엄청 추우니까 두꺼운 코트를 꼭 입어요.

> **A:** It's freezing outside! I can't feel my hands.
>
> **B:** Come in quick — I'll make some hot tea.

### It's pouring. — 비가 엄청나게 쏟아지고 있어요.

- *It's pouring outside, so let's wait here a little longer.*
  - 밖에 비가 억수같이 쏟아지고 있어서 조금 더 기다려요.

> **A:** Did you bring an umbrella today?
>
> **B:** No, and it's pouring out there — great timing!

### Looks like it's going to rain. — 비가 올 것 같아요.

- *Looks like it's going to rain — we should head inside soon.*
  - 비가 올 것 같아요 — 빨리 안으로 들어가요.

> **A:** Should we stay for the outdoor concert?
>
> **B:** Looks like it's going to rain — maybe not.

### Is this weather for real? — 이 날씨 진짜야? (날씨가 너무 극단적일 때 쓰는 표현)

- *Is this weather for real? It's snowing in April!*
  - 이 날씨 진짜야? 4월에 눈이 오다니!

> **A:** Is this weather for real? It hit 40 degrees today.
>
> **B:** I know — I'm melting just standing here.

### It's been a long day. — 오늘 하루가 정말 길고 힘들었어요.

- *It's been a long day — I just want to go home and rest.*
  - 오늘 하루가 너무 길었어요 — 그냥 집에 가서 쉬고 싶어요.

> **A:** You look exhausted. Are you okay?
>
> **B:** It's been a long day — back-to-back meetings.

### Great party. — 파티 정말 좋다! (파티 자리에서 분위기를 띄우는 표현)

- *Great party! I've really enjoyed meeting everyone here.*
  - 파티 정말 좋네요! 여기서 여러분을 만나 즐거웠어요.

> **A:** Great party, right? The host really outdid themselves.
>
> **B:** Totally — the food alone was worth coming for.

### How have you been? — 그동안 어떻게 지냈어요? (오랜만에 만난 사람에게)

- *How have you been? I haven't seen you in months!*
  - 그동안 어떻게 지냈어요? 몇 달째 못 봤잖아요!

> **A:** Oh wow, it's you! How have you been?
>
> **B:** Really good, thanks — staying busy as always!

### How are you doing? — 어떻게 지내요? (일상적인 안부 인사)

- *How are you doing? You look really well today!*
  - 어떻게 지내요? 오늘 정말 좋아 보이네요!

> **A:** Hey! How are you doing these days?
>
> **B:** Not bad at all — life is pretty good!

### How is your family? — 가족들은 어떻게 지내요?

- *How is your family? Are the kids doing well in school?*
  - 가족들은 어떻게 지내요? 아이들은 학교에서 잘 지내고 있어요?

> **A:** How is your family? It's been so long!
>
> **B:** They're all great — thanks so much for asking.

### How is work going? — 일은 어떻게 돼가요?

- *How is work going? Have you settled into your new role yet?*
  - 일은 어떻게 돼가요? 새 직책에 잘 적응했어요?

> **A:** How is work going for you lately?
>
> **B:** Really well — I just got a promotion!

### How are things going lately? — 요즘 어떻게 지내요? / 요즘 어떻게 돼가요?

- *How are things going lately? I heard you moved to a new city.*
  - 요즘 어떻게 지내요? 새 도시로 이사했다고 들었어요.

> **A:** How are things going lately? You seem different.
>
> **B:** Honestly, hectic but exciting — lots of changes!

### You haven't changed a bit. — 하나도 안 변했네요! (오랜만에 만났을 때 칭찬으로 쓰는 말)

- *Wow, you haven't changed a bit since high school!*
  - 와, 고등학교 때랑 정말 하나도 안 변했네요!

> **A:** You haven't changed a bit — still the same smile!
>
> **B:** Ha, thank you — you look amazing yourself!

### Nice to see you again. — 다시 만나서 반가워요.

- *It's really nice to see you again after all these years.*
  - 이렇게 오랜만에 다시 만나니 정말 반가워요.

> **A:** Nice to see you again! It's been forever.
>
> **B:** Same here — we really need to catch up!

### Where have you been all this time? — 그동안 어디 있었어요? / 왜 이렇게 오래 연락이 없었어요?

- *Where have you been all this time? We really missed you!*
  - 그동안 어디 있었어요? 많이 보고 싶었어요!

> **A:** Where have you been all this time? No calls, no texts!
>
> **B:** I know, I'm so sorry — I was studying abroad.

### Fancy meeting you here. — 여기서 만나다니 뜻밖이네요! (예상치 못한 장소에서 아는 사람을 만났을 때)

- *Fancy meeting you here — I had no idea you liked jazz!*
  - 여기서 만나다니 뜻밖이네요 — 재즈를 좋아하는지 몰랐어요!

> **A:** Fancy meeting you here at the airport!
>
> **B:** I know, right? Where are you headed?

### What brings you here? — 여긴 어쩐 일이에요? / 여기는 무슨 일로 오셨어요?

- *What brings you here? I thought you were based in Busan.*
  - 여긴 어쩐 일이에요? 부산에 계신 줄 알았는데.

> **A:** What brings you here today? Business or pleasure?
>
> **B:** Just visiting an old friend who lives nearby.

### I didn't expect to see you here. — 여기서 당신을 보게 될 줄 몰랐어요. (뜻밖의 만남에 놀라며)

- *I didn't expect to see you here at this conference!*
  - 이 컨퍼런스에서 당신을 보게 될 줄 정말 몰랐어요!

> **A:** I didn't expect to see you here — what a surprise!
>
> **B:** Small world! It's so great to run into you.

### Look who it is. — 이게 누구야! (반갑고 놀라운 만남에 쓰는 감탄 표현)

- *Look who it is — I haven't seen you since graduation!*
  - 이게 누구야 — 졸업식 이후로 처음 보네요!

> **A:** Look who it is! I can't believe you're here!
>
> **B:** Ha! What are the odds? Come here, give me a hug!

### Tell me a little bit about yourself. — 자신에 대해 조금 말씀해 주세요.

- *The interviewer smiled and said, 'Tell me a little bit about yourself.'*
  - 면접관이 웃으며 '자신에 대해 조금 말씀해 주세요'라고 했다.

> **A:** Tell me a little bit about yourself, Jenny.
>
> **B:** Sure! I'm a graphic designer from Busan.

### No way. — 말도 안 돼. / 설마.

- *No way! You actually won first place?*
  - 말도 안 돼! 진짜 1등을 한 거야?

> **A:** I bumped into our old teacher at the mall today.
>
> **B:** No way! What did she say?

### That's interesting. — 그거 흥미롭네요.

- *That's interesting — I never looked at it from that angle.*
  - 그거 흥미롭네요, 그런 각도에서 생각해 본 적이 없었어요.

> **A:** I collect antique maps as a hobby.
>
> **B:** That's interesting! How did you get started?

### I totally agree. — 완전히 동의해요.

- *I totally agree that we should take more breaks at work.*
  - 직장에서 휴식을 더 취해야 한다는 데 완전히 동의해요.

> **A:** This café has the best coffee in the city.
>
> **B:** I totally agree. I come here every morning.

### That sounds like fun. — 재미있겠는데요.

- *A weekend road trip to the coast? That sounds like fun!*
  - 해안으로 주말 로드트립? 재미있겠는데요!

> **A:** We're planning a hiking trip next Saturday.
>
> **B:** That sounds like fun! Can I come along?

### Good for you. — 잘됐네요. / 대단해요.

- *You finally got your driver's license? Good for you!*
  - 드디어 운전 면허를 땄어요? 잘됐네요!

> **A:** I quit social media for an entire month.
>
> **B:** Good for you! That takes real discipline.

### That's funny. — 그거 웃기네요. / 신기하다.

- *That's funny — I was just about to call you!*
  - 신기하다, 나도 막 전화하려던 참이었어요!

> **A:** I dreamed about our old school last night.
>
> **B:** That's funny, I did too! What a coincidence.

### Come on, no way. — 에이, 말도 안 돼.

- *Come on, no way that story is actually true!*
  - 에이, 그 얘기가 사실일 리가 없잖아요!

> **A:** I heard Jake won the lottery last week.
>
> **B:** Come on, no way! He's unbelievably lucky.

### What was I talking about? — 내가 뭐 얘기하고 있었지?

- *Sorry — what was I talking about? I completely lost my thought.*
  - 미안해요, 내가 뭐 얘기하고 있었지? 생각이 완전히 끊겼어요.

> **A:** Wait, what was I talking about? I got distracted.
>
> **B:** You were telling me about your trip to Japan.

### Speaking of which... — 그러고 보니... / 그 얘기가 나왔으니...

- *Speaking of which, did you hear about the new café downtown?*
  - 그러고 보니, 시내에 새로 생긴 카페 들었어요?

> **A:** I've been craving Italian food lately.
>
> **B:** Speaking of which, there's a new pasta place nearby!

### Now that you mention it... — 그러고 보니... / 네가 말하니까 생각나는데...

- *Now that you mention it, I do feel a bit tired today.*
  - 그러고 보니, 오늘 좀 피곤한 것 같기도 하네요.

> **A:** You look a little pale today, are you okay?
>
> **B:** Now that you mention it, I didn't sleep well.

### Anyway... — 어쨌든... / 그나저나...

- *Anyway, let's get back to the main point.*
  - 어쨌든, 본론으로 돌아가죠.

> **A:** Anyway, we still need to decide on a venue.
>
> **B:** Right. How about the rooftop restaurant?

### Are you friends with him? — 그 사람이랑 친해요?

- *Are you friends with him? You two seemed to know each other.*
  - 그 사람이랑 친해요? 서로 아는 것 같던데요.

> **A:** Are you friends with him? He keeps waving at you.
>
> **B:** Yeah, we went to high school together.

### Make yourself at home. — 편하게 있어요. / 집처럼 편하게 지내세요.

- *Please make yourself at home — the kitchen is right down the hall.*
  - 편하게 있어요, 부엌은 복도 끝에 있어요.

> **A:** Come in! Make yourself at home.
>
> **B:** Thank you, your place is so cozy!

### Thanks for coming. — 와 줘서 고마워요.

**Q1.** My boss introduced me to his business partner for the first time, and I smiled and said, '____'
