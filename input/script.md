A Little History of Halloween and Related Celebrations
The Celts — About 2,000 years ago
The ancient Celts, especially in Ireland and Scotland, celebrated the end of the harvest season and the beginning of winter. People believed that spirits of the dead could return to the world of the living.
Christianity — Around 1,000 years ago
As Christianity spread through Europe, some older Celtic traditions became connected with Christian holidays.
All Saints' Day — November 1
In the Christian religion, November 1 became known as All Saints' Day — a day to remember and honor Christian saints. The evening before was called All Hallows' Eve, which eventually became Halloween. 
November 2 became known as All Souls' Day, a day to remember the dead.
Día de los Muertos (Day of the Dead) — Celebrated in Mexico and other communities on November 1 and 2, this holiday honors and remembers loved ones who have died, often with colorful decorations, flowers, candles, favorite foods, and family gatherings.


The Middle Ages — Early forms of trick-or-treating
In parts of Europe, people went from house to house asking for food in exchange for prayers for the dead. Other traditions involved wearing costumes and receiving food or small gifts. These customs helped inspire modern trick-or-treating.
The United States — 19th Century (1800s)
Irish and Scottish immigrants brought Halloween traditions to America, especially during the 1800s. Celebrations gradually became more popular.
The 1930s and Beyond
Halloween became more of a community and children's holiday. Trick-or-treating became increasingly popular, especially after World War II.
Halloween Today — October 31
Today, Halloween is celebrated with costumes, carved pumpkins (jack-o'-lanterns), decorations, parties, haunted houses, and trick-or-treating. For most Americans, it is a fun, largely non-religious holiday.

Scary Games

It was fun to discover a commonality among three games played in Iran, Korea, and the US. They are played to supposedly call upon the deceased or to answer questions the players have.

❀ Korea’s Bunshinsaba and Japan’s Kokkuri-san: traditionally involves a pen held by the participants often with just yes or no, but sometimes also letters for spelling. The pen's movement is interpreted as a response from a spirit. 
❀ Iran has the same type of game with their وح احضار (Loh-e Ehzar - meaning "Tablet of Summoning")

Halloween Celebrations Around Austin

Top Halloween celebrations and events around Austin feature immersive walking trails, haunted houses, festive pop-up bars, and music festivals: 
Family-Friendly & Immersive Attractions
Pumpkin Nights: Running through November 1, 2026, at Pioneer Farms (10621 Pioneer Farms Dr). Features a walking trail of over 5,000 hand-carved real and synthetic pumpkins, fire dancers, food trucks, and themed display realms. Hours are 6:00 PM – 9:00 PM (events through 10:45 PM). 
Spookydale at St. Elmo Brewing Company: A kid-friendly haunted house, crafts, face painting, and a children's costume contest.
Haunted Houses & Thrills
House of Torment: Located at 2632 Ridgepoint Dr, this massive 20,000+ square-foot haunted attraction features three detailed walk-through mazes, live actors, and special effects, open on select nights through November 9, 2026.
Scream Hollow: Located in Smithville (about 30 miles east of downtown Austin). Features four haunted houses across 18 woods-shrouded buildings, a Halloween museum, food trucks, bars, and live music.
Trail of Terror: Located north of Austin at The Fieldhouse at The Crossover, offering a foggy, scare-filled walk through the woods through November 1, 2026.
Halloween Pop-Up Bars & Parties
Gibson Street Bar: Hosting the annual "Twisted Carnival" Halloween pop-up bar through November 1, 2026, with sinister decor, themed cocktails, and guest DJs.
Nickel City: Transforming into a Simpsons-themed "Moe's Tavern" pop-up for the month with Krusty Burgers, wings, and specialty cocktails.
Black Lagoon at King Bee: An international goth and horror-inspired cocktail pop-up serving themed drinks like the Book of Blood and Death Rattle.
Freaky Deaky Fest: A two-day electronic dance, house, and techno music festival happening October 30–31, 2026, at the Travis County Expo Center.



The Barrington Halloween House is a famous, free, family-friendly Halloween yard haunt and neighborhood attraction.       HELD ONLY ON HALLOWEEN NIGHT
Location: 11306 Barrington Way (also cited around 11301 Barrington Way), Austin, TX 78759.
Schedule: Open on Halloween night starting at 6:00 PM, with yard displays and lights visible through the season.
Theme: Features elaborate, movie-set-level themes (such as Día de los Muertos or custom walk-through horror concepts) created by homeowners Sabrina and Stephen Butler. 
Crowds & Experience: Draws roughly 2,000 visitors and hands out hundreds of pounds of candy. 
Charity: An important part of the Barrington Halloween House charity. They accept donations for local charities, like Dell Hospital and Central Texas Food Bank.
